# Read English — Course Design Spec (the contract every level follows)

One free course that takes ANY learner — a 5-year-old, a teenager, a 45-year-old, a native speaker or an Urdu-first ESL learner — from "cannot read a letter" to "reads a policy paper critically". Built only from what the evidence supports. Research base: `research/01`–`07` (read the relevant file before writing a level).

## 1. Evidence verdict — what we took from each method

| Method | Verdict | What we keep | What we drop | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Synthetic (systematic) phonics | **Backbone** | Explicit GPC teaching in utility order, blend-first, decodable practice, mastery gates | Letters-free PA drills beyond a 1–2 lesson on-ramp; decodables forever | 01 (NRP d=0.41; Torgerson; Ehri; Share) |
| Analytic phonics / onset-rime | Secondary layer | Word-family/pattern work AFTER blending is automatic | As the primary way to read new words | 02 (Clackmannanshire) |
| Whole language | Mostly rejected | Rich literature, choice, love of reading | Guessing, "psycholinguistic guessing game" | 02 |
| Balanced literacy / three-cueing | Cueing banned | Read-alouds, independent writing w/ choice | MSV / "look at the picture" / "what would make sense" for word solving; permanent levelling | 02 (EdReports, Reading Recovery 2022 reversal) |
| Look-say / sight-word flashcards | Rejected | Automaticity as the GOAL | Rote whole-word drilling — use heart words (map, flag only the irregular part) | 02, 05 |
| Language Experience Approach | **Keep for adults/older beginners** | Learner dictates → first connected text is their own words | — | 02, 04 |
| Interactive read-aloud | **Pillar** | Scripted discussion + Tier-2 vocab every lesson (oral language strand) | Read-aloud as reward/filler | 02 |
| Orton-Gillingham / Structured Literacy | **Structure** | Explicit, systematic, cumulative, diagnostic; 6 syllable types; spelling rules; syllable division as flexible first guess | Claims that multisensory is *required* (optional engagement only); syllable rules as law (VCV ~50% predictive) | 03 (Stevens 2021) |
| Fluency (NRP, Kuhn & Stahl, Rasinski) | Strand from L2 | Model → assisted → independent; wide + repeated; phrase-cued; Readers Theatre; WCPM **plus** prosody | Unsupported silent reading as fluency instruction; speed-only goals | 05 |
| Vocabulary (Beck/McKeown/Kucan) | Strand all levels | Tier-2 focus, friendly definition + examples + generative use, 3+ exposures | Dictionary-copying | 05 |
| Morphology | L4–L7 | 20 prefixes (97% coverage), suffixes, roots — for decoding + spelling + meaning check | Selling it as a comprehension shortcut | 05 (Goodwin & Ahn) |
| Comprehension strategies | Brief, then embedded | ~6 strategies, few lessons each, ≤10% of time; reciprocal teaching as routine | Strategy units that eat the year | 06 (Willingham, Okkinga) |
| Knowledge building | **Spine from L5** | Content-rich text sets that spiral (science, history, civics, health, money) | Content-free "skills" passages | 06 (Tyner, Grissmer) |
| Text structure | Mandatory in L6 | 5 structures + signal words (g=0.56; 0.96 for strugglers) | — | 06 |
| Writing-to-read | Strand all levels | Dictation early; summary/response after every extended text from L5 | — | 06 (Graham & Hebert) |
| Critical / lateral reading | Mandatory L6–L7 | SHEG civic online reasoning moves | — | 06 |
| TaRL / placement by skill | **Delivery model** | Place by skill not age; re-place every level | Age/grade gating | 04 |

## 2. The non-negotiable rules (critics check every one)

1. **No cueing.** Never prompt "look at the picture / guess / what makes sense" to solve a word. Prompt: "Point to each sound. Say the sounds. Blend." "Does that make sense?" is allowed only AFTER a word is decoded.
2. **Decodable = decodable.** In L1–L4, every word in a practice text uses only GPCs already taught + heart words already taught (list them). Target ≥95% decodable; the rest are pre-taught story words, flagged.
3. **Blend before segment; letters with sounds.** Each new pattern: hear → see → blend (read) → segment (spell/dictate).
4. **Mastery gate, not calendar.** Each lesson ends with a check: real words + pseudowords, ≥90% to move on; what to do if not.
5. **Heart words, never flashcard drilling.** Show which part is regular, mark the irregular "heart" part. Many "sight words" become fully decodable later — say when.
6. **Two registers, one skill.** Every decodable text exists as **Track A (child)** and **Track B (teen/adult: work, money, family, health, phone, bus, market, news)** with the *same* target patterns. Adult track contains nothing child-coded.
7. **Comprehension from day one.** Every lesson has a Listen & Talk block (oral language + Tier-2 vocab) even before the learner can read much.
8. **Two metrics.** Track decoding and comprehension separately; never report one as the other.
9. **Urdu/South-Asian notes** where relevant: v/w, th /θ/ /ð/, short vowels (a/e/i/o/u distinctions, vowels are written in English unlike everyday Urdu), consonant clusters (no epenthesis: "school" not "iskool"), left-to-right, letter shape confusions (b/d/p/q).
10. **No streak-shaming, pause-safe.** Any lesson can be resumed; "skip if you know it" = take the mastery check first.
11. **Multisensory = optional.** Tapping, tracing, air-writing allowed and labelled optional.
12. **Honest citations.** Research claims cite `research/0X` file; nothing invented.

### Master sequence L1–L4 (builders MUST use exactly this order so decodability holds across levels)

**Level 1** — 1.1 Sounds in words (oral on-ramp: hear/blend/segment 2–3 sound words, mouth positions; letter s,a appear at end) · 1.2 s a t · 1.3 p i n · 1.4 m d · 1.5 g o · 1.6 c k ck · 1.7 e u · 1.8 r h · 1.9 b f l · 1.10 ff ll ss zz (FLOSS) + -s plural · 1.11 j v w · 1.12 x y z qu · 1.13 Review: all letters, letter names vs sounds, b/d/p/q · 1.14 Level 1 mastery check.
Heart words L1 (CANONICAL schedule — tools/decodable.py enforces it): 1.02 a I · 1.03 the is · 1.04 to of · 1.05 and was · 1.06 said you · 1.07 are he · 1.08 she we · 1.09 me be · 1.10 do what · 1.11 they one · 1.12 have go · 1.13 no so.

**Level 2** — 2.1 sh · 2.2 ch, tch · 2.3 th (voiced/unvoiced) · 2.4 wh · 2.5 ng, nk · 2.6 initial blends (st sp sn sm sl sw sk sc bl cl fl gl pl br cr dr fr gr pr tr tw) · 2.7 final blends (-st -nd -nt -mp -sk -lt -ft -lk) · 2.8 3-letter blends (str spr scr spl squ shr thr) · 2.9 -s/-es · 2.10 -ed (/t/ /d/ /ed/) · 2.11 -ing, -er (no base change) · 2.12 compound words + closed 2-syllable VC/CV (rabbit, napkin, sunset) · 2.13 Review · 2.14 Mastery check.
Heart words L2 (CANONICAL): 2.01 says for · 2.02 there where · 2.03 were from · 2.04 come some · 2.05 done want · 2.06 put push · 2.07 pull full · 2.08 who could · 2.09 would should · 2.10 your four · 2.11 many any her · 2.12 does goes two · 2.13 again friend because.

**Level 3** — 3.1 a_e · 3.2 i_e · 3.3 o_e, u_e, e_e · 3.4 VCe + drop-e rule (hope→hoping) · 3.5 open syllables (he, go, hi, me; ro-bot, mu-sic, V/CV division) · 3.6 y as /ē/ and /ī/ (happy, my) · 3.7 soft c, soft g (+ -ce -ge -dge) · 3.8 ar · 3.9 or, ore · 3.10 ai, ay · 3.11 ee, ea(/ē/) · 3.12 oa, ow(/ō/), oe · 3.13 igh, ie · 3.14 ue, ew, ui(/oo/) · 3.15 oo (moon/book) · 3.16 -ild -ind -old -ost (kind, cold, most) · 3.17 Review · 3.18 Mastery check. *(Revised after L3 R1 critic: ar/or moved before vowel teams, matching UFLI (r-controlled lessons 77–83 before vowel teams 84+) and RWI Set 2 — stops the park/start/for leaks.)*
Heart words L3 (CANONICAL): 3.01 once only · 3.02 very every · 3.03 great eye · 3.04 busy people · 3.05 water laugh · 3.06 walk talk · 3.07 buy answer · 3.08 whole earth. Any other not-yet-decodable word in a text = pre-taught 'story word', max 2 per text, flagged.

**Level 4** — 4.1 ar extended (multisyllabic ar: garden, market, carpet; review) · 4.2 or/ore extended (+ war/wor quirk: warm, work, word) · 4.3 er, ir, ur · 4.4 air, are, ear, eer (+ war/wor quirks) · 4.5 oi, oy · 4.6 ou, ow(/ow/) · 4.7 aw, au, al(l) · 4.8 ea(/ĕ/), other alternates; silent letters kn wr gn mb · 4.9 ph, ch(/k/ /sh/), gh · 4.10 consonant-le · 4.11 -tion, -sion, -ture, -ous, -cial/-tial · 4.12 syllable division VC/CV, V/CV, VC/V (flex rule), schwa · 4.13 suffix spelling: 1-1-1 doubling, change y to i, drop-e review · 4.14 multisyllabic word attack routine (mark vowels → peel affixes → chunk → blend → flex) · 4.15 Off-ramp: first authentic (non-decodable) texts + how to handle an unknown word without guessing · 4.16 Mastery check.

## 4. Lesson template — Levels 1–4 (20–35 min; tutor, parent or self-learner)

```
# L<level>.<n> — <Title>
Goal · New today · Review · Heart words · Time · Materials (paper, pencil; optional audio)
1. Warm-up review (2–3 min) — flash sounds already known; read 5 review words
2. Hear it (2 min) — oral PA with the new sound (first/last sound, blend, segment) — then show the letter
3. Meet it (3 min) — explicit: "This letter/letters say /x/." Mouth cue. Urdu-speaker tip if relevant. Optional multisensory.
4. Blend it (5 min) — word list: 10–15 real words + 5 pseudowords (label "alien words"/"nonsense words"), cumulative
5. Spell it (4 min) — dictation: sounds → words → one sentence (segment with fingers, write)
6. Heart word(s) (2 min) — map it: which sounds are regular, which is the heart part
7. Read it (5–8 min) — decodable text: Track A (child) + Track B (adult), same patterns; 3 questions (2 literal, 1 think)
8. Listen & Talk (5 min) — a short read-aloud passage (above decoding level, read TO the learner) + 1–2 Tier-2 words (friendly definition, 2 examples, "your turn") + 2 discussion questions
9. Check (2 min) — 5 real + 5 pseudo words + 1 dictated word; ≥90%? → next lesson. <90%? → exact reteach step.
Tutor notes: common errors + corrections (never "guess"), for adults, for young kids.
Decodability audit: list of every grapheme and heart word used in section 7.
```

Levels 5–7 use a **session** template (30–45 min): Retrieval warm-up · Word work (morphology/vocab) · Fluency or close reading · **Prime the topic (3–5 min: brief world/topic knowledge before reading — IES Rec 3A, strong evidence; a picture/map description, 2–3 key facts, one orienting question linking to what the learner already knows)** · Knowledge text + discussion (reciprocal roles, with Support/Challenge branches and a mastery gate before each release step) · Write to read · Check. Every text given in full (write original texts; public-domain excerpts allowed with source) with Track A / Track B framing where relevant (from L6 one text set works for teens+adults; kids track keeps age-appropriate topics).
