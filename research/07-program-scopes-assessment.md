# 07 — Program Scopes & Assessment: Comparative Evidence Base

Research pass on the published scope-and-sequence of the leading phonics/reading programs (US and UK), their evidence ratings, and the free/open assessment tools used for placement and progress monitoring. Compiled 2026-09-28 via live web search/fetch. Where a full lesson-by-lesson sequence is paywalled or only summarized on secondary sites (not the primary PDF), that is labeled explicitly — no invented lesson content.

## 1. Each Program's Sequence

### 1.1 UFLI Foundations (University of Florida Literacy Institute, K–2, free program)

**Structure:** 128 core lessons + 10 "Getting Ready" lessons + 10 review lessons = 148 total. Organized into units: Alphabet (Lessons 1–34), Phonics/Advanced Phonics, then Morphology/Affixes (Lessons ~119–128).

| Lesson range | Content (confirmed from public excerpts) |
|---|---|
| Getting Ready (×10) | Print concepts, phonological awareness lead-in, letter formation |
| 1–4 | Single letter-sounds introduced individually: e.g. L1 short **ă**, L2 **/m/**, L3 **/s/**, L4 **/t/** |
| 5 | VC/CVC blending begins as soon as a handful of sounds are known — UFLI does NOT wait for full alphabet mastery before blending |
| ~6–18 | Continue single letters + remaining short vowels; L18 = short **/ĕ/** |
| 19 | Short Vowel Review (consolidation checkpoint) |
| 20 | -s suffix /s/ (plural/3rd person) |
| 21–34 | Remainder of Alphabet unit: digraphs (sh, ch, th, wh), consonant blends, welded sounds (-ng, -nk), floss rule (ck, ff, ll, ss) — sequence continues cumulatively (full lesson-by-lesson list beyond L20 not independently verified in this pass; secondary source (spellingtestbuddy.com) confirms general shape but is not the primary PDF) |
| 35–~90 | Advanced phonics: long-vowel patterns (vowel-consonant-e, vowel teams: ai/ay, ee/ea, oa/ow, igh, etc.), r-controlled vowels, diphthongs (oi/oy, ou/ow), silent letters |
| ~90–118 | Syllable types (closed, open, vowel-team, r-controlled, vowel-consonant-e, consonant-le), syllable division |
| 119–128 | Affixes/morphology: -sion/-tion, -ture, -er/-or/-ist, -ish, -y, -ness, -ment, -able/-ible, bi-/tri-/uni-, Affixes Review |

Each lesson also embeds phonemic awareness, sight-word instruction ("heart words"), and connected/decodable text reading — UFLI explicitly integrates PA + phonics + fluency + a small amount of morphology, but comprehension instruction is minimal/not the focus (it's a foundational-skills-only program, meant to sit alongside a separate comprehension/knowledge curriculum).

**Source:** UFLI Scope & Sequence PDF (https://www.clarkness.com/Reading%20files/UFLI%20Scope%20and%20Sequence/UFLI%20Scope%20and%20Sequence%201-128.pdf — fetched but returned as non-extractable binary/compressed text via WebFetch; details above triangulated from search-result excerpts of that same document plus https://spellingtestbuddy.com/resources/ufli-scope-and-sequence/ and https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/foundations/printable-resources/). **Label: partially reconstructed from public secondary summaries — full lesson list 21–118 not independently confirmed line-by-line in this pass.**

**Evidence rating:** EdReports — "Meets Expectations" for foundational skills instruction, Grade 1 (https://edreports.org/reports/detail/ufli-ela-k-2-2025/first-grade). No independent WWC efficacy rating found in this pass (EdReports reviews standards-alignment, not causal efficacy — these are different review types, per Fordham Institute commentary found in search).

### 1.2 Letters and Sounds (UK DfE, 2007 — now superseded but still widely used as a base)

| Phase | Focus | New graphemes/content |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | Pre-phonics: environmental sounds, rhythm/rhyme, alliteration, voice sounds, oral blending/segmenting | 7 aspects, no letters yet |
| Phase 2 | First letter-sounds, VC/CVC blending | s, a, t, p, m, i, d, n, g, o, c, k, ck, e, r, u, l, h, f, b, ll, ss, ff (in that taught order) |
| Phase 3 | Remaining single letters + most common digraphs/trigraphs (25 new graphemes) | j, v, w, x, y, z, zz, qu + digraphs (ch, sh, th, ng, ai, ee, igh, oa, oo, ar, or, ur, ow, oi, ear, air, ure, er) |
| Phase 4 | No new graphemes — consolidation of adjacent-consonant blending (CVCC, CCVC, CCVCC), polysyllabic words | — |
| Phase 5 | Alternative graphemes/pronunciations, new spellings for known sounds | ay, ie, ea, oy, ir, ou, ue, aw, wh, ph, ew, oe, au, ey, a-e, e-e, i-e, o-e, u-e |
| Phase 6 | Spelling development: past tense, prefixes/suffixes, spelling rules — no longer "phonics" per se | — |

**Source:** https://readingchest.co.uk/advice/letters-and-sounds-guide/, https://home.oxfordowl.co.uk/reading/what-is-letters-and-sounds/, https://letters-and-sounds.com/what-is-letters-and-sounds/. Note: DfE removed Letters and Sounds 2007 from its own validated-programme list in 2021 because it doesn't meet current SSP validation criteria as a standalone commercial-grade programme (still free and widely used as the base UK sequence many commercial schemes derive from).

**Evidence:** Not independently efficacy-tested as a branded program (it's a government framework, not a commercial curriculum) — DfE's own guidance says schools using it should supplement it to meet current validation standards.

### 1.3 Jolly Phonics (7 groups × 6 sounds = 42 sounds; DfE-validated)

| Group | Sounds |
|---|---|
| 1 | s, a, t, i, p, n |
| 2 | ck, e, h, r, m, d |
| 3 | g, o, u, l, f, b |
| 4 | ai, j, oa, ie, ee, or |
| 5 | z, w, ng, v, oo (two sounds) |
| 6 | y, x, ch, sh, th (two sounds) |
| 7 | qu, ou, oi, ue, er, ar |

Each sound is taught with a multisensory action/story (synthetic phonics + "5 skills": learning letter sounds, letter formation, blending, identifying sounds in words, spelling tricky words). Jolly Phonics groups sounds specifically to maximize early blending opportunity (Group 1 alone permits many CVC words). Programme continues into Jolly Grammar for morphology/grammar as a separate follow-on product — Jolly Phonics itself is decoding-only, minimal built-in comprehension/fluency instruction (relies on separate readers).

**Source:** https://www.structural-learning.com/post/jolly-phonics-teachers-guide-synthetic, https://jollyreading.in/jolly-phonics-7-groups-letter-sound-words/, https://jollylearning.com/en-gb/our-programmes/jolly-phonics.

**Evidence rating:** DfE-validated SSP programme (UK). No US WWC/EdReports rating found (not typically used in a US EdReports-reviewed core curriculum context).

### 1.4 Read Write Inc. (RWI) — Sets 1/2/3 Speed Sounds (DfE-validated, one of the two most-used UK programmes)

| Set | Sounds (taught order) |
|---|---|
| Set 1 | Single-letter: m a s d t i n p g o c k u b f e l h r j v y w z x. Then two-letter "special friends": sh, th, ch, qu, ng, nk, ck |
| Set 2 | ay, ee, igh, ow (blow), oo (zoo), oo (look), ar, or, air, ir, ou (out), oy |
| Set 3 | ea (tea), oi (spoil), a-e (cake), i-e (smile), o-e (home), u-e (huge), aw (yawn), are (care), ur (nurse), er (letter), ow (brown), ai (snail), oa (goat), ew (chew), ire (fire), ear (hear), ure (pure) |

RWI pairs each Set with matched decodable Storybooks/Non-fiction, and drives comprehension through partner-talk "Fred Talk"/"Read Speed Sounds" routines plus a structured comprehension check after each text — stronger built-in fluency/comprehension loop than Jolly Phonics, still light on morphology compared to UFLI/Sounds-Write's later stages.

**Source:** https://blogs.glowscotland.org.uk (Set 2/3 parent guidance PDF), https://www.st-josephs.oxon.sch.uk PDF, https://home.oxfordowl.co.uk/reading/reading-schemes-oxford-levels/read-write-inc-phonics-guide/.

**Evidence rating:** DfE-validated SSP; the most widely used validated programme in England by usage share per Teacher Tapp data cited in search results.

### 1.5 Wilson Reading System (WRS) — 12 Steps (Orton-Gillingham-based, Grades 2–12 + adults, structured literacy Tier 3)

| Steps | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1–6 (Foundation) | Closed syllables → vowel-consonant-e (v-e) syllables → open syllables → consonant-le syllables → suffixes → multisyllabic words with taught patterns |
| 7–12 (Advanced) | Sound options/alternate pronunciations, contractions, r-controlled syllables, vowel-digraph/diphthong "d" syllables, suffixes attached to changing base words, Greek combining forms, advanced morphology |

Each step lists target words plus projected grade levels for introduction/application/mastery. WRS explicitly teaches total word structure (not just phonics) and moves students from a phonological to an orthographic focus over the 12 steps; fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension work is threaded through every step (not siloed at the end) — broader skill coverage than Jolly Phonics/UFLI in this respect, at the cost of much slower pace (designed as intensive 1:1 or small-group intervention, not a whole-class core program).

**Source:** https://uurc.utah.edu/research/_resources/wrs-161007-mblake.pdf, https://www.wilsonlanguage.com/knowledge-library/programs/wilson-reading-system/wilson-reading-system-curriculum/, scope chart at https://resources.finalsite.net/.../WRS_Scope_Sequence_Steps_1-6.pdf (Steps 1–6 only located as an open PDF; Steps 7–12 reconstructed from secondary description, not the primary chart).

**Evidence rating:** WWC Evidence Snapshot rated it based on one qualifying study (urban/suburban Pittsburgh, PA, <350 students); Evidence for ESSA rates it **"Promising"** — significant positive effects on Woodcock Word Attack (ES +0.46) and Word Identification (ES +0.15). https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/evidencesnapshot/546, https://www.evidenceforessa.org/program/wilson-reading-system/.

### 1.6 Barton Reading & Spelling System — 10 Levels (1:1 tutoring, dyslexia-focused, no teaching credential required)

| Level | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Phonemic awareness (oral only, no print) |
| 2 | Basic consonants + short vowels, blending into words, simple spelling |
| 3 | Closed syllables expanded; letter-combination "units" (-ck, -tch, -dge) |
| 4 | Syllable division; vowel teams introduced |
| 5 | Advanced/less-common vowel teams (ea, we, us-type combinations) for fluency and spelling accuracy |
| 6–8 | Advancing phonetic concepts toward grade-level reading (prefixes, suffixes, multisyllabic decoding — exact per-level breakdown not confirmed beyond this general description) |
| 9 | Influence of foreign languages on English spelling |
| 10 | Latin roots and Greek word parts |

Completion is described as reaching approximately a 9th-grade reading level. Barton is deliberately designed to be delivered by parents/tutors without a teaching license, one-to-one only (no classroom/group version) — this is its key structural difference from WRS, RWI, Jolly Phonics, and Sounds-Write, all of which support group/classroom delivery.

**Source:** https://bartonreading.com/levels/, https://homeschoolacademics.org/the-10-levels-of-the-barton-reading-and-spelling-system/, https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Intervention/734. **Label: Levels 6–8 content reconstructed only at a general-description level — no primary lesson-by-lesson PDF located in this pass (Barton materials are sold, not published free).**

**Evidence rating:** WWC Intervention Report exists (https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/InterventionReports/wwc_barton_070110.pdf) — full rating text not extracted in this pass; flagged for follow-up if needed. Evidence for ESSA page located (https://www.evidenceforessa.org/program/barton-reading-and-spelling-system/) but specific tier not confirmed in the search excerpt returned.

### 1.7 Sounds-Write (UK, linguistic-phonics approach, DfE-validated)

Three-tier code structure, not a fixed lesson-count like the others:

| Tier | Content |
|---|---|
| Initial Code (~Reception) | Single graphemes for single phonemes only (m→/m/, d→/d/, a→/æ/, etc.) — expected fully covered by Easter of Reception year; by end of Reception, first 2–3 Extended Code correspondences (ae, ee, possibly oe) also introduced |
| Extended Code (~Year 1 onward, "lifelong process") | Multiple spellings for the same sound and multiple sounds for the same spelling; by end of Year 1, pupils following recommendations should have covered the first 25 Extended Code units |
| Polysyllabic Words (Year 1 onward, continuing through primary) | Systematic teaching of multisyllable decoding/spelling, of increasing complexity, continuing right through the end of primary school |

Sounds-Write is explicitly built around 4 core concepts (letters represent sounds; a sound can be spelled with 1–4 letters; the same sound can be spelled different ways; the same spelling can represent different sounds) taught through 3 skills (blending, segmenting, phoneme manipulation) applied cumulatively — every new unit revisits all previously taught code, making it one of the more deliberately cumulative/interleaved sequences reviewed here.

**Source:** Sounds-Write's own published Initial Code / Extended Code / Polysyllabic Words scope-and-sequence PDFs (https://sounds-write.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Initial-Code-Scope-and-Sequence.pdf etc. — fetched but returned as non-extractable compressed PDF via WebFetch; content above triangulated from https://sounds-write.co.uk/the-cumulative-teaching-in-phonics-sounds-write/ and https://www.structural-learning.com/post/soundswrite-teachers-guide-linguistic-phonics). **Label: unit-by-unit list within Extended Code not independently itemized in this pass — only the 3-tier structure and coverage milestones are confirmed.**

**Evidence rating:** DfE-validated SSP programme (confirmed on current validated list per search).

### 1.8 Adult program: Reading Horizons Discovery / Wilson for adults

- **Wilson Reading System** is explicitly marketed for "Grades 2–12 **and adults** with word-level deficits" — same 12-step sequence as above, used unmodified for adult learners (no separate adult scope-and-sequence document located).
- **Reading Horizons Discovery** — a K–3-oriented foundational program built around ~42 phonograms with an explicit "most common words" (MCW) track alongside phonics/phonemic-awareness tasks per lesson. A distinct **Reading Horizons Elevate** product line exists for older students/adults (not directly verified in this pass — the scope-and-sequence PDFs located were all K–3/Grade 2-3 versions: https://help.readinghorizons.com/s/article/New-Reading-Horizons-Scope-and-Sequence, https://readinghorizons.com/Media/Default/Documents/Resources/RHDScopeAndSequence.pdf).
- No public scope-and-sequence for "Sounds of Reading" was located in this pass; treat as **not found / possibly a private-label or misremembered title** — flagging rather than fabricating content.

**Overall gap:** every program surveyed here except Barton/WRS is designed grade-K-3-first; genuine ground-up **adult literacy** scope-and-sequences (accounting for adult learners' existing oral vocabulary, life experience, and motivation/pacing needs) are thin in the free/public record. This is itself a finding — see Section 3.

## 2. Cross-Program Comparison

### Consensus order of skills (all 8 programs agree on this backbone):
1. Phonological/phonemic awareness first or alongside first letter-sounds (oral blending/segmenting precedes or accompanies print)
2. Single letter-sounds, taught individually, starting with sounds that permit early CVC blending (s, a, t, p, m, n cluster appears in Jolly Phonics Group 1, Letters and Sounds Phase 2, RWI Set 1, and UFLI's earliest lessons — near-universal agreement on "high-utility sounds first")
3. Short-vowel CVC blending/segmenting before any digraphs
4. Common consonant digraphs (sh, ch, th, wh) and consonant blends
5. Long-vowel patterns / vowel teams (multiple spellings for the same sound)
6. R-controlled vowels, diphthongs, less-common vowel teams
7. Syllable types / syllable division (explicit in UFLI, WRS, Barton; less explicit as a named stage in Jolly Phonics/RWI, which lean more on whole-word decodability than syllable-type labeling)
8. Morphology/affixes (explicit and extensive in UFLI's final ~10 lessons, WRS Steps 7–12, Barton Levels 9–10; comparatively thin in Jolly Phonics and RWI, which hand this off to separate follow-on products — Jolly Grammar, RWI's own upper-KS2 comprehension programme)

### Where they disagree:
- **Pace of introducing letter names vs. sounds:** UFLI explicitly starts blending before the whole alphabet is known; Letters and Sounds Phase 2 introduces 19 single letters across many weeks before Phase 3's digraphs even start — a much slower "wait for mastery" pace than RWI or Jolly Phonics, which move through their first sound-group fast specifically to enable early blending.
- **Grouping logic:** Jolly Phonics groups sounds explicitly to maximize blendable words per group (a designed constraint); Letters and Sounds/UFLI order by developmental/frequency logic without that same explicit "max blends per group" design goal stated in the sources.
- **Unit of progression:** Wilson and Barton organize around **syllable types** as the primary structural unit from very early on; RWI/Jolly Phonics/Letters and Sounds organize around **individual graphemes** grouped into loose sets, with syllable-type instruction appearing later and less explicitly (if at all, for Jolly Phonics).
- **Code complexity model:** Sounds-Write's Initial/Extended/Polysyllabic 3-tier model is a genuinely different organizing principle (mapping direction: sound→spelling and spelling→sound both taught, code complexity as the axis) versus the "sequential letter/group" model used by Letters and Sounds, Jolly Phonics, and RWI.
- **Delivery model constrains sequence pace:** Barton is 1:1-only, so its levels can move at the pace of a single learner (10–15 lessons/level, learner-paced); RWI/Jolly Phonics are whole-class group models, constrained to move at a class pace regardless of individual mastery — a structural, not just content, disagreement.

## 3. Gaps None of Them Cover Well

1. **True adult-first design.** Every program above (except Barton's "no age stated"/WRS's "and adults" note) is designed for classroom K-3 children first, with adult use being a repurposing rather than a ground-up redesign. None of the free/public materials located address adult-specific needs: existing oral language competence that should let an adult skip slow oral-blending-only early stages, shame/embarrassment management for adult non-readers, self-paced/asynchronous delivery, or adult-relevant vocabulary and text content (all decodable texts found in these programs' public samples are child-content).
2. **ESL/multilingual learners.** None of the 7 core programs reviewed have a public ESL-specific track. EdReports' review of UFLI explicitly flags "minimal strategies for leveraging students' home languages" as a gap. Programs built around English's sound-spelling code assume English is the learner's spoken language; a non-native speaker decoding a word they don't know orally is a distinct problem none of these programs solve.
3. **Advanced comprehension and academic vocabulary.** All 7 are explicitly *foundational/decoding* skill programs; several (UFLI, RWI) explicitly state they must be paired with a separate knowledge-building/comprehension curriculum. None claim to carry a learner from decoding through advanced reading comprehension, inference, or academic vocabulary at any level — comprehension coverage across this whole set tops out at "read a decodable text and answer basic questions."
4. **Fluency progression as a designed, measured strand (vs. an assumed byproduct).** Only WRS explicitly threads fluency work through every step; the others treat fluency as an outcome of repeated decodable-text reading rather than a separately sequenced skill with its own scope (e.g., no program reviewed publishes a fluency-rate target ladder analogous to their phonics scope).
5. **Cross-program placement compatibility.** There is no published crosswalk letting a learner tested against one program's benchmarks (e.g., DIBELS Nonsense Word Fluency) be reliably placed into a different program's scope-and-sequence (e.g., Barton Level 4) — every program assumes its own assessment feeds its own placement.

## 4. Assessment Tools

| Tool | Free? | What it measures | How to use for placement/progress |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UK Phonics Screening Check** | Yes — past papers 2012–2026 freely available (excl. 2020/2021, cancelled) | Decoding 40 real + pseudo words; statutory Year 1 check in England, pass mark ~32/40 | One-time snapshot of decoding ability at a fixed grapheme-set exposure point; past papers double as free practice/placement probes at any age since the word list itself is level-graded. Sources: sats-papers.co.uk, phonicsscreening.co.uk, phonicscheck.co.uk, primarytools.co.uk |
| **CORE Phonics Survey** | Yes, free download | ~10 phonics/decoding skills in sequence: letter names → letter sounds → CVC/short vowels → digraphs → blends → long vowels/vowel teams → r-controlled → multisyllabic words. ~15 min individual administration | Administer top-down; find the point where accuracy breaks down — that skill is the placement point for instruction start. Ideal first-pass screener because it directly maps onto most programs' scope-and-sequence order. Source: corelearn.com, reallifeintheclassroom.com |
| **DIBELS 8th Edition** | Yes — paper-based benchmark + progress-monitoring materials free from University of Oregon | K–8: phonological awareness, alphabetic principle/nonsense word fluency (extended through Gr 3, now including advanced phonics patterns), oral reading fluency (2-min passages, 20 alternate forms/grade), plus comprehension/vocabulary measures at some grades | Use for periodic (3×/year) universal screening + weekly/biweekly progress monitoring once a student is below-benchmark; ORF scores map directly onto Hasbrouck-Tindal norm tables. Source: dibels.uoregon.edu |
| **Acadience Reading** | Freely available benchmark goals/materials (successor brand built on the same research lineage as DIBELS, uses Hasbrouck-Tindal-aligned ORF benchmarks) | Same construct family as DIBELS: phonemic awareness, decoding, ORF, comprehension | Same use pattern as DIBELS; choose one or the other, not both, to avoid norm-table confusion since each publishes its own slightly different benchmark numbers |
| **Really Great Reading (Diagnostic Decoding Survey family)** | Yes, free ("complimentary") | Beginning Decoding Survey → Advanced Decoding Survey (branching by performance), plus separate Phonological Awareness Survey, Letter Knowledge Survey, Sight Word Survey, and the "Orbit" placement test for older struggling readers pinpointing where advanced phonics/morphology breaks down. 5–7 min each | Best tool for **placement into a specific program's scope-and-sequence point** because results feed the free web-based "Grouping Matrix" tool for instructional grouping by decoding-deficit type. Source: reallygreatreading.com/diagnostics |
| **PAST (Phonological Awareness Screening Test, Kilpatrick)** | Yes, fully free (Forms A–D) from thepasttest.com | Oral-only phonological awareness across four **unevenly-sized** sections in increasing difficulty — Basic Syllable (12 items, deletion), Onset-Rime (10 items, deletion+substitution interleaved), Basic Phoneme (10 items, deletion+substitution interleaved), Advanced Phoneme (20 items, deletion+substitution+**vowel substitution** interleaved) — no rhyme-judgment section and no pure phoneme-blending section anywhere in the instrument. Scored on two **independent** outputs: Highest Correct Level and Highest Automatic Level, with automaticity defined by a strict **2-second** response cutoff (respond before the 2-second count = automatic; the ~2.5–3+ second range is where non-automatic responses typically fall, not the cutoff itself) | 4–9 min; use BEFORE any print-based decoding assessment, especially for a learner who fails a decoding survey — determines whether the root deficit is phonemic awareness itself (needs oral-only intervention first) rather than letter-sound mapping |
| **Hasbrouck-Tindal ORF Norms** | Yes, free reference table (Reading Rockets hosts the 2017 update) | Not a test itself — a norm table (WCPM benchmarks fall/winter/spring, Grades 1–8) that any oral reading fluency measure's raw score can be compared against | Use to interpret ORF scores from DIBELS/Acadience/any 1-minute cold read — gives percentile-equivalent context regardless of which specific ORF probe was used |

## 5. Recommended Placement-Test Design for Our Course

Given the course must place learners of **any age** (young child through adult, native or non-native English speaker) at an appropriate entry point in a single sitting, the design borrows the strongest element from each tool above:

**Stage 0 — Intake screen (2 min):** Ask directly: can you read any English at all? Have you had prior reading instruction in another writing system (relevant for non-native adults — different failure mode than a never-taught native-speaking child)? This routs immediately: total non-reader → Stage 1; some decoding → Stage 2.

**Stage 1 — Oral phonological awareness (PAST-informed, not a PAST clone, ~5 min):** Administer
a short oral-only ladder **inspired by** the real PAST's overall shape — syllable-level deletion
→ onset-rime deletion/substitution interleaved → phoneme deletion/substitution interleaved →
advanced phoneme + vowel-substitution interleaved — scored on two independent outputs, Highest
Correct Level and Highest Automatic Level (2-second response cutoff), matching Kilpatrick's
actual PAST's (Forms A–D, thepasttest.com) general design principles. This course's ladder does
**not** reproduce PAST's copyrighted items, nor its exact section sizes (12/10/10/20) — it is an
original instrument built for this course, informed by PAST's structure rather than a
reproduction of it. A learner who fails onset-rime or phoneme-level items needs oral PA work
before any print-based phonics placement is meaningful — this catches the case CORE/DIBELS miss
(they assume some baseline PA and jump straight to print). Kilpatrick built PAST without a
rhyme-judgment or pure-blending section specifically because those item types hit a ceiling
early and stop discriminating struggling readers past kindergarten — exactly the population
(older children, teens, adults) this course serves, which is why this course's Stage 1 follows
PAST's general shape rather than a simpler rhyme/blend/segment/delete ladder.

**Stage 2 — Letter/sound knowledge (CORE Phonics Survey style, ~3 min):** Letter names, then letter sounds, for learners who pass Stage 1. Age-neutral (no child-specific content) if we build our own letter cards rather than reusing a children's-branded tool.

**Stage 3 — Decoding ladder (Really Great Reading Beginning→Advanced Decoding Survey style, ~7 min):** Real and pseudo-words in ascending order matching our course's own scope-and-sequence checkpoints: CVC → digraphs/blends → long-vowel/vowel-team patterns → r-controlled → multisyllabic/morphology. Include **pseudowords** at each tier (not just real words) so we measure decoding skill itself rather than sight-word memorization — this is the single most load-bearing design choice, borrowed from DIBELS Nonsense Word Fluency and Really Great Reading's own approach, and it is what lets the same instrument work for a non-native adult who may have memorized some real English words visually without being able to decode novel ones.

**Stage 4 — Oral reading fluency, only if Stage 3 clears CVC+digraph level (1 min timed):** Grade-appropriate-equivalent passage (built with graded, age-neutral adult/general content, not child picture-book content), scored as WCPM and interpreted against Hasbrouck-Tindal norms for the nearest equivalent grade-level text difficulty — reframed for our course as "reading level 1/2/3..." rather than a school grade, to stay age-neutral.

**Stage 5 — Brief comprehension check (2–3 min, only for learners clearing Stage 4):** Since none of the surveyed programs test this well, add a short passage + 3 literal/1 inferential question set to catch the "can decode fluently but doesn't comprehend" profile — common in older struggling readers and some ESL learners — and route them to a comprehension-focused track rather than restarting basic phonics.

**Placement rule:** enter the learner at the earliest scope-and-sequence checkpoint where accuracy drops below ~90% (matching CORE Phonics Survey's own placement logic) — never at the point of total failure, and never skip ahead past a skill gap even if a later skill was passed (a common false-placement error when only testing the "ceiling" skill).

**Total time:** ~15–20 minutes for a full profile, shorter (~5 min) for a learner who is clearly a fluent decoder already (early-exit after Stage 3/4 success). Re-administer Stage 3–5 periodically (every 4–6 weeks) as a progress-monitoring pass, keeping Stage 1/2 as one-time diagnostic unless a learner is stalled.
