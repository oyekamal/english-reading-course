# L2.11 — Adding -ing and -er (no base change)

**Goal:** Read and spell words with `-ing` (an action happening now) and `-er` (the
person or thing that does it, or "more"), where the base word needs no spelling
change at all.
**New today:** `-ing`, `-er` (added straight onto the end — no doubling, no dropping
letters; those spelling rules are their own lesson later, in Level 4)
**Review:** `-ed`; `-s/-es`; all blends and digraphs so far
**Heart words:** many, any, her
**Time:** 20–25 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Flash: `-ed` words `jumped, grinned, wanted, fixed, planted`. Read 5 review words:
`wished, checked, packed, jogged, rested`.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say: "Listen: **jump**, **jumping**, **jumper**." Ask what changes each time —
*jumping* means it's happening right now; *jumper* means the person doing it.
Same base word, two different jobs added to the end.

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
Say: "`-ing` and `-er` both just get added straight onto the end of these words —
nothing else changes." Show a base word (`jump`) with both endings side by side:
`jump` → `jump`+`ing` = `jumping`; `jump` → `jump`+`er` = `jumper`. Explicitly flag
the limit: "Some base words DO change their spelling before adding these endings
(like doubling a letter) — you'll learn exactly which ones and why in Level 4. For
now, every word in this lesson is a 'no change' word: the base stays exactly the
same."

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
Real words: `jumping, wishing, fixing, checking, resting, packing, camping,
landing, planting, helping, filling, printing, jumper, fixer, helper`
Pseudowords: `florbing, vashing, plemter, stronker, drelling`

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate: 1) sounds — base word, then base + `-ing`, then base + `-er` 2) words:
`checking`, `helper`, `printing` 3) sentence: **"Sam is helping and checking the
list."**

## 6. Heart word(s) (2 min)
- **many** — heart part: `a` says a short-e-like sound instead of the expected
  short a, and the final `y` acts as a vowel (a Level 3 topic) — mark the whole
  word as heart for now.
- **any** — same heart pattern as "many": `a` says an unexpected sound, final `y`
  as vowel; point out they share the same irregular chunk `an-`.
- **her** — heart part: the whole word is irregular for now (it needs the
  r-controlled vowel this course doesn't formally teach until Level 4); mark it
  fully heart, note it means "belonging to her" or "that person," and it's the
  most common word so far that needs this specific r-controlled sound.

## 7. Read it (5–8 min) — Fluency routine (model→echo→choral→independent)

**Track A (child)**
> Min is packing and stacking many bags. / "Are you helping her?" / Mom asks Dad. /
> "I am helping her fast," / Dad says. / Min is filling and checking any bag Dad
> hands her. / "You are a fast helper," / Dad says. / Min is still packing, / glad
> to help.

*(46 words.)*

Questions: (1) What is Min packing and stacking? (2) Who is Min helping? (3) Why do
you think Dad calls Min "a fast helper"?

**Track B (adult)**
> Sam is checking and fixing many vans. / "Are you helping her?" / his boss asks. /
> "I am helping her fast," / Sam says. / Sam is stacking and packing any bin his
> boss hands her. / "You are a fast helper," / his boss says. / Sam is still
> checking, / glad to help.

*(48 words.)*

Questions: (1) What is Sam checking and fixing? (2) Who is Sam helping? (3) Why do
you think Sam's boss calls him "a fast helper"?

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud only:

> The night before her first big exam, Ayesha felt more **anxious** than she had
> ever felt before. Her stomach fluttered, her hands felt cold, and she kept
> checking the clock, worried she was somehow forgetting something important. Her
> older sister noticed how anxious she looked and sat down beside her. "Being a
> little anxious means you care," her sister said, "but let's make a short plan so
> the anxious feeling has less to hold onto." Together they wrote down exactly what
> Ayesha needed for the morning, checked it twice, and set two alarms. Ayesha still
> felt a bit anxious as she fell asleep, but far less than before — and in the
> morning, she walked into the exam room calm and ready.

**Tier-2 word: anxious** — friendly definition: "feeling worried or nervous about
something that might happen." Examples: "He felt anxious before his first day at a
new job." "She was anxious about the storm outside." Your turn: ask "What is
something that has made you feel anxious?"

Discussion questions: (1) What made Ayesha feel anxious? (2) What did her sister
suggest to help?

## 9. Check (2 min)
5 real words: `jumping, fixing, helper, packing, checking`
5 pseudowords: `florbing, vashing, plemter, stronker, drelling`
1 dictated word: `resting`
**≥90%?** → L2.12. **<90%?** → Reteach: return to step 3, re-sort 5 fresh base words
into "add -ing" and "add -er" piles, confirming no spelling change is needed for any
of them, then repeat steps 4–9.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** doubling a final consonant that doesn't need doubling yet
  (writing "jumpping"). Correction: point out this lesson's whole rule is "no
  change" — if a learner over-applies doubling, that's actually a sign they're
  ready for the Level 4 doubling-rule lesson early; note it and move on calmly, no
  need to correct harshly.
- **Common error:** confusing `does`/`goes` with their Level 1 heart-word bases
  (`do`/`go`) when reading fast. Correction: point out the added `es` each time and
  connect back to the base word explicitly.
- **For adults:** `-er` as "the person who does X" (helper, fixer, printer) is a
  small but genuinely useful piece of word-building the learner can now use to guess
  at unfamiliar job-titles or object names they meet outside the lesson.
