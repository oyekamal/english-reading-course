# L2.01 — sh

**Goal:** Read and spell words with the digraph `sh` at the start and end of words.
**New today:** `sh` (one sound, /ʃ/, spelled with two letters)
**Review:** all Level 1 single letters, especially `s` and `h` (which combine to make this new sound)
**Heart words:** says, for
**Time:** 20–25 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil; optional letter tiles/cards for `s h` and a blank one for "sh"

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Flash these Level 1 letters, learner says the sound, not the letter name: `s a t p i n m d g o c k e u r h b f`.
Read these 5 review words: `sat, hen, cup, pig, mud`.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say: "Listen: **ship**. What's the first sound you hear? Not /s/-/h/ — one sound, /sh/."
Say pairs and ask learner to spot the /sh/ sound: *ship / sip*, *wish / win*, *shop / sob*.
Have the learner put a finger on their lips and feel the long, quiet "shhh" — contrast
it with the short, separate /s/ and /h/ they already know.

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
Show the two letters `s` and `h` together. Say: "When these two letters sit together,
they don't say /s/-/h/. Together they make ONE new sound: /sh/, like telling someone
to be quiet." Write `sh` large. Point out it can start a word (**sh**ip) or end a word
(fi**sh**).
**Urdu-speaker tip:** /ʃ/ exists in Urdu (ش) and is not a hard sound to produce — the
only new thing here is that in English it is spelled with **two letters that keep
their own separate sounds everywhere else**. Point this out explicitly so the learner
doesn't try to sound `s` then `h` and blend them.
*Optional multisensory:* trace the letters `s h` while saying /sh/ once, not twice.

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
Real words (cumulative — Level 1 letters + `sh` only):
`ship, shop, shed, shut, shin, shell, shall, gosh, posh, hush, wish, fish, dish, rush, cash`
Pseudowords (label clearly: "these are alien words — no meaning, just practice
sounds"): `shap, shom, hesh, vosh, nash`
Routine for each word: point to each letter/team, say each sound, then blend
left to right. Never allow a guess from the first letter alone — every word is
sounded all the way through (DESIGN.md rule 1).

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate sounds → words → sentence, learner segments with fingers first, then writes:
1. Sounds: `/sh/` (write `sh`), `/a/`, `/sh/` again in final position (as in *cash*)
2. Words: `shop`, `wish`, `fish`
3. Sentence: **"I wish for a fish."**

## 6. Heart word(s) (2 min)
- **says** — heart part: the whole word is irregular for a beginning reader — it is
  spelled like it should rhyme with "days" or "plays," but it actually rhymes with
  "fez" (/sɛz/). Mark the whole word as heart for now. Point out it is the closest
  sibling of the Level 1 heart word "said" — both are the "tell/ask" word family and
  both break the same way.
- **for** — heart part: the whole word is irregular for now (it needs the
  r-controlled `or` sound this course doesn't formally teach until Level 4); mark it
  fully heart, and note it sits right beside "of" — a Level 1 heart word the learner
  already knows — as a small memory anchor.

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

**Track A (child)**
> Dad and I go to the shop. Dad gets fish for us, and a dish for Mom. "The shop is
> shut at six," Dad says. I wish for a big ship in the tub! Mom can put cash in a
> box for the shop. "It is on the shelf," Mom says.

*(51 words.)*

Questions: (1) What does Dad get at the shop? (2) What time did the shop shut? (3)
Why do you think the child wants a ship in the tub?

**Track B (adult)**
> Sam has a job at the fish shop. He gets cash for his boss, and fish for the shop.
> "The shop is shut at six," Sam's boss says. Sam and his mom wish for ten fish.
> "It is on the shelf, not in his bag," Sam says.

*(50 words.)*

Questions: (1) What is Sam's job? (2) What time does the shop shut? (3) Why does Sam
rush?

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read this passage TO the learner — it is above their decoding level; they only listen
and talk.

> A fisherman named Tom worked on a small boat every morning. One day, Tom caught a
> fish that was absolutely **enormous** — far bigger than any fish he had ever seen
> before. It was so large that he could barely lift it into the boat! Tom's friends
> at the harbor could not believe their eyes. "That fish is enormous!" they shouted.
> Tom laughed and said an enormous fish like that would feed the whole street for a
> week. He shared it with every family on his road, and everyone had a wonderful
> dinner that night.

**Tier-2 word: enormous** — friendly definition: "much, much bigger than normal —
so big it's almost hard to believe." Examples: "An elephant is enormous compared to
a mouse." "That was an enormous plate of food!" Your turn: ask the learner to use
"enormous" about something in their own house or day.

Discussion questions: (1) Why couldn't Tom lift the fish easily? (2) What would you
do if you caught an enormous fish?

## 9. Check (2 min)
5 real words: `ship, wish, cash, hush, dish`
5 pseudowords: `shob, fesh, wosh, shig, dansh`
1 dictated word: `shut`
**≥90% (at least 10 of 11)?** → go to L2.02.
**<90%?** → Exact reteach: go back to step 2 (Hear it) and re-drill the /sh/ sound in
isolation with 5 fresh minimal pairs (*sip/ship, sop/shop, sock/shock, sell/shell,
sin/shin*) before repeating steps 4–9. Do not move on by calendar.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** reading `sh` as two separate sounds ("s...h...ip"). Correction:
  cover the rest of the word, show only `sh`, say "one sound" while sliding a finger
  under both letters together, then reveal the rest of the word.
- **Common error:** confusing `sh` with `s` alone when reading fast (skipping the h).
  Correction: go back to the minimal pairs (*sip/ship*) and have the learner
  physically point to both letters before saying the sound.
- **For adults:** skip nothing, but move through steps 1–6 faster — an adult's oral
  vocabulary and attention span make the mechanical GPC drill go quicker; spend the
  saved time on the Track B text and the Listen & Talk discussion instead of cutting
  either.
- **For young kids:** the optional multisensory trace (step 3) and physical "lips
  together, quiet shhh" gesture in step 2 both help; keep them, but never make them
  mandatory — some learners find tracing tedious, not helpful.
