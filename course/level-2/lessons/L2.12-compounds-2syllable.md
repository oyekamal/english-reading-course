# L2.12 — Compound words and closed 2-syllable words

**Goal:** Read and spell compound words (two whole words stuck together: *sunset*)
and closed-syllable 2-syllable words (*rabbit, napkin*) by splitting them into two
readable chunks first.
**New today:** compound words; the VC/CV 2-syllable split (find the vowel in each
half, split between the two middle consonants, read each closed syllable on its
own, then blend the two chunks together)
**Review:** everything so far — this lesson is the first time all of it gets used
inside a single longer word
**Heart words:** does, goes, two
**Time:** 25–30 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Flash: `-ing/-er` words `jumping, fixing, helper, packing, checking`. Read 5 review
words: `wanted, grinned, buses, catches, string`.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say: "Listen: **sunset**. Is that one word-part or two?" (Two — "sun" and "set,"
both words the learner already knows, stuck together.) Then: "Listen: **rabbit**.
Two word-parts again, but this time they're not two separate real words — just two
readable chunks: rab, bit."

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
**Compound words** — say: "These are just two whole words you already know, stuck
together with no change at all: sun + set = sunset. Read each word, then say them
fast together."
**Closed 2-syllable words (VC/CV)** — say: "For a longer word that ISN'T two whole
words, look for the vowel in each half. Find where two consonants sit between the
two vowels, and split the word right between those two consonants." Model on the
board: `rabbit` → find vowels `a` and `i` → two consonants `bb` between them → split
`rab / bit` → read `rab` (closed syllable, short a) → read `bit` (closed syllable,
short i) → blend `rabbit`. Do the same with `napkin` → `nap / kin`.
*Optional multisensory:* draw a line with a pencil between the two syllables before
reading each half.

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
Real words: `sunset, catfish, backpack, bathtub, pigpen, rabbit, napkin, muffin,
picnic, cactus, magnet, insect, instant, contest, hotdog`
Pseudowords (2-syllable): `vantic, plemzen, brostup, chunnet, dravin`

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate: 1) say a 2-syllable word, learner claps/marks the split point before
writing 2) words: `napkin`, `sunset`, `rabbit` 3) sentence: **"Min packed a napkin
for the picnic."**

## 6. Heart word(s) (2 min)
- **does** — heart part: the whole word is irregular for a beginning reader (`o`
  says a short-u sound, `es` says just /z/); mark it fully heart for now, note it's
  the "he/she/it" form of "do" (a Level 1 heart word).
- **goes** — heart part: the whole word is irregular (`o` says its own name-ish
  sound via a silent-e-like pattern not yet taught, `es` says /z/); mark it fully
  heart, note it's the "he/she/it" form of "go" (a Level 1 heart word).
- **two** — heart part: the whole word is irregular (the `w` is silent, and the
  spelling doesn't match the sound at all); mark it fully heart, note it's the
  number after one.

## 7. Read it (5–8 min) — Fluency routine (model→echo→choral→independent)

**Track A (child)**
> Min packs a backpack / for a picnic. / Does Min pack a napkin? / Min packs a
> napkin / and a muffin. / Dad packs two bits of catfish. / Min spots a rabbit /
> and a magnet in the grass. / Dad and Min sit / and watch the sunset. / Min goes
> to get the picnic bag, / then goes back to sit with Dad. / "Let's have a
> picnic," / Min says, / and Dad grins.

*(65 words.)*

Questions: (1) What does Min pack for the picnic? (2) What does Min spot in the
grass? (3) Why do you think Min wants a picnic?

**Track B (adult)**
> Sam packs his backpack / for his shift. / Does Sam pack a magnet? / Sam packs a
> magnet / for fixing the bus panel. / Sam checks for an insect problem / in the
> back bin. / Sam fixes a dent with the magnet trick. / This shop had a contest /
> for the best fix. / Sam goes to get his kit, / then goes back to fix the last
> dent. / At sunset, / the job is done, / and Sam packs up, / glad and full.

*(75 words.)*

Questions: (1) What tool does Sam use to fix the dent? (2) What did the shop have a
contest for? (3) Do you think Sam will enter the contest again? Why or why not?

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud only:

> A wealthy trader in the old bazaar was known across the whole street for being
> unusually **generous**. Every evening, before he closed his stall, he would give
> away whatever fruit and bread had not sold that day — never selling it cheap at
> the last minute, always simply giving it to whoever needed it most. His neighbors
> thought he must be losing money, but the trader just smiled and said being
> generous had brought him more good luck and more loyal customers than any clever
> deal ever had. One winter, when his own stall caught fire, every single family he
> had once helped showed up the next morning with tools, wood, and cash to help him
> rebuild — proof, he said, that generous kindness always finds its way back.

**Tier-2 word: generous** — friendly definition: "willing to give more than expected
— your time, money, or things — to help someone else." Examples: "She was generous
with her time, helping every new student settle in." "He gave a generous amount to
the school fund." Your turn: ask "Tell me about someone you know who is generous."

Discussion questions: (1) What did the trader do every evening? (2) What happened
when his own stall caught fire?

## 9. Check (2 min)
5 real words: `napkin, sunset, rabbit, magnet, picnic`
5 pseudowords: `vantic, plemzen, brostup, chunnet, dravin`
1 dictated word: `catfish`
**≥90%?** → L2.13. **<90%?** → Reteach: return to step 3, re-practice the
vowel-find-and-split routine on 5 fresh 2-syllable words with the pencil-line
technique before repeating steps 4–9.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** trying to read a 2-syllable word as one giant blend without
  splitting first, and losing track partway through. Correction: insist on the
  pencil-line split every time until it becomes automatic — this is the seed of the
  Level 4 multisyllabic word-attack strategy (research/05 §5d), so it's worth
  building the habit correctly now.
- **Common error:** splitting in the wrong place (e.g. `ra/bbit` instead of
  `rab/bit`). Correction: point at the two vowels first, then find the consonant(s)
  exactly between them, and split there — not by guessing where it "looks right."
- **For adults:** this lesson is a genuine turning point — explicitly tell the
  learner "you can now read a word twice as long as anything before this lesson,"
  because it's true and it's motivating.
