# L2.08 — Three-letter blends (str spr scr spl squ shr thr)

**Goal:** Read and spell words that start with a three-consonant blend.
**New today:** `str spr scr spl squ shr thr`
**Review:** 2-letter initial and final blends; `sh, ch, tch, th, wh, ng, nk`; Level 1
letters
**Heart words:** who, could
**Time:** 20–25 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Flash: final blends `nest, hand, jump, desk, gift`. Read 5 review words: `stop,
plan, camp, mask, tent`.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say: "Listen: **strap**. How many sounds at the start now — two, or three?" (Three:
/s/-/t/-/r/, all heard, all in a row, no gaps.) Point out most of these are simply a
2-letter blend the learner already knows (`st, sp, sc/sk-sound, sh, th`) plus one
more letter (`r` or `l`) tacked on — not a brand-new concept, just one more sound in
the chain.

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
Show `str, spr, scr, spl, squ, shr, thr`. Say: "Same rule again — every letter keeps
its own sound, said back-to-back, no vowel snuck in between, now three sounds
instead of two." Point out `squ` is special: it's always `s` + `qu`, and `qu`
(Level 1) already says /kw/, so `squ` is really /s/-/k/-/w/, three sounds hiding in
three letters that look like they might be fewer.
**Urdu-speaker tip:** the same cluster-epenthesis pattern flagged in L2.06 (adding a
tiny extra vowel before an initial cluster) applies here too, and gets slightly
harder with three consonants in a row — keep drilling the "no gap, no extra sound"
rule from L2.06 with these longer clusters specifically (*strap*, not "suh-trap" or
"is-trap").

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
Real words: `strap, string, strict, scrap, scrub, split, splash, squid, squint,
shrimp, shrub, throb, thrill, thrust`
Pseudowords: `strab, scrin, splob, shreck, thruv`

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate: 1) sounds — `/str/`, `/spl/`, `/thr/` (all three sounds of each blend) 2)
words: `strap`, `splash`, `thrill` 3) sentence: **"The shrimp can splash and
thrash."** — check: "thrash" has `th`+`r`+`a`+`sh`, fully cumulative, good, or
simplify to "The shrimp can splash in the tub."

## 6. Heart word(s) (2 min)
- **who** — heart part: the whole word is irregular — `wh` here says /h/, not the
  usual /w/ from L2.04, and `o` says /oo/; mark the whole word as heart, but tell
  the learner explicitly it's the ONE exception to the `wh`=`/w/` rule they just
  learned, so they don't doubt the rule itself.
- **could** — heart part: irregular vowel spelling and a silent `l`; only the final
  `d` is fully regular. Point out "could" rhymes with "would" and "should," coming
  in the next two lessons — same heart pattern, three words.

## 7. Read it (5–8 min) — Fluency routine (model→echo→choral→independent)

**Track A (child)**
> "Who has a shrimp and a squid?" / Min asks. / "Min has a shrimp and a squid," /
> Dad says. / "Could we splash them in the sink?" / Min asks. / "You could splash
> them in the tub," / Dad says. / Min and Dad splash the shrimp and squid in the
> tub. / Min grabs a strap and helps scrub the tub. / "This is a thrill!" / Min
> says, / and grins.

*(64 words.)*

Questions: (1) What two things does Min splash in the tub? (2) What does Min grab
to help scrub? (3) Why do you think Min calls it "a thrill"?

**Track B (adult)**
> "Who could help scrub the shop?" / Sam's boss asks. / "I could," / Sam says, /
> and grabs a strap to fix the bin. / Sam splits the job with a pal. / Sam scrubs
> the mats, / and checks the straps on the bin. / Sam thinks the shop is strict
> but fun. / At the end, / Sam is glad the job is done.

*(56 words.)*

Questions: (1) What does Sam grab to fix the bin? (2) Who does Sam split the job
with? (3) Do you think Sam's shop is a good place to work? Why or why not?

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud only:

> A young dog named Bruno was usually eager to try anything new, but the first
> time his owner brought out a bicycle, Bruno was strangely **reluctant**. He
> backed away, tail low, refusing to go anywhere near the strange spinning
> wheels. His owner tried treats, gentle words, even a favorite toy — but Bruno
> stayed reluctant, watching from a safe distance. Then one afternoon, the owner
> simply sat beside the bicycle and let Bruno sniff it in his own time, with no
> pushing at all. Slowly, Bruno's reluctant ears perked up, and he stepped closer.
> By the end of the week, the once-reluctant Bruno was trotting happily alongside
> the bicycle on every walk.

**Tier-2 word: reluctant** — friendly definition: "not wanting to do something, and
holding back or hesitating before doing it." Examples: "She was reluctant to try
the spicy food at first." "He felt reluctant about speaking in front of the class."
Your turn: ask "What is something you were once reluctant to try, but ended up
liking?"

Discussion questions: (1) Why was Bruno reluctant at first? (2) What finally helped
Bruno get used to the bicycle?

## 9. Check (2 min)
5 real words: `strap, scrub, split, shrimp, thrill`
5 pseudowords: `strab, scrin, splob, shreck, thruv`
1 dictated word: `string`
**≥90%?** → L2.09. **<90%?** → Reteach: isolate whichever 3-letter blend failed,
strip it back to its known 2-letter blend + 1 extra sound, drill 5 fresh words,
repeat steps 4–9.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** dropping the middle sound of a 3-letter blend (reading "strap"
  as "sap" or "tap"). Correction: point to all three letters in turn, requiring all
  three sounds before blending.
- **Common error:** the `squ` = /skw/ mapping feels unintuitive to some learners
  because `q` never appears alone. Correction: bridge explicitly from the already-known
  `qu` (Level 1) — "you know `qu` says /kw/; `squ` is just `s` plus that."
- **For adults:** three-letter blends are the last purely mechanical GPC content
  before the level moves into meaningful word-ending grammar (2.09–2.12) — this is a
  good checkpoint to affirm progress explicitly.
