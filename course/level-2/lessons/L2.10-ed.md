# L2.10 — Adding -ed (three sounds: /t/ /d/ /ɪd/)

**Goal:** Read and spell words with the `-ed` ending, in all three of its sounds.
**New today:** `-ed` says `/t/` (after an unvoiced sound: jump**ed**), `/d/` (after a
voiced sound: grinn**ed**), or `/ɪd/` (after `t` or `d`: want**ed**, end**ed**) — one
spelling, three sounds, and none of them is "ed" said as its own syllable except the
`/ɪd/` case.
**Review:** `-s/-es`; all blends and digraphs so far
**Heart words:** your, four
**Time:** 25–30 min (three sounds for one spelling is a genuinely tricky idea — take
the extra time)
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Flags: `-s/-es` words `wishes, boxes, buses, cats, jumps`. Read 5 review words:
`fixes, catches, dogs, hands, thinks`.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say three `-ed` words: **jumped**, **grinned**, **wanted**. Ask: "Do they all end
the same way?" Have the learner listen closely — *jumped* ends /t/, *grinned* ends
/d/, *wanted* ends with a whole extra syllable, /ɪd/. Clap syllables again: *jumped*
(1 clap), *wanted* (2 claps).

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
Say: "`-ed` is always spelled the same three letters, no matter which of the three
sounds it makes. Here's how to tell which sound: if the word already ends in `t` or
`d` (like *want, end, land, plant*), `-ed` says its own little /ɪd/ syllable. If it
doesn't, listen to the very last sound before the `-ed` — if your voice buzzes right
before it, `-ed` says /d/; if it doesn't buzz, `-ed` says /t/." Practice sorting
aloud: *jump* (no buzz → /t/), *grin* (buzz → /d/), *want* (ends in t → /ɪd/).
**Urdu-speaker tip:** the three-way `-ed` sound split doesn't map onto any single
Urdu spelling habit, so it needs to be taught and practiced as its own explicit
listening skill, not assumed obvious from the spelling — spend real time on step 2's
listening/sorting task rather than moving straight to reading the spelling.

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
Real words: `jumped, wished, fixed, checked, jogged, grinned, hugged, filled,
wanted, ended, landed, planted, packed, camped, rested`
Pseudowords: `flisked, brammed, wanded, chosted, plemmed`
Sort each one into /t/, /d/, or /ɪd/ before moving to the next word.

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate: 1) say a word, learner decides which of the three `-ed` sounds it needs
before writing 2) words: `jumped`, `grinned`, `wanted` 3) sentence: **"Sam packed
the box and wanted a rest."**

## 6. Heart word(s) (2 min)
- **your** — heart part: the whole word is irregular for a beginning reader (it
  needs the r-controlled vowel this course doesn't formally teach until Level 4);
  for now, mark it fully heart, note it means "belonging to you."
- **four** — heart part: same story — irregular for now, taught properly in Level
  4; mark it fully heart, note it's the number after three.

## 7. Read it (5–8 min) — Fluency routine (model→echo→choral→independent)

**Track A (child)**
> Min jumped and jogged for fun. / "Did you want your bag?" / Mom asked. / "I
> packed four mugs in it," / Min said. / Min packed four mugs in a bag, / and
> rested on the step. / "You packed your bag fast," / Mom said, / and hugged
> Min. / Min grinned and rested, / glad the job was done.

*(53 words.)*

Questions: (1) How many mugs did Min pack? (2) What did Min pack the mugs in? (3)
Why do you think Min is glad at the end?

**Track B (adult)**
> Sam checked and fixed the vans. / "Did you want your list checked?" / his boss
> asked. / "I checked four vans," / Sam said. / Sam packed his bag, / and rested
> for ten. / "You fixed your job fast," / his boss said, / and grinned. / Sam
> packed, / jogged off, / and rested, / glad the job was done.

*(59 words.)*

Questions: (1) What did his boss check for Sam? (2) How many vans did Sam check?
(3) Why do you think Sam grinned at the end?

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud only:

> Uncle Hamid had asked his nephew, Bilal, to water the plants every single day
> while he was away — just that one job. When Hamid came home a week later and
> found every plant wilted and brown, he was **stubborn** about not letting it go.
> "It's just water, once a day," he said, again and again, refusing to accept
> Bilal's excuses about being busy with exams. Bilal, just as stubborn, insisted
> exams mattered more than plants. Neither would budge for two whole days. Finally,
> Hamid's wife stepped in and pointed out that both of them were being equally
> stubborn over something they could fix together in ten minutes. They laughed,
> apologized, and replanted the garden side by side that weekend.

**Tier-2 word: stubborn** — friendly definition: "not willing to change your mind or
give in, even when maybe you should." Examples: "The stubborn mule would not move
an inch." "She was stubborn about doing her homework her own way." Your turn: ask
"Tell me about a time you or someone you know was being stubborn."

Discussion questions: (1) Why was Uncle Hamid stubborn? (2) What finally solved the
disagreement?

## 9. Check (2 min)
5 real words: `jumped, grinned, wanted, fixed, planted`
5 pseudowords: `flisked, brammed, wanded, chosted, plemmed`
1 dictated word: `checked`
**≥90%?** → L2.11. **<90%?** → Reteach: repeat step 2's listen-and-sort task with 5
fresh words (mix of all three sounds), have the learner sort BEFORE spelling, then
repeat steps 4–9.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** reading every `-ed` as its own syllable ("jump-ed," "grinn-ed").
  Correction: back to the buzz/no-buzz test from step 3 — most `-ed` words are one
  syllable, not two.
- **Common error:** writing `-t` or `-d` phonetically instead of the standard
  spelling `-ed` (writing "jumpt"). Correction: point out `-ed` is ALWAYS spelled the
  same three letters regardless of the sound — this is a spelling convention to
  memorize once, not something to sound out letter by letter when writing.
- **For adults:** this rule genuinely upgrades spelling accuracy on words adults
  already say correctly but sometimes misspell — worth stating that explicitly as a
  practical, immediate payoff.
