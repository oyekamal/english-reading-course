# L2.03 — th (voiced and unvoiced)

**Goal:** Read and spell words with `th`, in both its sounds: unvoiced /θ/ (think,
bath) and voiced /ð/ (this, then).
**New today:** `th` — one spelling, two sounds
**Review:** `sh`, `ch`, `tch`; Level 1 letters
**Heart words:** were, from
**Time:** 25–30 min (this lesson carries the most important Urdu-speaker content in
the level — don't rush it)
**Materials:** paper, pencil; a small hand mirror is genuinely useful here (optional)

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Flash: `ch`/`tch` words `chat, catch, much, fetch, rich`. Read 5 review words: `ship,
wish, chop, hutch, dish`.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say: "Listen: **this**. Put your tongue between your teeth and blow air gently while
buzzing your voice — feel it?" Then: "Now listen: **think**. Same tongue position,
but no buzz, just air." Have the learner put a finger on their throat: /ð/ (this,
that, them, then) buzzes; /θ/ (think, thin, math) does not. This is the ONE new
sound-pair in English that genuinely does not exist in Urdu, so give this step real
time.

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
Show `th`. Say: "Two letters, and it can be either of two sounds you just felt — the
spelling doesn't tell you which; you learn each word's sound as you meet it, and most
of the short function words (this, that, then, them) use the buzzing one, while most
other words (think, bath, math) use the quiet one."
**Urdu-speaker tip (important):** Urdu has no interdental sound at all, so speakers
often substitute an aspirated /tʰ/ (like the "t" in "top" with a puff of air) for
/θ/, and a plain /d/ for /ð/ — so "think" becomes "tink" and "this" becomes "dis."
This is not a mistake to be embarrassed about — it is a completely predictable,
well-documented substitution (research/04 §6), and the fix is purely physical: **the
tongue tip must go between the top and bottom teeth**, not touch behind the top
teeth like it does for `t` or `d`. Practice minimal pairs slowly, in a mirror if
available: *think / tink*, *thin / tin*, *this / dis*, *than / Dan*. The right-hand
word in each pair is what it sounds like if the tongue stays behind the teeth
(Urdu-style `t`/`d`) instead of poking out between the teeth (English `th`).
*Optional multisensory:* hold a finger just in front of the lips while saying `th` —
feel the air; for `t`/`d` there is little to no air felt in the same spot.

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
Real words (mix of both sounds — do not pre-sort them for the learner; part of this
skill is learning which is which by meeting the word): `think, thin, thud, thick,
moth, math, bath, path, this, that, then, them, with, than, myth`

Wait — replace `myth` (uses `y` as a vowel, not taught until Level 3): use `thump`
instead only from L2.07 on; for now finalize the list at 14 words:
`think, thin, thud, thick, moth, math, bath, path, this, that, then, them, with, than`

Pseudowords: `thab, mith, thosh, vith, thon`

For each word, say whether it buzzes (voiced) or not (unvoiced) AFTER blending it,
never before — the learner decodes first, then classifies the sound, same order as
every other GPC in this course (DESIGN.md rule 3).

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate: 1) sounds — `/th/` (voiced, as in *this*), `/th/` (unvoiced, as in *think*) —
same spelling both times, point that out explicitly 2) words: `bath`, `this`, `path`
3) sentence: **"This bath is not on that path."**

## 6. Heart word(s) (2 min)
- **were** — heart part: the whole word is irregular (sounds like "her" without the
  r-sound the spelling suggests); mark the entire word as heart for now, note it
  often follows "we/you/they" (we were, they were).
- **from** — heart part: irregular for now (the `o` says an unexpected short-u-like
  sound, and it sits right after `fr`, a blend not formally taught until L2.06);
  mark the whole word as heart, note it pairs naturally with "to" (a Level 1 heart
  word) — "from here to there."

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

**Track A (child)**
> Min and Dad were at the bath. "Where were you?" Mom asks. "I ran from the path
> to fetch this," Dad says. Min sits in the bath with a thin moth. "Can a
> moth swim in a bath?" Min asks. "Not this moth," Dad says, "but you can!"

*(53 words.)*

Questions: (1) What does Min bring into the bath? (2) What does Min ask Dad? (3) Do
you think a real moth can swim? Why or why not?

**Track B (adult)**
> Sam and his boss were at the shop. "Where were you?" his boss asks. "I ran from
> the bus stop to fetch this," Sam says. Sam sits and acts fast. "I can fix this
> fast," Sam says. His boss sits, then says, "I will check on this and fix it
> fast."

*(55 words.)*

Questions: (1) Where had Sam gone before his boss asked? (2) What does Sam say he
can do fast? (3) How do you think Sam feels at the end?

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud only:

> Grandpa was usually calm, but one afternoon he got absolutely **furious**. His
> favorite garden tools had gone missing from the shed, and he was sure someone had
> taken them without asking. He searched the whole yard, muttering to himself,
> getting more and more furious with every empty corner. Then his granddaughter
> walked out holding the tools. "I borrowed them to fix my bike, Grandpa — I forgot
> to tell you!" she said. Grandpa's furious face softened into a small smile. "Next
> time," he said, "just tell me first, and I won't have to get furious over nothing."

**Tier-2 word: furious** — friendly definition: "extremely angry — much angrier than
just upset or annoyed." Examples: "She was furious when her phone broke on the first
day." "The furious customer demanded a refund." Your turn: ask "Tell me about a time
someone you know was furious. What happened?"

Discussion questions: (1) Why did Grandpa get furious? (2) Was Grandpa still furious
at the end? What changed?

## 9. Check (2 min)
5 real words: `think, this, bath, that, moth`
5 pseudowords: `thab, mith, vith, thon, thosh`
1 dictated word: `path`
**≥90%?** → L2.04. **<90%?** → Reteach: repeat step 2's mirror/finger drill with 5
new minimal pairs (*thin/tin, thick/tick, math/mat, bath/bat, than/Dan*) before
repeating steps 4–9. Do not move on until the tongue-position fix is consistent —
this sound is worth the extra session.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error (Urdu-L1 and many other L1s):** `/θ/`→`/tʰ/`, `/ð/`→`/d/`
  substitution. Correction: physically show tongue-between-teeth in a mirror; do NOT
  just repeat the word louder — repetition without a position correction won't fix
  it (research/04 §6).
- **Common error (any learner):** reading `th` as /t/-/h/ separately. Correction:
  same "one sound, slide your finger under both letters together" technique from
  L2.01/L2.02.
- **For adults:** this is often the single most persistent pronunciation habit from
  childhood English learned without explicit `th` instruction — an adult can usually
  self-correct within one session once shown the tongue position, faster than a
  young child; don't assume it will take longer just because it's "an accent."
- **For young kids:** the mirror is genuinely fun for this lesson — use it, but keep
  it labeled optional per DESIGN.md rule 11.
