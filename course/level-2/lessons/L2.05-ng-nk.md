# L2.05 — ng, nk

**Goal:** Read and spell words ending in `ng` and `nk`.
**New today:** `ng` /ŋ/, `nk` /ŋk/ (both are single sound-units at the end of a
syllable, right after a vowel)
**Review:** `sh, ch, tch, th, wh`; Level 1 letters
**Heart words:** done, want
**Time:** 20–25 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Flash: `wh` words `when, which, whip, whiz, whack`. Read 5 review words: `thin,
shop, chat, that, wish`.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say: "Listen: **sing**. The end sound isn't /n/-/g/ separately — it's one sound made
in the back of the throat, /ŋ/." Have the learner hum the end of "sing" and feel it
in the back of the mouth/nose, not the front like a plain `n`. Then: "Listen:
**sink**. Same back-of-throat /ŋ/ sound, plus a /k/ right after it — that's `nk`."

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
Show `ng`. Say: "These two letters together, right after a vowel, make one
back-of-the-throat sound: si**ng**, ki**ng**." Show `nk`. Say: "This pair is that
same throat sound plus /k/: si**nk**, ba**nk**." Point out both only appear at the
end of a word part, never at the start.

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
Real words: `sing, king, long, hang, rang, ring, song, wing, sink, pink, bank, tank,
honk, junk, chunk`
Pseudowords: `zang, hink, sonk, zung, yonk`

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate: 1) sounds — `/ng/`, `/nk/` 2) words: `king`, `sink`, `bank` 3) sentence:
**"The king put his cash in the bank."**

## 6. Heart word(s) (2 min)
- **done** — heart part: `o` says a short-u sound (as in "come/some" from L2.04)
  and the final `e` is silent; mark `o`+silent-e as heart, `d` and `n` are fully
  regular.
- **want** — heart part: `a` says an unexpected sound (closer to short o than
  short a); mark only the `a` as heart, `w`, `n`, and `t` are fully regular and
  already known.

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

**Track A (child)**
> Min sings a song. "I want a pink hat," Min says. "When the song is done, you can
> have a pink hat," Dad says. Min sings and sings until the song is done. Dad
> claps, and hangs a pink hat on Min.

*(43 words.)*

Questions: (1) What does Min want? (2) When does Dad say Min can have the hat? (3)
Why do you think Min keeps singing?

**Track B (adult)**
> Sam wants his job done fast. "When is the job done?" his boss asks. "It is done
> at six," Sam says. Sam picks up his bag and a pink tin king from a bin. "This
> king is done for," Sam says, and grins. Sam wants to fix it, but the king is
> just junk.

*(53 words.)*

Questions: (1) What does Sam want done fast? (2) What does Sam pick up from the
bin? (3) Do you think Sam can fix the tin king? Why or why not?

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud only:

> After a full day at the farm, feeding the animals, mending fences, and carrying
> heavy buckets of water, farmer Iqbal was completely **exhausted**. His arms ached,
> his legs felt like stone, and he could barely climb the steps to his own porch. His
> son offered to finish the last chore, but Iqbal was too exhausted even to explain
> what needed doing — he just pointed and nodded. He sat down, closed his eyes, and
> within minutes he was fast asleep in his chair, still wearing his muddy boots.
> When he woke an hour later, he felt much better, though still a little exhausted,
> and very glad his son had taken care of the rest.

**Tier-2 word: exhausted** — friendly definition: "so tired that you have almost no
energy left at all — more tired than just sleepy." Examples: "After running the
whole race, she was exhausted." "He was exhausted after moving all those boxes."
Your turn: ask "What is something that makes YOU feel exhausted?"

Discussion questions: (1) Why couldn't Iqbal explain the last chore to his son? (2)
What helped Iqbal feel better?

## 9. Check (2 min)
5 real words: `sing, king, sink, bank, ring`
5 pseudowords: `zang, hink, sonk, zung, yonk`
1 dictated word: `pink`
**≥90%?** → L2.06. **<90%?** → Reteach: repeat step 2's hum/throat-feel drill with 5
fresh minimal pairs (*sin/sing, win/wing, ran/rang, sun/sung — note "sung" also
reviews the vowel change, acceptable as exposure, not taught as a rule yet, thin/thing*)
before repeating steps 4–9.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** reading `ng`/`nk` as separate letters (n...g, n...k).
  Correction: same "one sound, slide a finger under both/all letters" technique used
  for every digraph so far.
- **Common error:** confusing `ng` and `n` alone when the word is read fast (e.g.
  reading "sing" as "sin"). Correction: point back to the throat-hum feeling from
  step 2 as the physical check.
- **For adults:** "bank" becoming fully decodable today is a genuinely satisfying,
  practical win — call it out explicitly ("you can now read and spell a word you
  use every week").
