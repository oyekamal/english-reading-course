# L2.04 — wh

**Goal:** Read and spell words that start with `wh`.
**New today:** `wh` (usually the plain /w/ sound; a few speakers/dialects say a
puff-of-air /hw/ — either is acceptable)
**Review:** `sh, ch, tch, th`; Level 1 letters
**Heart words:** come, some
**Time:** 20–25 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Flash: `th` words `think, this, bath, that, path`. Read 5 review words: `chat, wish,
math, hutch, then`.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say: "Listen: **when**. First sound — same as **w**et? Yes, in most everyday speech
`wh` just says /w/. Some people add a tiny puff of air before it (/hw/) — both are
fine, and this course teaches the simpler /w/ so nothing here contradicts either
way a learner already speaks."

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
Show `wh`. Say: "Two letters, and for reading purposes, it says /w/, the same sound
as the letter `w` alone." Point out that `wh` almost always starts a word (never
ends one) and is common in question words the learner already knows by heart —
`what` (Level 1 heart word) is the same `wh` they're meeting formally today; point
that out explicitly so it clicks that "what" was always partly decodable, they just
hadn't met `wh` yet.

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
Real words: `when, which, whip, whiz, wham, whiff, whack, chip, shop, this, bath,
much` (last 4 are cumulative review from `ch/sh/th`, mixed in on purpose)
Pseudowords: `whob, whass, whith, chesh, wathin`

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate: 1) sounds — `/wh/` at the start of a word 2) words: `when`, `whip`, `which`
3) sentence: **"Which whip did Sam wish for?"**

## 6. Heart word(s) (2 min)
- **come** — heart part: the `o` says a short-u sound instead of the expected short
  o, and the final `e` is silent and doesn't make the vowel long (which will look
  odd once VCe is taught in Level 3 — flag that now: "come" is a real exception to
  the Level-3 silent-e rule, worth remembering). Mark only `o` + the silent `e` as
  heart.
- **some** — same heart pattern as "come": `s` is regular, `om` sounds like short-u +
  m, final `e` silent. Point out "come" and "some" rhyme and share the same
  irregular spelling pattern — one memory, two words.

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

**Track A (child)**
> Min asks Mom, "When will Dad come back?" "Dad will come back at six," Mom says.
> "Which mug is for some milk?" Min asks. "This mug," Mom says, and fills it with
> milk. Min sits and grins. When Dad comes back, Min claps and grins.

*(48 words.)*

Questions: (1) What time will Dad come back? (2) What does Min ask for? (3) Why do
you think Min claps when Dad comes back?

**Track B (adult)**
> Sam asks his boss, "When will the cash come in?" "The cash will come in at six,"
> his boss says. "Which bin is for some of the stock?" Sam asks. "This bin," his
> boss says, and fills it with stock. Sam sits and grins. When the cash comes in,
> Sam claps and grins.

*(50 words.)*

Questions: (1) What time will the cash come in? (2) What does Sam fill the bin
with? (3) Why do you think Sam claps when the cash comes in?

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud only:

> Little Ana loved puzzles more than anything, but one rainy afternoon she found
> herself completely **puzzled**. Her grandmother had given her a box with no
> picture on the lid and no instructions inside — just three hundred loose pieces.
> Ana sat there, puzzled, turning pieces over in her hands, unsure where to even
> begin. She tried the edges first, but she was still puzzled about which piece
> connected to which. Finally, her grandmother sat beside her and said, "When you
> are puzzled, start with the corners — there are only four, and they always fit
> first." Ana found a corner, then another, and slowly her puzzled frown turned into
> a grin.

**Tier-2 word: puzzled** — friendly definition: "confused, not sure what to do or
think, because something is hard to understand." Examples: "He looked puzzled when
the teacher asked a tricky question." "I was puzzled about how the magic trick
worked." Your turn: ask "What is something that has puzzled you recently?"

Discussion questions: (1) Why was Ana puzzled? (2) What tip helped her stop being
puzzled?

## 9. Check (2 min)
5 real words: `when, which, whip, whiz, whack`
5 pseudowords: `whob, whass, whith, chesh, wathin`
1 dictated word: `whiff`
**≥90%?** → L2.05. **<90%?** → Reteach: repeat step 2/3, contrasting `wh` words
against plain `w` words (*when/wen, which/witch — note "witch" also reviews `tch`!*,
whip/wip) before repeating steps 4–9.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** trying to say a hard /hw/ puff-of-air sound and getting stuck.
  Correction: tell the learner plain /w/ is completely acceptable; don't let
  perfectionism about a minor dialect variant slow down real reading progress.
- **Common error:** forgetting `wh` only starts words, guessing it could go anywhere.
  Correction: point out every `wh` word they've now met starts with it — no
  exceptions to find.
- **For adults:** connect `wh` explicitly back to question words they already use
  constantly in speech (when, which, why later) — this is a fast, satisfying "oh,
  THAT'S why it's spelled that way" moment for an adult learner.
