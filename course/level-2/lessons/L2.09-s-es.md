# L2.09 — Adding -s and -es (plural and 3rd-person endings)

**Goal:** Read and spell words with the `-s` and `-es` endings, and know the rule
for which one a word needs.
**New today:** the `-es` spelling rule (after `s, x, z, ch, sh`, add `-es` instead of
plain `-s`, because you cannot say two hissing/buzzing sounds in a row without a
vowel between them)
**Review:** all blends and digraphs so far; the plain `-s` ending (already taught in
Level 1)
**Heart words:** would, should
**Time:** 20–25 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Flash: 3-letter blends `strap, scrub, split, shrimp, thrill`. Read 5 review words:
`cats, dogs, hands, jumps, thinks` (plain `-s`, all already decodable from Level 1).

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say two words aloud: "cats" then "wishes." Ask: "Does 'wishes' just add one sound at
the end, like 'cats' did, or does it add a whole extra syllable?" (A whole extra
syllable — /wɪ-ʃɪz/.) Have the learner clap the syllables: *cats* (1 clap), *wishes*
(2 claps).

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
Say: "Try saying 's' right after another 's', 'sh', 'ch', 'x', or 'z' sound with
nothing in between — go on, try 'wishs'." (It's nearly impossible to say cleanly.)
"That's why English adds a little vowel + `-es` after those six endings: `s, x, z,
ch, sh` — it gives your mouth room to make both sounds clearly. Everywhere else,
plain `-s` is enough." Write the rule as a simple checklist the learner can use:
"Does the word end in `s, x, z, ch,` or `sh`? → add `-es`. Anything else? → add
just `-s`."

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
Real words: `wishes, dishes, boxes, buses, brushes, catches, fixes, crashes,
benches, cats, dogs, hands, jumps, drinks, thinks`
(first 9 need `-es`; last 6 are plain `-s`, used deliberately side-by-side so the
learner practices applying the rule, not just memorizing a list)
Pseudowords: `florbes, vozzes, crings, shalps, twenns`

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate: 1) the rule out loud — learner says which ending a word needs before
writing it 2) words: `wishes`, `catches`, `hands` (mixed on purpose) 3) sentence:
**"Sam fixes the buses and checks the boxes."**

## 6. Heart word(s) (2 min)
- **would** — heart part: irregular vowel spelling and a silent `l`; only the final
  `d` is fully regular. Point out "would" rhymes with "could" (L2.08) and "should."
- **should** — same heart pattern as "would"/"could." Three rhyming heart words
  (could, would, should) taught across three lessons — point this out explicitly;
  one memory pattern covers all three.

## 7. Read it (5–8 min) — Fluency routine (model→echo→choral→independent)

**Track A (child)**
> Min washes the dishes and stacks the boxes. / "Would you help with the mugs?" /
> Mom asks. / "I would," / Min says, / and picks up the mugs. / "Should I stack
> them fast?" / Min asks. / "You should stack them fast," / Mom says. / Min
> washes, / stacks, / and fixes the mugs / until the job is done.

*(55 words.)*

Questions: (1) What two jobs does Min do first? (2) What does Min ask Mom about
stacking? (3) Why do you think Mom wants Min to be careful with the mugs?

**Track B (adult)**
> Sam fixes the vans and checks the boxes. / "Would you help me?" / his boss asks.
> / "I would," / Sam says, / and picks up the boxes. / "Should we stack them
> fast?" / Sam asks. / "You should stack them fast," / his boss says. / Sam
> checks, / stacks, / and fixes the vans / until the job is done.

*(56 words.)*

Questions: (1) What two jobs does Sam do first? (2) What does Sam ask his boss
about stacking? (3) Why do you think Sam's boss wants them to be careful?

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud only:

> When the class hamster went missing from its cage on a Friday afternoon, every
> student was **delighted** to hear it had been found safe behind the bookshelf on
> Monday morning. The teacher was delighted too, and let the whole class take
> turns holding it gently, one by one. One boy, who had been especially worried all
> weekend, was so delighted that he asked if he could bring the hamster home just
> for one night, to make sure it was really okay. His teacher smiled at how
> delighted — and relieved — he looked, and said yes, as long as his parents agreed.
> That evening, the whole family took turns being delighted by the tiny hamster
> running on its wheel.

**Tier-2 word: delighted** — friendly definition: "very happy and pleased about
something good that happened." Examples: "She was delighted to see her old friend
again." "He was delighted with his birthday present." Your turn: ask "What is
something that has delighted you recently?"

Discussion questions: (1) Why was everyone delighted on Monday? (2) What did the
worried boy ask to do?

## 9. Check (2 min)
5 real words: `wishes, boxes, buses, cats, jumps`
5 pseudowords: `florbes, vozzes, crings, shalps, twenns`
1 dictated word: `catches`
**≥90%?** → L2.10. **<90%?** → Reteach: re-drill the checklist from step 3 with 5
fresh word pairs, sorting them into "-s pile" and "-es pile" before repeating steps
4–9.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** adding `-es` to every word out of over-generalization (writing
  "catses" or "handses"). Correction: go back to the six-letter checklist (`s x z
  ch sh`) and have the learner check the actual last sound before choosing.
- **Common error:** adding plain `-s` after `sh`/`ch` and producing an unreadable
  cluster ("wishs"). Correction: have the learner try to say it aloud — the
  difficulty is the built-in check.
- **For adults:** this is genuinely useful, transferable spelling knowledge (not
  just a reading skill) — many adults have half-internalized this rule from years of
  reading but never had it stated explicitly; naming it can fix long-standing
  spelling uncertainty fast.
