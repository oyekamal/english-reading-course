# L2.06 — Initial blends (st sp sn sm sl sw sk sc bl cl fl gl pl br cr dr fr gr pr tr tw)

**Goal:** Read and spell words that start with a two-consonant blend, keeping both
consonant sounds distinct (unlike a digraph, both letters keep their own sound).
**New today:** initial blends `st sp sn sm sl sw sk sc` (s-blends) and `bl cl fl gl
pl br cr dr fr gr pr tr tw` (l-blends and r-blends)
**Review:** `sh, ch, tch, th, wh, ng, nk`; Level 1 letters
**Heart words:** put, push
**Time:** 25–30 min (first fluency-routine lesson — allow extra time)
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Flash: `ng/nk` words `sing, bank, king, sink, long`. Read 5 review words: `whip,
that, chin, wash — wait, use "wish"`, `thin`.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say: "Listen: **stop**. How many separate sounds can you hear at the start — one, or
two?" (Two: /s/ then /t/, both fully heard — unlike `sh`/`ch`, which are ONE sound
for TWO letters, a blend is TWO sounds for TWO letters, said quickly together with
nothing in between.) Contrast directly: "shop has one sound at the start; stop has
two."

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
Show a few blend pairs (`st, sp, sn, sm, sl, sw, sk, sc`, then `bl, cl, fl, gl, pl`,
then `br, cr, dr, fr, gr, pr, tr, tw`). Say: "Each of these is two letters, two
sounds, said back-to-back with no gap and no extra vowel between them."
**Urdu-speaker tip (important):** a well-documented pattern for Urdu/Hindi-L1
speakers is inserting a small extra vowel sound before a word-initial `s`-blend —
saying "school" as if it were "sakool" or "iskool." This is not in the four numbered
trouble spots from research/04, but follows directly from research/01's general
point about explicit contrastive drilling for consonant clusters that work
differently in the learner's L1 — treat it as a practical tutor tip. **Drill:** say
the two consonant sounds as one unbroken unit before the vowel ever starts — no
sound, however small, in between. Practice: *stop* (not "suh-top"), *snap* (not
"suh-nap"), *step* (not "is-tep").
*Optional multisensory:* tap the desk once per sound while blending (`s`-tap,
`t`-tap, then the vowel).

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
Real words: `stop, spin, snap, smell, slip, swim, skin, scan, black, clap, flag,
plan, drum, frog, trip`
Pseudowords: `stad, blen, drin, spuff, glim`

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate: 1) sounds — `/st/`, `/pl/`, `/dr/` (say both sounds of each blend, ask the
learner to write both letters) 2) words: `stop`, `plan`, `drum` 3) sentence: **"The
frog can swim and hop."**

## 6. Heart word(s) (2 min)
- **put** — heart part: the `u` says a short-oo sound (like "push," not like "cut");
  mark only the `u` as heart, `p` and `t` are fully regular.
- **push** — same `u` heart pattern as "put" — point out they rhyme in that one
  vowel sound; `p`, `sh` are fully regular (and `sh` review from L2.01).

## 7. Read it (5–8 min) — first Fluency Routine lesson

**Fluency routine (do this before the questions):**
1. **Model** — tutor/audio reads the text aloud once at a natural pace.
2. **Echo** — learner repeats each `/`-marked chunk right after the model.
3. **Choral** — tutor and learner read the whole text together, out loud.
4. **Independent** — learner reads the text alone.
Do NOT skip straight to independent reading — this routine is the point of the
lesson, not an extra.

**Track A (child)**
> Min and Dad go to the pond. / "Can we swim?" / Min asks. / "Push the flip flops
> on fast," / Dad says. / A frog sits on a black rock. / Min wants to grab it, /
> but the frog can hop and skip fast! / "Let's clap and stomp," / Min says, / "then
> we can swim." / Dad and Min swim, / and Min wants to put the flip flops on the
> rock.

*(62 words.)*

Questions: (1) What does Dad tell Min to do fast? (2) What is sitting on the black
rock? (3) Why do you think the frog hops away?

**Track B (adult)**
> Sam must push to get the shop's stock done. / He picks up a black pen / and jots
> the stock on a pad. / A big box hits the step, / and Sam has to dash and shut it
> fast. / "I still must push to get this done," / Sam says, / "but a big box will
> not stop me." / Sam sits, / and gets the job done. / Sam wants to put the pen
> back on the desk.

*(72 words.)*

Questions: (1) What does Sam use to write the stock list? (2) What stops Sam for a
moment? (3) Do you think Sam finishes the job? Why or why not?

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud only:

> Before crossing the busy market road, Aunt Rukhsana was always very **cautious**.
> She would stop at the edge, look left, then right, then left again, even if the
> road looked completely empty. Her nephew used to tease her about being so
> cautious, saying it took forever just to cross one small street. One evening,
> though, a bicycle came speeding around the corner at exactly the moment they
> stepped off the curb — and because Aunt Rukhsana had been cautious and was
> watching closely, she pulled her nephew back just in time. After that, her nephew
> never teased her again. "Better cautious than sorry," she always says, and now he
> says it too.

**Tier-2 word: cautious** — friendly definition: "careful, checking for danger
before doing something, so you don't get hurt." Examples: "Be cautious near the hot
stove." "The cautious driver checked both mirrors before turning." Your turn: ask
"When should someone be extra cautious?"

Discussion questions: (1) What did Aunt Rukhsana's caution prevent? (2) Can you
think of another time being cautious would help someone?

## 9. Check (2 min)
5 real words: `stop, plan, drum, skin, frog`
5 pseudowords: `stad, blen, drin, spuff, glim`
1 dictated word: `snap`
**≥90%?** → L2.07. **<90%?** → Reteach: return to step 2/3, isolate whichever specific
blend family failed (s-blends, l-blends, or r-blends) and re-drill just that family
with 5 fresh words before repeating steps 4–9.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** inserting a schwa between the two blend consonants ("suh-top").
  Correction: the tap-per-sound multisensory cue in step 3, and modeling the word at
  normal speed several times so the learner hears there is no gap.
- **Common error:** dropping the second consonant of the blend and reading only the
  first ("top" for "stop"). Correction: point to both letters and require the
  learner say both sounds before allowing the blend.
- **For adults:** the fluency routine (model→echo→choral→independent) can feel
  unfamiliar or "school-like" to an adult — frame it honestly as "this is proven to
  build reading smoothness faster than reading alone" (research/05 §5b) rather than
  apologizing for it.
- **For young kids:** keep each round of the fluency routine short and upbeat — this
  is a good lesson to add a favorite stuffed animal as the "audience" for the
  independent-read step, entirely optional.
