# Level 7 Mastery Check — The Course Finish Line

This is the final exam for the whole course. It is not timed like a quiz — it is a genuine
multi-source synthesis task, the same shape as PIAAC's top literacy tasks and CEFR's C1/C2 reading
descriptors (research/06 §1.11): read several dense, contrasting sources and produce one written
synthesis that a reasonable, informed adult would recognize as fair, accurate, and well-reasoned.

**Time:** untimed, but expect 60–90 minutes including a genuine reread; can be split across two
sittings. **Materials:** notebook or document to write the final response; access to your Level 7
lesson notes is allowed and expected (this tests applied skill, not memorized definitions).

**Passing standard:** score ≥80% on the rubric below (Part A) AND complete Part B (the applied
skills checklist) with no more than one "not yet" item. If you score below that, see "If you don't
pass yet" at the end — this is diagnostic, not a wall.

**A note on the scenario:** the city, the traffic study, the environmental report, the op-ed, and
the easement agreement in Part A are all constructed for this exam — there is no real "downtown
bypass" case behind them. The scenario is built to be realistic enough that the reading and
reasoning skills transfer directly to a real policy question, not to be mistaken for one.

## Part A — The synthesis task (the exam itself)

### Research question

**Should a city government approve a proposed new highway bypass around its downtown, built to
reduce truck traffic through residential streets?**

### Read all four sources below, then complete the tasks that follow.

> **Source 1 — City traffic department data summary**
>
> The proposed bypass would divert an estimated 8,000 truck trips per day away from Main Street
> and the three residential streets currently used as a de facto truck route. A 2023 city-
> commissioned traffic study, using sensor data from the current routes plus modeling for the
> new road, projected: downtown truck traffic down 65–80%; average car commute time downtown
> reduced by 4–6 minutes; new bypass corridor would run within 200 meters of a public elementary
> school and a wetland area currently used for stormwater drainage. The study's traffic
> projections used industry-standard modeling but noted actual results "may vary depending on
> future freight volume growth," which the same study projects at 3–5% annually.

> **Source 2 — Historical context excerpt, adapted from a 1998 city council meeting record**
>
> "The council notes that when the current truck route through residential streets was designated
> in 1974, it was intended as a temporary measure pending construction of an outer loop road — a
> project shelved in 1976 due to budget constraints and never revisited. Residents on the affected
> streets have raised concerns about noise and safety at every council session on this topic since
> at least 1989, without resolution." *Context note: this shows the current arrangement was never
> meant to be permanent and the underlying problem has been documented for decades — useful for
> understanding how long-standing the issue is, but it does not by itself tell you whether today's
> specific bypass proposal is the right fix.*

> **Source 3 — Environmental science summary**
>
> An independent environmental consulting firm's report (commissioned by a local conservation
> group, not the city) examined the proposed route's proximity to the wetland area. The report,
> based on soil and water samples from 14 sites along the proposed corridor plus modeling of
> runoff patterns, found construction would likely increase impervious surface runoff into the
> wetland by an estimated 20–30%, potentially degrading water quality relevant to a protected
> salamander species documented in the wetland since a 2019 survey. The report recommends either
> rerouting around the wetland (adding an estimated 1.2 km and significant cost) or constructing
> retention ponds to manage runoff, and states its 14-site sample, while consistent with standard
> environmental assessment practice, would benefit from a larger follow-up study before
> construction begins.

> **Source 4 — Op-ed, local newspaper**
>
> "Every year the council delays, another year of diesel trucks rumbles past an elementary school
> playground, and every year of delay is a year some council member will have to explain to a
> parent whose child has to breathe it. The choice here is simple: build the bypass now, or admit
> the council cares more about a handful of salamanders than about children's lungs. Opponents
> point to 'environmental concerns' — but where was this environmental passion in 1974, when the
> original truck route was drawn straight past three residential streets and nobody blinked? This
> sudden environmental awakening, right when a real solution is finally on the table, deserves
> exactly the skepticism it's getting from residents who've waited fifty years for relief."

> **Source 5 — Excerpt, Proposed Property Easement Agreement (offered to landowners along the
> bypass corridor)**
>
> §3. Grant of Easement. Landowner grants to the City a permanent easement across the described
> 12-meter corridor for construction, maintenance, and operation of a public roadway, including
> the right to remove trees, alter drainage, and install utility lines within the easement area,
> at the City's sole discretion, without further compensation beyond the one-time payment
> specified in §5.
>
> §5. Compensation. The City shall pay Landowner a one-time payment of $18,500 upon execution of
> this agreement, which constitutes full and final compensation for the easement granted,
> including any diminution in the remaining property's value, present or future, arising from the
> roadway's construction or operation, including noise, light, or vibration.
>
> §7. Landowner Obligations. Landowner shall not construct any permanent structure, plant any tree
> exceeding 3 meters in mature height, or alter drainage within the easement area without prior
> written approval from the City's Public Works Department, an approval which may be withheld at
> the Department's discretion and is not subject to appeal.
>
> §9. Binding on Successors. This easement is permanent and runs with the land; it binds all
> future owners of the property regardless of whether they were party to or aware of this
> agreement at the time of purchase.

### Tasks

**A1 (8 pts) — Discipline-specific reading check.** For each of Sources 1–4, name the ONE
discipline-specific move from Sessions 2–9 that is most relevant to reading it critically (e.g.,
"Source 1: data-in-text — check what the projection is actually based on and its stated
uncertainty"). One sentence per source, 4 sentences total.

**A2 (8 pts) — Fallacy check.** Source 4 contains at least two identifiable moves from Session 7's
list (name each fallacy, quote the sentence). Identify both.

**A3 (8 pts) — Corroboration/agreement check.** What do Sources 1, 2, and 3 agree on regarding
timeline and severity of the underlying problem? What does Source 4 add that the other three
don't (an emotional framing, not new data) — and is that addition evidence, or rhetoric?

**A4 (12 pts) — Weight-of-evidence ranking.** Rank Sources 1–4 by evidence strength for
answering the actual research question, using Session 4/11's ranking logic. Justify your #1 and
your #4 in 1–2 sentences each.

**A5 (12 pts) — Lateral-reading check.** Source 3 is commissioned by "a local conservation group,"
not an independent party with no stake. Does that fact alone disqualify its findings? What
specific detail in Source 3 (not its funding source) should actually determine how much to trust
it (hint: reread its own final sentence)?

**A6 (15 pts) — Contract/policy reading check (Source 5, exit can-do #4).** Using Session 9's
four questions (obligation, condition, waived right, narrowing clause), answer in your own words:
(a) beyond "letting a road be built," what specifically does §3 let the City do to the land
without further payment? (b) does §5's one-time payment cover future noise/vibration problems —
quote the exact phrase that answers this; (c) if someone buys this property years from now
without ever having seen this agreement, are they still bound by §7's restrictions? Quote the
clause that answers this; (d) name the single obligation in §7 most likely to surprise a landowner
who assumed they could landscape freely once the easement was granted.

**A7 (37 pts) — Final written synthesis (250–400 words).** Write a synthesis that a city council
member could actually use to make a decision. It must:

- State what's well-corroborated across Sources 1–4 (specific, not vague).
- State what remains genuinely contested or under-evidenced, and by which specific source's own
  admitted limitation.
- Represent the strongest version of BOTH the "build it" and "protect the wetland" positions
  fairly — not straw-manned.
- Explicitly flag Source 4's fallacious reasoning as rhetoric, not evidence, without dismissing
  that residents' fifty-year timeline concern (Source 2) is itself real and well-corroborated.
- Note, in one sentence, what Source 5 adds to the decision (the City will need affected
  landowners to sign an agreement with real, non-trivial terms — this is a real cost of the
  project, not just an engineering question).
- End with your own reasoned recommendation, naming the SPECIFIC piece of evidence that most
  influenced it, and one condition or piece of missing evidence that would change your mind.

## Rubric

| Score band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 90–100 | All tasks complete and accurate; A6 correctly quotes the narrowing/binding clauses; A7 cleanly separates fact/uncertainty/rhetoric, represents both sides fairly, names specific evidence for the final judgment, states a real condition that would change the writer's mind. |
| 80–89 (pass line) | A1–A6 mostly correct (allow 1 minor miss across all six); A7 present, fair to both sides, but may be vaguer on which specific evidence drove the judgment, or skip the "what would change my mind" element. |
| 60–79 | A1–A6 show gaps in 2+ tasks (e.g., missed a fallacy, misread a contract clause, or ranked evidence without justification); A7 picks a side without engaging the other fairly, or treats Source 4's rhetoric as if it were evidence. |
| Below 60 | Sources summarized separately with no real synthesis; fallacies or contract obligations not identified; A7 reads as a simple opinion unconnected to the sources. |

## Part B — Applied skills checklist (self- or tutor-assessed against your Part A answers)

Mark each ✓ (clearly demonstrated in Part A) or "not yet":

1. ☐ Sourced and contextualized a document (identified stake/bias) — A1, Source 2.
2. ☐ Applied claim/method/sample-size reading to a data or science source — A1, Sources 1 & 3.
3. ☐ Distinguished evidence from rhetoric/fallacy — A2.
4. ☐ Corroborated across independent sources — A3.
5. ☐ Weighed evidence strength with justification, not just intuition — A4.
6. ☐ Applied a lateral-reading/credibility judgment correctly (funding ≠ automatic invalidity;
   the actual tell is the source's own admitted limitation) — A5.
7. ☐ Represented opposing positions fairly in writing (no straw man) — A7.
8. ☐ Produced a genuine synthesis (not parallel summaries) with a stated, evidence-linked
   judgment — A7.
9. ☐ Read a contract/policy document and correctly stated an obligation, a condition, and a
   clause that narrows a friendlier general impression — A6 (Source 5, exit can-do #4).
10. ☐ Used Level 7 academic vocabulary correctly and unprompted across written answers (spot-check
    A1–A7 for accurate use of, e.g., *infer, corroborate, bias, correlation/causation, fallacy,
    claim/counterargument, obligation/clause/waive, synthesis* — exit can-do #10).

## If you don't pass yet

This is diagnostic, not a wall. Match your weakest task back to its session, redo that session's
Close reading and Write to read once more, and retake only Part A (a fresh attempt — the source
texts here can be reused; the point is the skill, not surprise): A1/A5 gaps → revisit Sessions
2–3, 8; A2 gaps → revisit Session 7; A3/A4 gaps → revisit Sessions 4–5, 10–11; A6 gaps → revisit
Session 9; A7 gaps generally → revisit Session 11's synthesis rubric specifically, since A7 is
built on exactly that structure at higher stakes.

**On passing:** a learner who passes this check, per the CEFR/PIAAC mapping in README.md, is
reading at CEFR C1 (stretching into C2 on literary/figurative text per Sessions 12–13) and
PIAAC Literacy Level 4–5 — the course's actual, evidence-grounded finish line (research/06 §1.11).
There is no Level 8; from here, the recommendation is real, self-selected reading across all five
disciplinary "rooms" from Session 1, at increasing volume, applying these moves without being
told to.
