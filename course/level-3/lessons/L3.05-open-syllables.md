# L3.05 — Open Syllables

**Goal:** Read single-letter open syllables (he, go, hi, me — a single vowel at the end of a word/syllable says its long sound) and two-syllable open+closed words (ro-bot, mu-sic) using V/CV division.
**New today:** open syllable long vowels (a e i o u alone at a syllable's end = long)
**Review:** all VCe patterns
**Heart words:** water, laugh (review: busy, people)
**Time:** 30 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Read: hoping, smiling, cute, home, time. Heart words: busy, people, great, eye.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say *he, we, go, so, hi*. "These are short words — just one consonant and one vowel, no silent e, and yet the vowel is long. Listen to where your mouth stops — the vowel is at the very end, nothing closes it in."

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
"A syllable that ends in a vowel with nothing after it is called an **open syllable** — the vowel is 'open,' free, so it says its long name. Compare: 'hop' (closed by p, short o) vs. 'ho' (open, long o, as in the first part of 'hotel')." For 2-syllable words, teach the **V/CV** division pattern: find the vowel, look at the next consonant, divide **before** it if it makes the first syllable open (long vowel) — check: does it make a real/recognizable word? ro-bot, mu-sic, pu-pil, fro-zen, ba-con.
**Flex-decode note:** V/CV doesn't always give a long vowel on the first try (e.g., "cabin" divides V/CV but is actually VC/CV with a short vowel) — so teach: "try open first (long vowel); if that's not a real word, try closed (short vowel) instead." This is the same flex-check habit from Lesson 3.03's u_e work.
**Urdu-speaker tip:** open-syllable long vowels in English (he, go) don't have a silent-letter cue the way VCe does — there is nothing to "spot," so rely on the rule (no consonant after the vowel = open = long).

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
**Single open syllables:** he, we, me, be, she, go, so, no, hi, I
**Two-syllable open+closed:** robot, music, pupil, frozen, bacon, tiger, spider, silent, program, item
**Pseudowords:** flobin, pademic (nonsense — apply V/CV, check it's not real, that's fine — it's a practice item), zoric, mubel, hitane

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate: 1) go 2) robot 3) music 4) "We go to see the tiger."

## 6. Heart words: water, laugh (2 min)
"Water — the 'wa' says /wo/, not the usual short a; whole word is a heart word for now. Laugh — the 'gh' says /f/, very unusual; whole word is a heart word." Say each 3x. Use: "I drink water." "We laugh a lot."

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

### Track A (child)
**The Silent Tiger**

> We go to spot a tiger. / The tiger is silent as it moves. / Because he had a project, a pupil at his class made a robot tiger. / It has one eye that shines. / "This is a great robot!" said the pupil. / We are so glad we came. / We go home and hum a tune, glad for the tigers.

Questions: 1) What did the pupil make? (literal) 2) What is unusual about the robot's eye? (literal) 3) What would you build if you could? (think)

### Track B (adult)
**A Great Program**

> Kate got a spot in a great program at her job site. / Because there were big tasks, she and the others made a robot to help. / The program runs a set of steps, silent and fast, no fuss. / "We tested it with one eye on the clock," said Kate. / The robot has done every task well. / Kate is glad she got to do this.

Questions: 1) What did Kate's team build? (literal) 2) How does Kate describe the program's steps? (literal) 3) What's a project you're proud you were part of? (think)

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud: *"Engineers who design robots must consider every possible task the machine will face, because a robot cannot adapt on its own the way a person can. A well-designed robot follows a precise set of instructions, and even a small gap in those instructions can cause a big mistake."*
Tier-2 word: **precise** — friendly definition: "exact, with no room for error." Examples: "A precise measurement uses the exact right amount." "She gave precise directions so no one got lost." Your turn: "Tell me about a time you had to be precise."
Discussion: 1) Why can't a robot adapt the way a person can? 2) When does being precise matter most in your life?

## 9. Check (2 min)
Real: go, robot, music, tiger, we. Pseudo: zoric, hitane, flobin, mubel, pademic. Dictated: "We go to see a robot."
**≥90%?** → L3.06. **<90%?** → Reteach V/CV with the "try open, then closed, check for real word" flex routine using 5 fresh 2-syllable words; do not move to y-as-vowel until this flex habit is solid, since Lesson 3.06 depends on the same check-a-real-word logic.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** reading 2-syllable open-syllable words as closed by default (saying "rob-ot" with a short o instead of "ro-bot"). Correction: cover the second syllable, read the first alone — "ro" — does it feel open? Then blend.
- **Common error:** treating single-letter open syllables (he, we, go) as heart words to memorize rather than decode. Correction: explicitly show the rule applies — "there's no consonant after the vowel, so it's open, so it's long — you can figure this one out, not just remember it."
- **For adults:** point out this rule explains many everyday words they already recognize by sight (hotel, total, item) — reframes "sight vocabulary" as decodable once the rule is known.
