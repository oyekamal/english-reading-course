# L3.01 — a_e (VCe with a)

**Goal:** Read and spell words with the vowel-consonant-e pattern using **a**, where a silent final e makes the vowel "say its name" (/ā/).
**New today:** a_e (cake, name)
**Review:** all Level 1–2 letters, digraphs, blends, closed syllables, suffixes -s/-es/-ed/-ing/-er
**Heart words:** once, only (first two of the 16 Level 3 heart words — canonical schedule teaches 2/lesson through L3.08)
**Time:** 25–30 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil; optional letter tiles or index cards for the "silent e slide-in" demo (optional/multisensory)

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Flash 5 known closed-syllable words: *plan, hop, cub, met, pin*. Read 5 known heart words: *are, they, once (new — hold), from, because*.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say *cap* / *cape*. "Listen — what's different?" (the vowel sound changes from short /a/ to long /ā/, and it sounds like the vowel is "saying its name.") Do 3 more oral pairs: *man/mane, tap/tape, rat/rate*. Learner identifies which word has the "long a."

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
Show: **a _ e**. "When a word ends in **vowel–consonant–e**, that final e is usually silent — it doesn't say its own sound. Its job is to reach back over one consonant and make the vowel before it say its name: /ā/." Write *cap* → add e and move it: *cape*. "The e slides in, the a says its name, the e stays silent." Mouth cue: /ā/ is a wide, relaxed open-mouth-then-glide sound — say *ay* as in "day" but do not write "ay" yet (that's Lesson 3.10; for now this is only the a_e spelling).
**Urdu-speaker tip:** Urdu has a long "aa" sound (as in "aap") but no silent-letter convention — a written letter that makes no sound of its own is a new idea. Say explicitly every time: "the e is silent — it just changes the a."
**Optional multisensory:** tap the vowel, sweep a finger from the e back to the vowel to show "the e reaches back."

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
Read these cumulatively, left to right, sound-by-sound then blended:
**Real words:** cake, name, same, made, gate, blame, plane, brave, shade, flame, grape, whale, skate, plate, snake, cane, cape, wave, cave, save
**Pseudowords ("alien words"):** mave, plake, snabe, trafe, zade

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate, segment with fingers first, then write: 1) cake 2) name 3) plane 4) "I made a cake." Check: did the silent e get added after one consonant, not two?

## 6. Heart words: once, only (2 min)
Say *once*. "The o says /w/ like in 'one,' the n-c-e spells /ns/. The whole first part is irregular — mark the whole word as a heart word, but notice it rhymes with the pattern we already know from 'one' (Level 1 heart word)." Write it, box it, say it 3 times.
Say *only*. "The 'on' part is regular (like 'once'), but the 'ly' here is easiest to learn as part of the whole word for now." Write it, box it, say it 3 times. Use both in a sentence: "I only ride it once."

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

### Track A (child)
**Jane and Kate at the Lake**

> Jane and Kate are at the lake. / Jane has a cane. / Kate has a cape. / They skate on the lake. / Once, Kate got a cramp. / Jane came and gave Kate a hand. / Kate said, "Thank you, Jane!" / They made a plan to skate again.

Questions: 1) What did Kate get? (literal) 2) What did Jane give Kate? (literal) 3) Why do you think Jane helped Kate? (think)

### Track B (adult)
**Jake's Plane**

> Jake has a case to take to the bank. / He made a plane trip. / At the gate, he got a snack and a cake. / Jake's plane was late, but he was not sad. / He said, "I have cash." / Jake sat and had a nap. / Once the plane came, he got on fast.

Questions: 1) What did Jake take to the bank? (literal) 2) Why was Jake not sad about the delay? (literal) 3) What do you do while you wait for something? (think)

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud TO the learner (above their decoding level): *"A long time ago, people who wanted to send a message far away used pigeons. A trained pigeon could find its way home from a hundred miles off. Today we call sending a fast, important message 'urgent' — it means it cannot wait."*
Tier-2 word: **urgent** — friendly definition: "something urgent needs attention right away, it can't wait." Examples: "A fire is an urgent problem." "Finishing homework the night before it's due can feel urgent." Your turn: "Tell me about a time something felt urgent to you."
Discussion: 1) How did people send fast messages before phones? 2) What makes something "urgent" instead of just "important"?

## 9. Check (2 min)
Real: cake, plane, same, gave, snake. Pseudo: fape, trane, zabe, plome (not a_e — should be misread/flagged as off-pattern, optional trick item), snade. Dictated: "Jane made a cake."
**≥90% (9/10)?** → go to L3.02.
**<90%?** → Reteach exact step 3 (the silent-e reach-back) with letter tiles; do NOT move on; re-check tomorrow with a fresh pseudoword set.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** reading the e as its own syllable ("cak-eh"). Correction: cover the e with a finger, read the word, then reveal the e and say "now the e makes the a say its name — read it again."
- **Common error:** adding the silent e to words that don't need it when spelling (e.g., "mate" → "matte" confusion goes the other way — usually the error is *omitting* the e: "cak" for "cake"). Correction: "Does one consonant sound cover the whole word, or is there a leftover e-job?" Have them check by covering the last letter.
- **For adults:** frame this explicitly as a *spelling convention*, not a phonics oddity — "English writing marks vowel length with a silent letter; many languages don't do this," which normalizes it as a system feature rather than an exception to memorize by rote.
- **For young kids:** the "silent e slide-in" (physically sliding an e-card into place after the consonant) makes the "reach-back" concrete; optional, not required.

## Decodability audit
**Heart words used:** a, are, he, of, said, the, they, once. "only" (this lesson's second new heart word, per the canonical 2/lesson schedule through L3.08) was not naturally usable in either practice text — it is still taught and checked explicitly in Step 6.
**Graphemes used beyond L1–2 code:** a_e (cake, name, same, made, gate, blame, plane, brave, cane, cape, wave, cave, save, skate, plate, snake, whale, flame, grape).
**Closed-syllable/blend/digraph review present:** cramp (cr- blend + -mp final blend), hand (-nd), thank (th + nk), case, bank, trip (tr-), snack (sn- + ck), cash (sh), fast (-st), sat, got, not, sad, gave, plan.
All words in both Track A and Track B texts are constructed only from Level 1–2 code + a_e + the heart words listed above. No pre-taught "story words" were needed this lesson.
