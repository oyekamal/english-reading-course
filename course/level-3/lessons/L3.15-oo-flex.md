# L3.15 — oo (Flex-Decoding Two Sounds)

**Goal:** Read words spelled with **oo**, which has two common sounds — /o͞o/ (moon) and /o͝o/ (book) — using an explicit flex-decode routine: try one sound, then the other, and check which gives a real word.
**New today:** oo — both sounds
**Review:** ue/ew/ui, u_e (same sound family as /o͞o/)
**Heart words:** answer (review: buy, talk, walk)
**Time:** 30 min (flex-decode routine needs full explanation time)
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Read: blue, new, fruit, chew, true. Heart words: buy, talk, water.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say *moon* then *book*. "Same spelling, oo — but two different sounds. Listen carefully: moon is a long, rounded /o͞o/. Book is a shorter, relaxed /o͝o/."

## 3. Meet it (3 min) — the flex-decode routine, explicit
"oo is the **first spelling you've met that genuinely has two common sounds with no simple rule to tell them apart just by looking**. Here is exactly what a skilled reader does with a word like this:
1. **Try the first sound** (usually /o͞o/, as in moon) — say the word.
2. **Ask: is that a real word I know?** If yes, you're done.
3. **If not, try the second sound** (/o͝o/, as in book) — say the word again.
4. **Ask again: is that a real word I know?** That's your answer.
This is decoding, not guessing — you are only checking your sound choice against your knowledge of real words, never against a picture or the surrounding sentence's meaning (that would be cueing, which we never do)." Model live with *moon* (first try /o͞o/ → real word, done) and *look* (first try /o͞o/ → "loonk," not real → try /o͝o/ → "look," real word, done).
**Urdu-speaker tip:** do a minimal-pair set explicitly — *full /o͝o/ vs fool /o͞o/*, *pull /o͝o/ vs pool /o͞o/* — these are genuinely separate English vowel phonemes that can both sound like a single rounded vowel to an Urdu-first ear; slow, side-by-side listening practice helps.

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
**/o͞o/ words:** moon, food, room, soon, cool, pool, tool, zoo, boot, roof
**/o͝o/ words:** book, look, took, foot, good, wood, hook, cook, stood, hood
**Mixed practice — flex the routine live on each:** moon, book, food, look, room, took, zoo, good
**Pseudowords (practice trying both sounds and picking the one that "sounds like a possible English word"):** troop-style "sproom," "blook," "twood," "froon," "gloot"

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate: 1) moon 2) book 3) look 4) "I took a good look at the moon."

## 6. Heart word: answer (2 min)
"Answer — the w is completely silent; whole word is a heart word." Say 3x. Use: "I know the answer."

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

### Track A (child)
**Looking at the Moon**

> At night, Sam and his dad look up at the moon. / "It is a good, cool night to look at the moon," said Dad. / They sit and look for as long as they can, because the moon is so bright. / Sam has a thing to think of: "What is the moon made of?" / Dad said, "That is a good thing to think of — let's find the answer in a book." / They read a book and find the answer, glad they took the time to look.

Questions: 1) What do Sam and his dad look at? (literal) 2) Where do they find the answer to Sam's question? (literal) 3) What question would you like to find the answer to? (think)

### Track B (adult)
**A Good Book on a Cool Evening**

> After her shift, Kate likes to sit in her room and read a good book. / When it is cool at night, she gets a hot cup and takes a look at her book. / She took a class once on this same topic, but a book gives her time to think it over. / "I find the answer to a lot of things in a good book," she said. / Soon, she is deep in the pages, glad for a quiet night.

Questions: 1) What does Kate do after her shift? (literal) 2) What does Kate say a book gives her? (literal) 3) What do you like to do to unwind after your shift? (think)

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud: *"Reading fiction before bed has been shown to help some people unwind, because following a story shifts attention away from the day's worries and toward a different, absorbing world."*
Tier-2 word: **absorbing** — friendly definition: "so interesting that it holds your full attention." Examples: "The movie was so absorbing, she forgot to eat." "He found the puzzle absorbing and lost track of time." Your turn: "Tell me about something you found absorbing."
Discussion: 1) Why might a story help someone unwind before bed? 2) What do you find absorbing enough to lose track of time?

## 9. Check (2 min)
Read each and apply the flex routine out loud (say which sound worked): moon, book, look, food, good, zoo, took, room.
Then real+pseudo mixed check: moon, book, soon, hook, cool. Pseudo: sproom, blook, twood, froon, gloot. Dictated: "I took a good look at the book."
**≥90% AND can narrate the flex routine unprompted for at least 2 of the 8 words above?** → L3.16. **<90% or can't narrate the routine?** → Reteach step 3 explicitly with the moon/look worked examples again; this routine is foundational for Level 4's r-controlled and diphthong flex-decoding, so do not rush past it.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** picking one oo sound and sticking with it for every new word without checking. Correction: insist on the two-step check every time for an unfamiliar word — this is the whole point of the lesson.
- **Common error:** treating the flex-check as "guessing" and getting discouraged. Correction: clarify explicitly — "you are not guessing the word; you already know both possible sounds. You're just checking which one makes a word you know. That's different from guessing from a picture."
- **For adults:** this is a good moment to name the skill directly — "you now have a strategy for any spelling that has more than one sound, which will come up again and again as you read more English." Confidence-building, not just a drill.
