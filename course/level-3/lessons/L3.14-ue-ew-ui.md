# L3.14 — ue, ew, ui

**Goal:** Read and spell the vowel teams **ue**, **ew**, and **ui**, which usually say /o͞o/ (as in "moon") and sometimes /ū/ (as in "cute").
**New today:** ue, ew, ui
**Review:** u_e (same sound family, different spelling)
**Heart words:** buy (review: talk, walk, laugh)
**Time:** 25–30 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Read: night, light, pie, high, bright. Heart words: talk, walk, water.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say *blue, chew, fruit*. "All the same /o͞o/ family you met with u_e — three more spellings."

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
"**ue** at the end of a word (blue, glue, true, clue). **ew** at the end of a word (chew, new, grew, blew). **ui** in the middle of a word (fruit, suit) — a smaller, less common set." Model sorting.
**Flex-decode reminder:** some ue/ew words say /ū/ instead of /o͞o/ (few, view is /ū/... actually "few" and "view" contain other patterns — for this lesson, keep the word list to the clean /o͞o/ set to avoid confusion; the /ū/-vs-/o͞o/ split you already practiced with u_e in Lesson 3.03 — apply the same "try one, check the other" habit if a new word doesn't sound right.
**Urdu-speaker tip:** /o͞o/ is a fairly close match to Urdu او-type rounded vowel; the three-spelling variety (ue/ew/ui) is the new content, not the sound itself.

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
**ue words:** blue, glue, true, clue, due
**ew words:** chew, new, grew, blew, flew, drew, crew, stew
**ui words:** fruit, suit
**Pseudowords:** drue, sprew, fluit, glew, treu

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate: 1) blue 2) new 3) fruit 4) "I have a new blue suit."

## 6. Heart word: buy (2 min)
"Buy — the u-y spelling is unusual and the whole word must be learned as a heart word; do not confuse with 'by' (heart word, Level 3.06 lesson) or 'bye.'" Say 3x. Use: "I will buy a new bag."

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

### Track A (child)
**A New Blue Bike**

> Sam and his mom go to buy a new bike. / "I want the blue one," said Sam. / The shop has a big, blue bike that is true to what Sam likes. / Sam's mom said, "Let's buy it, then get a snack." / They get a bag of fruit and chew on it as they walk home with the new bike.

Questions: 1) What color bike does Sam want? (literal) 2) What snack do they get after buying the bike? (literal) 3) What would you like to buy if you had the chance? (think)

### Track B (adult)
**Buying a New Suit**

> Because she had a big meeting, Kate had to buy a new suit. / She went to a shop and got a blue suit that fit true to size. / "This one is right for you," said the person at the shop. / Kate said, "That is true, I will buy this one." / She got a snack of fruit on her way home, glad to have a new suit for the big day.

Questions: 1) Why did Kate need a new suit? (literal) 2) What did the clerk say about the suit? (literal) 3) What do you buy when you need to look your best? (think)

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud: *"Choosing an outfit for an important meeting is about more than looking neat — it can genuinely affect how confident a person feels walking into the room, which in turn can affect how well they perform."*
Tier-2 word: **confident** — friendly definition: "sure of yourself, believing you can do something well." Examples: "She felt confident after she practiced her speech." "A confident smile can put others at ease." Your turn: "Tell me about a time you felt confident."
Discussion: 1) How might clothing affect how confident someone feels? 2) What makes you feel confident?

## 9. Check (2 min)
Real: blue, new, fruit, chew, true. Pseudo: glew, drue, sprew, fluit, treu. Dictated: "I will buy a new blue bag."
**≥90%?** → L3.15. **<90%?** → Reteach the position rule (ue/ew end-word, ui mid-word) with a sort activity; re-check with fresh words.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** confusing ue/ew endings (writing "chue" for "chew"). Correction: there is no hard rule to distinguish ue vs ew by sound alone — flag this as a short list of words to learn by pattern-recognition over time, same as ee/ea in Lesson 3.09.
- **Common error:** trying to use ui at the end of a word (rare/wrong) instead of ue. Correction: remind that ui is a middle-of-word pattern, and a small one (fruit, suit are the main examples).
- **For adults:** connect explicitly back to u_e (cute vs June) from Lesson 3.03 — same sound family, now with three more spellings; naming the connection reduces the feeling of "yet another new sound."
