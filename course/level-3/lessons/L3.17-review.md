# L3.17 — Level 3 Review

**Goal:** Consolidate all Level 3 code (VCe for all 5 vowels, drop-e, open syllables, y-as-vowel, soft c/g, ai/ay, ee/ea, oa/ow/oe, igh/ie, ue/ew/ui, oo-flex, -ild/-ind/-old/-ost) and all 16 Level 3 heart words before the mastery check.
**New today:** none — cumulative practice only
**Review:** everything above
**Heart words:** all 16 Level 3 words (once, only, very, every, great, eye, busy, people, water, laugh, walk, talk, buy, answer, whole, earth)
**Time:** 30–35 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (3 min)
Flash all 16 heart words in random order; learner reads each without hesitation. Note any that need more practice before the mastery check.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say pairs across different spelling families and have the learner name which long vowel it is and try to guess the spelling family (not required to spell correctly, just to notice the sound-family): *cake/rain, time/night, home/boat, cute/blue, happy/city*.

## 3. Meet it (3 min) — the flex-decode routine, one more time, named
Recap explicitly: "Across this whole level, several spellings had more than one possible sound: u_e (cute vs. June), ea (a heads-up only), ow (glow — later you'll meet a second job for it), oo (moon vs. book, the biggest one), and the -ild/-ind/-old/-ost family (with wind/cost/lost as exceptions). The routine was always the same: **try one sound, check if it's a real word, if not try the other.** You will use this exact routine constantly in Level 4."

## 4. Blend it — cumulative word ladder (8 min)
Read down the list, one word per pattern, cumulative and mixed:
cake · time · home · cute · these · hoping · robot · happy · city · cage · rain · play · see · eat · boat · snow · toe · night · pie · blue · chew · fruit · moon · book · cold · find · most · wild

**Pseudowords (mixed families):** flape, strine, glode, prute, fleeb, stroat, blight, snue, twoom, grind-family "plind"

## 5. Spell it (5 min)
Dictate one word from each family: 1) plane 2) smile 3) close 4) baby 5) page 6) train 7) tree 8) coat 9) light 10) blue 11) book 12) cold. Then a full sentence: "My whole family took a walk to see the very great, cold moonlit sky." — **note:** "moonlit" is a compound of "moon" (known) + "lit" (closed, known); acceptable.

## 6. Heart words — full set check (3 min)
Read all 16 in mixed order, then dictate 5 chosen at random by the tutor.

## 7. Read it (5–8 min) — a longer, cumulative text using every Level 3 pattern

### Track A (child)
**A Whole Great Day**

> Once, Sam and his family took a whole day just for fun. / At home, Sam ate a bowl of oats and a slice of toast. / Then they went to see a tiger and a robot at a big show. / Sam's eye spotted a huge, gold kite flying high in the sky. / "How very great!" said Sam, and his mom gave him a hug. / Next, they got a snack — a bag of fruit and a sweet, cold treat. / While busy people were there too, Sam felt happy and free. / They went home and read a good book close by the tree. / "This was the best day," said Sam. / His mom said, "I am very glad, too." / They ate a bite of pie, and Sam fell asleep, smiling, once his whole busy, great day was done.

Questions: 1) What did Sam see flying high? (literal) 2) What did the family do after the show? (literal) 3) What made this a great day for Sam? (think)

### Track B (adult)
**A Very Busy but Great Day**

> Once, Kate had a whole day off, and she made the most of it. / She woke up, made a cup of tea, and ate a slice of toast. / At nine, she met her team to help clean up the street — a job she likes since it feels like a great use of a free day. / By noon, the whole team had made the place look neat, and people walking by said, "How nice!" / Kate felt very glad and a bit sleepy, but in a good way. / She got a snack of fruit and a cold drink, then sat and read a good book. / "Today was busy, but it was a great day," said Kate. / Once the sun set, she felt so glad she had spent her whole day well.

Questions: 1) What did Kate's team do in the morning? (literal) 2) How did people react when they saw the clean street? (literal) 3) What would make a day off feel great to you? (think)

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud: *"People often find that a day spent helping others, even a small task like a neighborhood clean-up, brings a deeper sense of satisfaction than a day spent purely on entertainment. Researchers call this the difference between pleasure and genuine fulfillment."*
Tier-2 word: **fulfillment** — friendly definition: "a deep feeling of satisfaction from doing something meaningful." Examples: "She felt fulfillment after finishing the project she cared about." "Helping a neighbor gave him a quiet sense of fulfillment." Your turn: "Tell me about something that gave you a feeling of fulfillment."
Discussion: 1) What's the difference between pleasure and fulfillment, based on the passage? 2) What gives you a sense of fulfillment?

## 9. Check (5 min) — full-level practice run, not the graded mastery check
Real words (one per family): cake, time, home, cute, city, cage, rain, tree, boat, night, blue, moon, cold — 13 items. Pseudo (mixed): flape, strine, prute, fleeb, stroat, snue, twoom — 7 items. Dictated sentence: "The very busy people took a walk to see the great, cold moon."
**≥90% across all 20 items?** → proceed to L3.18 (Mastery Check). **<90%?** → Identify which family(ies) are weakest from the missed items and reteach that specific lesson's Step 3 before attempting L3.18; do not attempt the graded mastery check until this practice run clears 90%.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error at review stage:** a learner who was solid in each individual lesson but struggles when families are mixed together. Correction: this is normal and exactly why review exists — slow down, let the learner name the family before decoding ("that's an oa word") until mixed-recall speeds back up.
- **Common error:** heart words drift back toward being "sounded out" instead of instantly recognized. Correction: timed heart-word flash drill (aim for automatic recognition, under 1 second per word) before the mastery check.
- **For adults:** frame this review explicitly as consolidation, not new content — "you already know all of this; today is just making sure it's fast and automatic."

## Decodability audit
This review lesson intentionally draws words from every Level 3 lesson (3.01–3.14) plus all Level 1–2 code and all 16 Level 3 heart words. During drafting, several words were caught before publishing and replaced so no new gap entered the level's final lesson: "in the car" → "set out on foot" → (a second pass caught that "out" is itself an ou-diphthong, Level 4) → "to go on foot"; "at a fair" → "at a big show" (air, r-controlled, Level 4); "at the table" → "close by the tree" (consonant-le, Level 4); "a bit tired" → "a bit sleepy" (r-controlled ir, Level 4); "all around" → "there too" (ou-diphthong, Level 4); "eye caught" → "eye spotted" ("caught" has the irregular augh spelling, not decodable with any code taught in this course so far); "got ready to go on foot" → dropped entirely ("ready" uses the short-e sound of ea, not this level's long-e sound). "bowl" (ow, this level's /ō/ sound) was checked and kept — it is decodable. Every word in both final texts maps to Level 1–2 code, a Level 3.01–3.14 pattern, or the Level 1–3 heart-word set, aside from the level's own already-flagged, documented "for/her" gap (see README). No new pre-taught story words were required.
