# L3.08 — ar

**Goal:** Read and spell words with r-controlled **ar** (/ar/, as in car, farm, start) — the first r-controlled vowel, taught here (matching UFLI/RWI sequencing) before any vowel team so common everyday words stop leaking into earlier lessons.
**New today:** ar
**Review:** VCe (all 5 vowels), drop-e, open syllables, y=/ē//ī/, soft c/g, -ce/-ge/-dge
**Heart words:** whole, earth (last two of the 16 Level 3 heart words — all Level 3 heart words are now taught)
**Time:** 25–30 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Read: cake, time, robot, happy, city, page. Heart words: buy, answer, water.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say *car, far, star*. "Listen — the vowel sound changes when r comes right after it. It isn't a short a or a long a; it's a whole new sound, /ar/, and the r is part of the vowel, not a separate sound after it."

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
"When **r** follows a vowel, the r 'takes over' and changes the vowel sound — we call this **r-controlled**. Today's r-controlled vowel is **ar**, which says /ar/: car, far, star, park, yard, start, hard, farm, cart, shark, sharp, dark, part, smart, card, yarn, barn, harm, charm. It also appears inside longer words: mar-ket, gar-den, car-pet — read it the same way, one chunk at a time."
**Urdu-speaker tip:** Urdu has a rolled/tapped r that colors the vowel differently than English's r-controlled vowels; the English /ar/ is one blended sound, not "a" followed by a separate rolled "r" — model it as one continuous sound.

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
**Real words:** car, far, star, park, yard, start, hard, farm, cart, shark, sharp, dark, part, smart, card, yarn, barn, harm, charm, market, garden, carpet
**Pseudowords:** blar, starj, trarf, sparn, flarn

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate: 1) car 2) park 3) start 4) "The car is in the yard."

## 6. Heart words: whole, earth (2 min)
"Whole — the w is silent; the rest looks like an open-syllable word you already know ('ho'), but treat the whole word as a heart word. Earth — the 'ear' part says a sound you have not been taught yet; whole word is a heart word." Say each 3x. Use: "I ate the whole plate." "The earth is round." This completes all 16 Level 3 heart words — from the next lesson onward, every heart word your learner needs for this level has already been taught.

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

### Track A (child)

> Sam and his mom go to the farm. / A big, red barn is at the farm. / Sam pets a cute pup and a fat pig. / "A big cart!" said Sam. / He rides in the cart with his mom, because he likes it a lot. / They spot a hen in the yard. / Once they are done, they have a snack of grapes. / Sam has a whole, fine time at the farm.

Questions: 1) What does Sam ride in? (literal) 2) What do Sam and his mom find in the yard? (literal) 3) What would you like to see at a farm? (think)

### Track B (adult)

> Kate got a job at the market. / She helps at a cart that sells fresh grapes and plums. / At nine, she sets up the cart and starts her shift. / "This job is hard, but I like it," said Kate. / She helps many people at the market, because she likes to help. / Once her shift is done, Kate has a snack and a cup of milk. / She has a whole, fine time at the market.

Questions: 1) Where does Kate work? (literal) 2) What does Kate say about her job? (literal) 3) What is a job you think would be hard but good? (think)

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud: *"Farmers markets let shoppers buy food directly from the people who grew it, which often means the produce is fresher than what has traveled a long distance to a big store. Buying this way also supports the local economy."*
Tier-2 word: **economy** — friendly definition: "the way money and goods move around in a place — who earns it, who spends it, who it helps." Examples: "Buying from local shops helps the local economy." "A country's economy can grow or shrink." Your turn: "Tell me one way you could help your local economy this week."
Discussion: 1) Why might food from a farmers market be fresher? 2) What's one small way people can support their local economy?

## 9. Check (2 min)
Real: car, park, farm, start, yard. Pseudo: blar, starj, trarf, sparn, flarn. Dictated: "Sam and his mom start the car."
**≥90%?** → L3.09. **<90%?** → Reteach step 3 with the "r takes over the vowel" framing using a fresh set of ar words (spark, chart, harp, tart, art); re-check tomorrow.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** reading ar as two separate sounds (a short-a sound, then a rolled r). Correction: model it as one continuous blended sound — "ar is its own sound, said in one breath, not a + r."
- **Common error:** confusing ar with the plain short-a + r-as-a-separate-consonant reading some learners try on 2-syllable words (e.g., reading "market" as "ma-r-ket"). Correction: chunk it as "mar-ket," treating "mar" as one r-controlled unit.
- **For adults:** name explicitly that r-controlled vowels are a small, closed set (this lesson: ar; next lesson: or/ore; Level 4 finishes the set with er/ir/ur and air/are/ear/eer) — knowing there's an end point to the "new vowel sound" category reduces overwhelm.
