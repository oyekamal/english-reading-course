# L3.10 — ai, ay

**Goal:** Read and spell the long-a vowel team spelled **ai** (mid-word) and **ay** (end of word).
**New today:** ai, ay (both /ā/)
**Review:** a_e (same sound, different spelling)
**Heart words:** water (review: people, busy, once)
**Time:** 25–30 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Read: cage, city, bridge, huge, my. Heart words: people, great, busy.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say *rain, play, train, day*. "All of these have the long a sound — /ā/ — but spelled two new ways today."

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
"**ai** is used in the **middle** of a word (rain, train, paint). **ay** is used at the **end** of a word (day, play, stay) — English spelling almost never ends a word in plain 'ai.' So: middle of word → ai; end of word → ay. Same sound, position tells you the spelling." Model sorting words into the two columns by position.
**Urdu-speaker tip:** the /ā/ sound itself was already learned via a_e; today is purely a spelling-position rule, not a new sound to hear.

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
**ai (mid-word):** rain, paint, train, chain, wait, main, tail, snail, brain, faint
**ay (end-word):** day, play, stay, way, say, tray, gray, spray, clay, stray
**Pseudowords:** fraim, snay, blain, gaint, chay

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate: 1) rain 2) play 3) train 4) "We play in the rain."

## 6. Heart word: water (2 min)
"Water — the 'wa' says /wo/, not the usual short a; whole word is a heart word for now, though you'll see it decode more clearly once you know 'aw'-family sounds (later level)." Say 3x. Use: "I drink water every day."

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

### Track A (child)
**Rain on Play Day**

> It is play day at the park. / When rain starts, the class does not stay in — they play in the rain, and get wet from top to tail! / A snail comes on the wet path. / "There is a snail!" said Sam. / They wait for the rain to stop, then drink cold water and play again.

Questions: 1) What comes out when it rains? (literal) 2) What do the kids do when the rain starts? (literal) 3) What do you like to do on a rainy day? (think)

### Track B (adult)
**A Rainy Commute**

> Every day, Kate takes the train to her job. / Today, because rain made the stop wet, the train came late. / She had to wait, so she got a cup of hot water and a snack from a stand. / "I will just wait," she said to a man in line. / The train came, and Kate got a spot by chance.

Questions: 1) What made Kate's commute different today? (literal) 2) What did Kate do while she waited? (literal) 3) How do you stay calm when a plan is delayed? (think)

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud: *"When trains are delayed during heavy rain, it is often because water can seep into the electrical system that controls the tracks, forcing a temporary shutdown until it is safe again."*
Tier-2 word: **temporary** — friendly definition: "lasting only for a short time, not permanent." Examples: "A temporary fix will do for now, but we need a real one later." "The rain caused a temporary delay." Your turn: "Tell me about something temporary that felt like it lasted forever."
Discussion: 1) Why do trains sometimes stop running in heavy rain? 2) What's something in your life that was temporary but felt long?

## 9. Check (2 min)
Real: rain, play, train, day, wait. Pseudo: snay, blain, gaint, chay, fraim. Dictated: "We play in the rain."
**≥90%?** → L3.11. **<90%?** → Reteach the position rule (mid-word=ai, end-word=ay) with a sorting task; re-check tomorrow.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** writing "day" as "dai" or "rain" as "rayn" — mixing the two spellings. Correction: "Is the /ā/ sound in the middle of the word, or right at the end? That decides ai or ay."
- **Common error:** reading "said" (heart word, already known) as if it were an ai-word (/sād/). Correction: remind that "said" is a heart word already learned — it does not follow today's new rule.
- **For adults:** point out that this position-based rule (ai mid-word, ay end-word) is close to exceptionless — a rare case in English spelling worth naming as such, which builds confidence in the system.
