# L3.02 — i_e (VCe with i)

**Goal:** Read and spell VCe words with **i** (long /ī/).
**New today:** i_e (time, bike)
**Review:** a_e; all Level 1–2 code
**Heart words:** very, every (review: once, only)
**Time:** 25–30 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Read 5 a_e words from L3.01 (cake, name, gave, plane, skate). Read heart words: once, only, are, said.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Oral pairs: *pin/pine, kit/kite, rid/ride*. "Which one has the long i — the sound that says its own name, like the letter 'i'?"

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
"Same job as last time: vowel–consonant–e. This time the e reaches back to make **i** say its name: /ī/." Build *rip* → *ripe*.
**Urdu-speaker tip:** English long /ī/ (as in "time") is close to Urdu اے-like diphthong in some dialects but is genuinely a two-part glide (/ah-ee/); have the learner feel the jaw drop then close.

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
**Real:** time, bike, like, five, hide, ride, side, wide, mile, smile, slide, prize, drive, white, while
**Pseudo:** fripe, blite, stide, grine, quibe

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate: 1) time 2) bike 3) smile 4) "I like my bike."

## 6. Heart words: very, every (2 min)
"Very — the v-e-r part is regular, but the whole word is easiest to learn as one unit for now." Box it, say 3x, use in a sentence: "I am very glad." "Every — 'ev' + 'ery,' both roughly decodable once you know more patterns, but treat the whole word as a heart word for now." Box it, say 3x, use in a sentence: "I read every day."

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

### Track A (child)
**Mike's Bike Ride**

> Mike has a bike. / He likes to ride it. / Mike and his dog Spike go for a ride. / Once, Mike's bike went flat. / Mike did not stop. / He got off and did a quick fix. / Spike sat still. / "Only a bit," said Mike, and they went back.

Questions: 1) What is the name of Mike's dog? (literal) 2) What went wrong on the ride? (literal) 3) Why do you think Mike did not stop? (think)

### Track B (adult)
**A Bike to Save Time**

> Kate got a bike to save time. / She rides it to her job many times. / At nine, she locks the bike and clocks in. / Once, the bike would not go, and Kate had to get off and push it. / She was not glad, but she did not quit. / Only a quick fix did it, and the next ride was fine.

Questions: 1) Why did Kate get a bike? (literal) 2) What happened once, on one ride? (literal) 3) Have you ever had a plan go wrong but still worked out? (think)

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud: *"Before bikes had rubber tires, riders used wheels with iron rims. Every bump in the road shook the whole bike — people called it a 'bone-shaker.' A ride that should have felt smooth instead felt rough and tiring."*
Tier-2 word: **rough** — friendly definition: "not smooth; bumpy or hard to deal with." Examples: "A rough road shakes the car." "He had a rough day at work." Your turn: "Tell me about something rough you've felt or a rough day you've had."
Discussion: 1) Why did old bikes feel rough to ride? 2) What's the difference between rough and smooth?

## 9. Check (2 min)
Real: time, bike, smile, ride, five. Pseudo: grine, fripe, stide, blite, quibe. Dictated: "I like my bike."
**≥90%?** → L3.03. **<90%?** → Reteach step 3 with i vs a contrast (rip/ripe vs cap/cape) side by side.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** confusing i_e with a_e sound once both are known — mixing up which vowel says which name. Correction: point to the vowel letter itself: "what letter is right before the consonant? That's the one that says its name."
- **Common error:** b/d reversal inside VCe words (bike vs "dike"). Correction: hand-shape cue (make a bed with thumbs, "b" is the headboard on the left) — optional multisensory, not required.
- **For adults:** note explicitly that this is the *same rule* as Lesson 1, applied to a new vowel — reduces the feeling of "another whole new thing to learn."

## Decodability audit
