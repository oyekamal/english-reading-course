# L3.07 — Soft c, Soft g

**Goal:** Read words where c says /s/ (soft c, before e/i/y) and g says /j/ (soft g, before e/i/y), plus the endings -ce, -ge, -dge.
**New today:** soft c (city, ice), soft g (gem, cage), -ce, -ge, -dge
**Review:** VCe, open syllables, y-as-vowel
**Heart words:** buy, answer (review: walk, talk)
**Time:** 25–30 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Read: happy, my, home, robot, funny. Heart words: walk, talk, busy, people.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say *cat* vs *city*. "Same letter c — different sound. Listen: /k/ in cat, /s/ in city." Same for *go* vs *gem*: /g/ vs /j/.

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
"C usually says /k/ (cat, cup) — but before **e, i, or y**, it softens to /s/ (city, ice, cycle). G usually says /g/ (go, gum) — but before **e, i, or y**, it often softens to /j/ (gem, giant, gym) — though g has more exceptions than c (get, give stay hard — flag these as exceptions to notice, not a broken rule)." Also teach: **-ce** (dance, since), **-ge** (cage, huge), **-dge** (bridge, edge — dge is used right after a short vowel, ge after a long vowel or consonant).
**Flex-decode note:** when g is followed by e/i/y, try soft /j/ first; if the result isn't a recognizable word (e.g., "get," "give," "girl"), try hard /g/ instead — check against a real word, same routine as always.
**Urdu-speaker tip:** Urdu already distinguishes these two consonant qualities as separate letters/sounds; the challenge here is purely that English reuses one letter (c or g) for both, so the *rule* (what follows the letter) is the new learning, not the sounds themselves.

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
**Soft c:** city, ice, nice, rice, race, place, since, cent, cell, circle
**Soft g:** gem, cage, huge, page, stage, giant, gym, large, change, danger
**-dge:** bridge, edge, judge, fudge, ledge
**Pseudowords:** flice, gede, stadge, plage, wince

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate: 1) nice 2) cage 3) bridge 4) "The city has a big bridge."

## 6. Heart words: buy, answer (2 min)
"Buy — the u-y spelling is unusual; whole word is a heart word — do not confuse with 'by,' already known. Answer — the w is completely silent; whole word is a heart word." Say each 3x. Use: "I will buy a new bag." "I know the answer."

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

### Track A (child)
**The Bridge in the City**

> In the city, there is a big bridge. / Many people cross it often. / Once, my class went on a trip to visit it, because it is huge and made of stage-like steps. / When we got to the edge, we could spot a huge cage with a pet inside. / "So nice!" said the people, very glad. / We had a fine time at the bridge.

Questions: 1) What is huge and made of steps? (literal) 2) What did people see at the edge? (literal) 3) What is a nice place you have visited? (think)

### Track B (adult)
**A Change in Plans**

> Since the bridge in the city closed, many people had to change the plan. / Many people came to fix it, page by page of a plan they had made. / "It is a huge job, but it is not a bad job if we take the time to do it well," said the judge. / People are glad the bridge will be fine once it is fixed.

Questions: 1) Why did people have to change their plans? (literal) 2) What did the person in charge say about the job? (literal) 3) What do you do when your plans have to change? (think)

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud: *"Engineers who inspect a bridge look for tiny cracks long before the bridge is actually in danger of failing. Catching a problem early, while it is still minor, is far cheaper and safer than waiting until it becomes a crisis."*
Tier-2 word: **minor** — friendly definition: "small, not serious." Examples: "A minor scrape just needs a bandage." "It was a minor mistake, easy to fix." Your turn: "Tell me about a minor problem you fixed easily."
Discussion: 1) Why is catching a problem early better than waiting? 2) What's the difference between a minor and a major problem?

## 9. Check (2 min)
Real: city, cage, bridge, nice, huge. Pseudo: flice, gede, stadge, plage, wince(real — swap for "tredge"). Dictated: "The city has a nice bridge."
**≥90%?** → L3.08. **<90%?** → Reteach soft c/g with a sorting activity (hard vs soft, sorted by the letter that follows) before re-checking; flag any g-exceptions (get, give, girl) explicitly as memorized exceptions, not rule failures.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** applying soft c/g to every c/g regardless of the following letter (saying "/s/at" for "cat"). Correction: "What letter comes right after the c/g? Only e, i, y make it soft."
- **Common error:** confusing -ge and -dge (writing "brige" for "bridge"). Correction: teach the short-vowel check — "dge follows a short vowel sound (bridge, edge); ge follows a long vowel or another consonant (huge, page, change)."
- **For adults:** note that "get" and "give" are memorized exceptions to the soft-g rule, worth naming explicitly rather than letting the learner discover the rule "breaks."
