# L3.09 — or, ore

**Goal:** Read and spell words with r-controlled **or** (for, corn, short) and **ore** (more, store, before).
**New today:** or, ore
**Review:** ar
**Heart words:** none new — all 16 Level 3 heart words were completed at L3.08; today is decoding-only.
**Time:** 25–30 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Read: car, park, farm, start, market. Heart words: whole, earth, buy, answer.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say *for, corn, more, store*. "Same idea as ar — the r controls the vowel — but this time it's /or/."

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
"**or** usually appears in the middle or end of a short word: for, corn, fork, horse, storm, short, sport, north, born, torn. **ore** appears at the end of a word and says the same /or/ sound: more, store, before, wore, tore, core, snore, score, shore, chore." Model sorting: or in the middle vs. ore at the end.
**Flag, do not teach today:** a few or-spelled words (work, word, worm, world, worth) say a completely different sound — that's a separate quirk taught later, in Level 4; skip past any of those if they come up and just tell the learner the word.
**Urdu-speaker tip:** /or/ and /ar/ can sound close to an Urdu-first ear since both are r-colored back vowels; a quick side-by-side (car/for, star/store) helps the ear separate them.

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
**or words:** for, corn, fork, horse, storm, short, sport, north, born, torn
**ore words:** more, store, before, wore, tore, core, snore, score, shore, chore
**Pseudowords:** florp, storn, blore, trork, snorm

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate: 1) for 2) store 3) short 4) "I got a fork at the store."

## 6. Heart word review (2 min)
No new heart word today. Flash all 16 Level 3 heart words (once, only, very, every, great, eye, busy, people, water, laugh, walk, talk, buy, answer, whole, earth) for instant recognition.

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

### Track A (child)

> At the farm, Sam spots a horse. / The horse has a short, dark mane. / "Can I ride the horse?" asked Sam. / "Yes, hold on well," said his mom. / Sam gets on, and the horse trots because he holds on well. / Sam has a fun, short ride. / Once he is done, Sam pets the horse and says, "Thank you." / Sam has a whole, fine time at the farm.

Questions: 1) What does Sam ride? (literal) 2) What does the horse's mane look like? (literal) 3) What would you say to a horse you just rode? (think)

### Track B (adult)

> Kate has a list, so she goes to the store. / She gets corn, milk, and a snack from a stand. / Before she is done, she checks her list. / "I got what I came for," said Kate. / She goes to the front and gets a short line. / She is glad she made a trip to the store, because she got what she came for.

Questions: 1) What did Kate go to buy? (literal) 2) What does Kate check before she is done? (literal) 3) What is on your list next time you go to a store? (think)

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud: *"Before horses could be domesticated for riding, humans had to build a relationship of trust with an animal that is naturally alert to danger. A horse that trusts its rider will respond calmly even in a busy, noisy place."*
Tier-2 word: **domesticated** — friendly definition: "tamed and cared for by people, instead of living wild." Examples: "A domesticated horse can be ridden safely." "Dogs and cats are domesticated animals." Your turn: "Tell me about a domesticated animal you know."
Discussion: 1) Why do horses need to trust their rider? 2) What's the difference between a wild animal and a domesticated one?

## 9. Check (2 min)
Real: for, store, short, horse, corn. Pseudo: florp, storn, blore, trork, snorm. Dictated: "I got corn at the store."
**≥90%?** → L3.10. **<90%?** → Reteach step 3, sorting or (mid/end) vs. ore (end only) with a fresh word set; re-check tomorrow.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** trying to read "work"/"word" with the regular /or/ sound. Correction: these are a separate, later-taught exception — just tell the learner the word for now, don't drill it.
- **Common error:** confusing or/ore with ar (e.g., reading "for" as "far"). Correction: point to the exact letter right after the vowel-r chunk and contrast the two words side by side.
- **For adults:** point out or/ore closes out the two most common r-controlled spellings; er/ir/ur (a third spelling of a similar sound) comes in Level 4 — naming the pattern keeps the "new sound" count feeling finite.
