# L3.12 — oa, ow, oe

**Goal:** Read and spell the long-o vowel team spelled **oa** (mid-word), **ow** (often end-word), and **oe** (rare, end-word).
**New today:** oa, ow (/ō/), oe
**Review:** ai/ay, ee/ea; open-syllable long o (go, so, no)
**Heart words:** walk (review: laugh, water, once)
**Time:** 25–30 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Read: see, team, sleep, eat, green. Heart words: laugh, water, people.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say *boat, snow, toe*. "All long o — /ō/ — three spellings today."

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
"**oa** is used in the **middle** of a word (boat, coat, road, soap). **ow** (long-o sound) is common at the **end** of a word (snow, grow, own is an exception with a different vowel sound covered later — flag it) or before a final consonant (own, shown). **oe** is rare, mostly at the very end of a short list of words (toe, hoe, foe, doe)." Sort words by pattern.
**Flex-decode note:** ow already has a *different* job you have not been taught yet — later you will meet ow saying /ow/ as in "cow." For now, every ow word we meet says /ō/. If a new ow word doesn't sound right as /ō/, try /ow/ instead and check.
**Urdu-speaker tip:** the /ō/ sound is a fairly clean match to Urdu او — the challenge here is purely the three English spellings, not the sound itself.

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
**oa words:** boat, coat, road, soap, coach, toast, float, goat, foam, roast
**ow words (this lesson's sound only):** snow, grow, show, slow, blow, glow, flow, low, own, know
**oe words:** toe, hoe, foe, doe
**Pseudowords:** proat, snoat, droe, hoat, sloat

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate: 1) boat 2) snow 3) toe 4) "We row the boat in the snow."

## 6. Heart word: walk (2 min)
"Walk — the l is silent, and the 'al' says /aw/, a sound you have not met yet; whole word is a heart word for now." Say 3x. Use: "We walk to the shop."

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

### Track A (child)
**A Boat in the Snow**

> Sam and his dad walk to the pond with a fine boat. / When snow starts, the pond starts to glow white. / Because it is chilly, Sam puts on his coat and gets a snack of toast. / "Let's float the boat and see if it can go," said Dad. / The boat does float, and Sam claps and laughs. / They walk home, glad they came to play.

Questions: 1) What does Sam float on the pond? (literal) 2) What does Sam eat before they float the boat? (literal) 3) What do you like to do in the snow? (think)

### Track B (adult)
**A Slow Road Home**

> Kate had to walk home since the road was slow with traffic. / She put on her coat, since it was chilly, and got a snack of toast at a stand. / Another man said, "This road is always slow at this time." / Kate did not mind — she got to see the snow glow as she walked. / By the time she got home, she felt glad.

Questions: 1) Why did Kate walk instead of ride? (literal) 2) What did the man tell Kate? (literal) 3) What helps you feel calm on a slow day? (think)

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud: *"Traffic engineers study patterns in road congestion to figure out which streets need a second lane or a new signal. A small change, like a longer green light, can noticeably ease a slow commute for thousands of people."*
Tier-2 word: **noticeably** — friendly definition: "in a way you can clearly see or notice." Examples: "The room got noticeably quieter." "Her grades improved noticeably after she studied more." Your turn: "Tell me about a change you noticed clearly in your life."
Discussion: 1) How can one small change ease traffic for many people? 2) What's something that changed noticeably for you recently?

## 9. Check (2 min)
Real: boat, snow, toe, coat, road. Pseudo: proat, snoat, droe, hoat, sloat. Dictated: "We walk on the road in the snow."
**≥90%?** → L3.13. **<90%?** → Reteach the three spellings sorted by position (oa mid-word, ow end/before final consonant, oe rare/end); re-check with fresh words.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** reading "know," "own," "show" with the /ow/-as-in-cow sound instead of /ō/. Correction: "That sound comes later — for now, every ow we've met says /ō/; check: does /ō/ make it a real, known word?"
- **Common error:** using oe for common /ō/ words instead of oa/ow (over-generalizing the rare pattern). Correction: remind that oe is a short, closed list — mostly just toe, hoe, foe, doe.
- **For adults:** flag "own" and "know" explicitly as heart-word-adjacent — they follow today's rule fine for reading, but their spelling (silent k in "know," silent w in "own"... actually no silent w — clarify: "know" has a silent k, that's the notable irregularity) is worth naming.
