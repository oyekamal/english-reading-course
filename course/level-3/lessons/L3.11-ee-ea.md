# L3.11 — ee, ea

**Goal:** Read and spell the long-e vowel team spelled **ee** and **ea**.
**New today:** ee, ea (/ē/)
**Review:** ai, ay, open-syllable long e (he, we, me)
**Heart words:** laugh (review: water, people, once)
**Time:** 25–30 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Read: rain, play, train, day, stay. Heart words: water, great, busy.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say *see, tree, eat, team*. "All long e — /ē/ — spelled two ways today."

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
"**ee** is the most common spelling for /ē/ (see, tree, need, feet). **ea** also spells /ē/ in many words (eat, team, read, leaf) — but flag this: **ea** is a word you will meet again with a *different* sound (as in 'bread') later in the course — for now, in Level 3, every ea word you meet says /ē/; if you ever hit one that doesn't sound right, try the short-e sound instead and check for a real word." This is a flex-decode heads-up, not yet a full second-sound lesson.
**Urdu-speaker tip:** the long e sound itself (/ē/, as in "feet") is distinct from the short i sound (/ɪ/, as in "fit") — these can blur for an Urdu-first ear; do a short minimal-pair check: *fit/feet, sit/seat, hit/heat*.

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
**ee words:** see, tree, need, feet, green, sleep, sweet, three, queen, street
**ea words:** eat, team, read, leaf, seat, meat, clean, speak, dream, beach
**Pseudowords:** freet, blean, sneed, chead, plee

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate: 1) see 2) team 3) sleep 4) "I need to eat a snack."

## 6. Heart word: laugh (2 min)
"Laugh — the 'gh' says /f/, very unusual; whole word is a heart word." Say 3x. Use: "We laugh at a good joke."

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

### Track A (child)
**A Green Kite by the Beach**

> Sam and his team go to the beach. / When they see a green kite, they find a sweet, clean spot to sit. / "Let's eat lunch by the sea," said Sam. / They read a page and laugh at a funny bit. / A queen bee comes to see the sweet leaf, then goes home. / Sam and his team feel very happy after a fine day.

Questions: 1) What color is the kite? (literal) 2) What do Sam and his team do by the sea? (literal) 3) What do you like to do at the beach? (think)

### Track B (adult)
**A Clean Team**

> Each week, Kate and her team clean the street by the shop. / When they meet at nine, each one has a task. / Kate likes to see the street stay neat when they are done. / "We laugh a lot, but we get the job done," said Kate. / At the end, the team sits and has a sweet snack, glad for a job well done.

Questions: 1) What do Kate's team do each week? (literal) 2) What does Kate say about her team? (literal) 3) What is a task you do that feels good once it is done? (think)

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud: *"Communities that organize regular street clean-ups tend to build a stronger sense of civic pride, because neighbors who work side by side start to feel a shared responsibility for the place they live."*
Tier-2 word: **responsibility** — friendly definition: "a duty or job that is yours to take care of." Examples: "Feeding the dog is my responsibility." "Keeping the park clean is everyone's responsibility." Your turn: "Tell me about a responsibility you have."
Discussion: 1) Why might a clean-up build civic pride? 2) What responsibility do you take seriously?

## 9. Check (2 min)
Real: see, team, sleep, eat, green. Pseudo: freet, blean, sneed, chead, plee. Dictated: "I see a green tree."
**≥90%?** → L3.12. **<90%?** → Reteach ee vs ea as "two spellings, one sound (for now)" — do not yet introduce the short-e alternate sound of ea; that comes in Level 4. Re-check with a fresh word set.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** unsure whether a new word is spelled ee or ea (both sound the same right now). Correction: for reading, it does not matter which one is used — decode either the same way; for spelling, tell the learner this is one of a small set of words they will need to simply learn by sight until more spelling rules arrive later in the course.
- **Common error:** over-applying the "ea sometimes sounds different" flex note before it's needed. Correction: keep it as a one-time heads-up today, not a drill — the short-e-sound ea words are formally taught in Level 4.
- **For adults:** name explicitly that English has more than one spelling for several vowel sounds (a known, well-documented feature of the writing system) — this reduces the "am I doing something wrong" feeling for a learner used to a more one-to-one script.
