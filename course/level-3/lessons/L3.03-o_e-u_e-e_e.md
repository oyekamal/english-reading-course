# L3.03 — o_e, u_e, e_e (VCe with o, u, e)

**Goal:** Complete the VCe set — read and spell words with o_e (/ō/), u_e (/o͞o/ or /ū/), and e_e (rare, /ē/).
**New today:** o_e (home, rope), u_e — two sounds: /o͞o/ (rule, June) and /ū/ (cute, mule); e_e (these, Steve — rare, flag it)
**Review:** a_e, i_e
**Heart words:** great, eye (review: very, every)
**Time:** 30 min (slightly longer — three patterns today)
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Read: cake, time, bike, plane, smile. Heart words: very, every, once, only.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Oral pairs: *rob/robe, cut/cute, hop/hope*. Introduce a new idea: "cut→cute changes to a 'you' sound, not just a long u — listen: /kyoot/ vs /koon/ in 'June.' Both are spelled u_e, but they sound different. We'll check both today."

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
"Same silent-e rule, three more vowels. **o_e** says /ō/ (home). **u_e** can say two things — /ū/ as in 'cute' (say the letter name 'u') or /o͞o/ as in 'June' (like the oo in moon). **e_e** is rare — you'll mostly see it in a few words like 'these' and names like 'Steve.'"
**Urdu-speaker tip:** /ū/ in "cute" is a glide (y+oo) that doesn't exist as a single vowel in Urdu — model the two-part mouth shape (y-shape, then round lips).

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
**o_e real:** home, rope, hope, nose, bone, hole, joke, note, code, close, stove, drove, smoke
**u_e real (two sounds, sort them):** cute, mule, fuse, mute / rule, June, flute, tube, prune
**e_e real (rare — just these words):** these, Steve, theme
**Pseudo:** slobe, prute, glupe, chete, zope

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate: 1) home 2) cute 3) rule 4) "Steve has a cute home."

## 6. Heart words: great, eye (2 min)
"Great — looks like it should rhyme with 'meat,' but it does not; whole word is a heart word. Eye — spells a whole word with silent letters that just means the body part you see with; also a heart word." Say each 3 times. Use: "That is a great idea." "I have one eye open."

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

### Track A (child)
**Steve's New Home**

> Steve moved to a home. / It is big, and it has a gate. / Steve rode his bike home and did a jig. / He is very glad. / At home, Steve has a cute pet mule named Duke. / Duke likes a plate of grass. / "This home is very fine," said Steve, and he made a note to tell his pal.

Questions: 1) What kind of pet does Steve have? (literal) 2) How does Steve feel about his new home? (literal) 3) What would you like about a new home? (think)

### Track B (adult)
**A Very Long Shift**

> Steve got home very late from his shift. / His job is to help sick kids, and the shift ran long. / At home, he made a snack and sat next to the stove. / His wife left a note: "I made a plate for you." / Steve smiled. / It was a very long shift, but home felt like a rest at last.

Questions: 1) What is Steve's job? (literal) 2) What did his wife leave for him? (literal) 3) What do you do after a very long day? (think)

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud: *"Mules are often used on farms because they are known to be very sturdy. A mule can carry a heavy load over rough ground for a long time without tiring, which makes it a reliable partner for farm work."*
Tier-2 word: **reliable** — friendly definition: "something or someone you can count on, again and again." Examples: "A reliable car starts every time." "A reliable friend keeps their promises." Your turn: "Tell me about someone or something reliable in your life."
Discussion: 1) Why are mules useful on farms? 2) What makes a person reliable?

## 9. Check (2 min)
Real: home, cute, rule, note, June. Pseudo: slobe, prute, drine, chete, glupe. Dictated: "Steve has a cute pet."
**≥90%?** → L3.04. **<90%?** → Reteach the two u_e sounds separately (do NOT mix the drill — first master o_e alone, then split u_e into its two sound-families before re-checking).

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** guessing which u_e sound a new/pseudo word should use. Correction: this is the **first flex-decode moment** in the course — teach explicitly: "try the /ū/ sound first (say the letter name). Not a real word? Try /o͞o/. Real word now? That's it." Never let the learner guess-and-move-on without checking against a real/known word.
- **Common error:** treating e_e as a general rule ("every e_e word will be common") — it is not; flag explicitly that e_e is rare and mostly limited to a short, closed set of words.
- **For adults:** name the flex-decode routine by its purpose — "this is what a skilled reader does automatically; you're learning the conscious version first."
