# L3.04 — VCe + the Drop-e Rule

**Goal:** Apply the drop-e spelling rule when adding a vowel suffix (-ing, -ed, -er) to a VCe word: hope→hoping.
**New today:** drop-e rule (spelling rule, no new grapheme)
**Review:** a_e, i_e, o_e, u_e, e_e; -ing/-ed/-er suffixes (Level 2, no-base-change words)
**Heart words:** busy, people (review: great, eye)
**Time:** 25–30 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Read: home, cute, time, cake, note. Heart words: great, eye, very, every.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say *hope* then *hoping*. "What happened to the e when I added -ing?" (it disappeared). Contrast with a Level 2 word: *jump→jumping* (nothing dropped). "Why the difference?" Let the learner notice: VCe words drop the e before a suffix that starts with a vowel.

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
"Rule: if a word ends in silent e, and you are adding an ending that starts with a vowel (like -ing, -ed when it says /d/, or -er), **drop the e first**, then add the ending. hope → hop(e) + ing → hoping. This keeps the vowel sound long — if you kept the e, 'hopeing' would look wrong and the spelling pattern would be broken."
**Contrast rule (do NOT drop):** if the ending starts with a consonant, keep the e: hope + ful = hopeful (not taught as a suffix yet, just note the principle exists).
**Urdu-speaker tip:** this is a pure spelling convention with no sound difference to hear — it must be taught visually/explicitly, not "sounded out."

## 4. Blend it / apply it (5 min)
Model then have the learner build these: hope→hoping, ride→riding, make→making, smile→smiling, drive→driving, hide→hiding, close→closing, save→saving, joke→joking, skate→skating
Pseudowords (apply the rule to nonsense bases): grape→"is graping" (nonsense but rule-correct), zime→ziming, flobe→flobing

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate the *base word first*, then ask the learner to add -ing themselves: 1) hope → hoping 2) smile → smiling 3) "Jane is making a cake." (making = make + ing, drop-e)

## 6. Heart words: busy, people (2 min)
"Busy — spelled with a 'u' but says /ɪ/ like a short i; totally irregular, a true heart word. People — 'peo' is unusual, and the ending is a bit like a pattern you'll meet later; treat the whole word as a heart word for now." Say each 3 times. Use: "I am busy at work." "Many people live in this city."

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

### Track A (child)
**Making a Kite**

> Sam is making a kite. / He is using tape and a stick. / Every bit has to be just so. / Sam is smiling at his plan. / Once it is done, Sam is hoping for a fine time. / He is saving his kite for the big game. / "I made this alone," said Sam, very glad.

Questions: 1) What is Sam making? (literal) 2) What is Sam hoping for? (literal) 3) What have you made yourself that made you proud? (think)

### Track B (adult)
**Closing the Shop**

> At the end of every shift, Kate is closing the shop at nine. / She is checking the till and saving the cash. / Once the till is closed, she is locking every gate. / Kate is not rushing — she likes taking her time. / "I am making it safe," she said, smiling at her plan for a safe close.

Questions: 1) What is Kate's job at closing time? (literal) 2) Why does she take her time? (literal) 3) What routine do you follow at the end of your workday? (think)

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud: *"A shopkeeper's closing routine matters because a rushed close can leave a door unlocked or cash uncounted. A careful routine, done the same way every night, reduces the chance of a costly mistake."*
Tier-2 word: **routine** — friendly definition: "a set of steps you do the same way, again and again." Examples: "Brushing your teeth before bed is part of a routine." "A pilot follows a safety routine before every flight." Your turn: "Tell me about a routine you follow every day."
Discussion: 1) Why does a careful closing routine matter? 2) What's a routine you never skip?

## 9. Check (2 min)
Apply the rule live: hope→___, save→___, close→___, ride→___, make→___ (learner writes -ing form for each). Then read: hoping, saving, closing, riding, making.
**≥90% (9/10 across writing+reading)?** → L3.05. **<90%?** → Reteach with a physical letter-tile "e pops off" demo; isolate the rule from reading fluency (test the rule alone before re-checking blended reading).

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** keeping the e ("hopeing," "makeing"). Correction: "Does the ending start with a vowel? Then the e has to go — two vowels can't sit that close together in English spelling."
- **Common error:** dropping the e when it shouldn't be dropped (Level-2-only closed words like "jump" have no e to drop — watch for over-application, e.g., writing "jumping" as "jumpin" is a different, unrelated error to flag separately, not a drop-e error).
- **For adults:** frame explicitly as one of a small set of **suffix spelling rules** they will complete across Level 3–4 (drop-e here; 1-1-1 doubling and change-y-to-i come later) — naming the destination reduces "yet another random rule" fatigue.
