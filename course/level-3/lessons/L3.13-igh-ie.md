# L3.13 — igh, ie

**Goal:** Read and spell the long-i vowel team spelled **igh** and **ie**.
**New today:** igh, ie (/ī/)
**Review:** i_e (same sound, different spelling), y=/ī/ (my, fly)
**Heart words:** talk (review: walk, laugh, water)
**Time:** 25–30 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Read: boat, snow, toe, coat, road. Heart words: walk, laugh, people.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say *night, light, pie, tie*. "Long i again — /ī/ — same sound you know from i_e and from y (my, fly), two more spellings today."

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
"**igh** is a three-letter team where the g and h are silent — they just mark the i as long (night, light, high, sigh). **ie** at the **end** of a short word also says /ī/ (pie, tie, lie, die) — note this is a different job for 'ie' than the one you'll sometimes see later (as in 'field,' saying /ē/ — flag it, not taught yet)." Model both.
**Urdu-speaker tip:** three silent letters in a row (igh) doing the job of one sound can feel arbitrary — name it plainly: "gh is silent here, it's just a spelling marker, not a sound."

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
**igh words:** night, light, right, sight, high, sigh, might, tight, bright, fight
**ie words (this lesson's sound only):** pie, tie, lie, die
**Pseudowords:** stight, prigh, nie, plie, nighm

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate: 1) night 2) light 3) pie 4) "The light is bright at night."

## 6. Heart word: talk (2 min)
"Talk — the l is silent, like walk; whole word is a heart word." Say 3x. Use: "We talk on the walk home."

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

### Track A (child)
**A Bright Night**

> At night, Sam and his mom talk and gaze up at the sky. / Because the sky is bright, it glows white and high. / "Let's stay up a bit," said Mom. / They eat a slice of pie and talk of the sky. / Sam feels glad for this quiet, bright night with his mom.

Questions: 1) What do Sam and his mom look at? (literal) 2) What do they eat while they talk? (literal) 3) What do you like to do at night with your family? (think)

### Track B (adult)
**Talking It Over at Night**

> After a long shift, Kate and her friend Jane talk it over at night. / When they sit and gaze up, the sky is bright. / "I might take the job at another site," said Kate. / Jane said, "That is a big thing — talk to your team, then decide." / They eat a slice of pie and talk until it gets late.

Questions: 1) What choice is Kate talking about? (literal) 2) What does Jane tell Kate to do first? (literal) 3) Who do you talk to when you have a big choice to make? (think)

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud: *"Talking through a big decision with someone you trust often reveals a perspective you had not considered on your own. A friend can point out a risk or an option you were too close to the problem to notice."*
Tier-2 word: **perspective** — friendly definition: "a way of looking at or thinking about something." Examples: "From my perspective, the plan seems risky." "Getting a friend's perspective helped her decide." Your turn: "Tell me about a time someone's perspective changed your mind."
Discussion: 1) Why can a friend see something you can't see about your own choice? 2) Who gives you a helpful perspective when you're deciding something?

## 9. Check (2 min)
Real: night, light, pie, high, bright. Pseudo: stight, prigh, nie, plie, nighm. Dictated: "The light is bright at night."
**≥90%?** → L3.14. **<90%?** → Reteach igh as "three silent-ish letters, one long-i sound" using letter tiles to physically group i-g-h as one chunk; re-check with fresh words.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** trying to sound out g and h separately in igh ("nig-huh-t"). Correction: teach igh as one chunk to blend, not three separate sounds — "i, g, h together just say /ī/."
- **Common error:** reading ie words with the wrong sound once the /ē/ version (field, later level) is encountered outside class. Correction: for now, flag that ie has two jobs, but only the /ī/-at-the-end-of-a-word job is taught yet.
- **For adults:** point out igh mostly survives in a small, closed set of common words (night, light, right, sight, high, might, tight, bright, fight, sigh) — worth learning as a set rather than expecting to meet many new igh words later.
