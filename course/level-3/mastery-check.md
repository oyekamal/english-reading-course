## 5. Connected-text reading (WCPM + prosody + comprehension)

Use the phrase-cued version below (marked with `/`) for echo reading first, then have the learner read the un-marked version independently while you time it and note errors for WCPM, then ask the 3 comprehension questions.

### Track A (child) — "A Great Game Day" (~262 words)

Once, Kate and her whole family went to a big game day. / The sky was bright and still. / Kate's dad said, "Let's ride the big wheel." / Kate felt very glad, but a bit shy. / At the top, she could see the whole place — a huge lake, a green patch of grass, and a road that led home.

Next, they got in line for a game. / A kid named Mike was smiling and holding a huge, gold prize — a cute stuffed mule. / "How nice!" said Kate. / She tried the game too, took aim, threw well, and won a prize — a fine kite with a blue tail.

By noon, the family was hungry. / They got a plate of rice and a slice of pie. / Kate's baby was busy eating a sweet treat, getting sticky hands. / "This food is so good," said Mom. / They sat and ate by a tree, glad for the rest.

Then, they went to see a robot show. / A huge robot could talk and even sing a tune. / It moved on wheels, slow and easy, and gave a wave to everyone watching. / Kate's dad said, "That is one bold robot!" / Everyone laughed.

As the day grew late, the sky went gold, then a deep, cool blue. / Kate felt a bit sleepy but very happy. / "This was the best day," she said, holding her new kite close. / Once home, she fell asleep fast, smiling, glad for a whole day of fun.

**Comprehension (literal):** 1) What did Kate win at the game? 2) What did the family eat at noon? 3) What did the robot do at the show?
**Flex-decode narration prompt:** "When you got to the word 'moon' or 'book' earlier this level, what did you do to figure out which oo sound to use? Tell me the steps."

### Track B (adult) — "A Great Day Off" (~290 words)

Once, Kate had a whole day off, and she and her team made a plan for fun. / The sky was bright and still. / "Let's start with a big hike," said Jake, one of her team. / Kate felt very glad for the plan.

At the top of the hill, she could see the whole place — a huge lake, a green patch of grass, and a road that led back to the shop.

Next, they went to a game night at a friend's home. / A man named Mike was smiling and holding a huge, gold prize he had won — a set of blue mugs. / "How nice!" said Kate. / She tried her luck too, took aim, threw well, and won a prize — a cute stuffed mule for a friend.

By noon, the team was hungry. / They got a plate of rice and a slice of pie at a shop close by. / Kate's friend was busy eating a sweet treat, getting sticky hands. / "This food is so good," said Jake. / They sat and ate by a tree, glad for the rest.

Then, they went to see a robot show close to the shop. / A huge robot could talk and even sing a tune. / It moved on wheels, slow and easy, and gave a wave to everyone watching. / "That is one bold robot," said Kate. / Everyone laughed.

As the day grew late, the sky went gold, then a deep, cool blue. / Kate felt a bit sleepy but very happy. / "This was a great day off," she said, holding her prize close. / Once home, she fell asleep fast, smiling, glad for a whole day of fun.

**Comprehension (literal):** 1) What did Kate's team decide to do first? 2) What did Kate win at game night? 3) Where did the team eat at noon?
**Flex-decode narration prompt:** same as Track A above.

## 6. Scoring & decision rule

| Component | Pass threshold |
|---|---|
| Heart words | ≥14/16 instant recognition |
| Real words | ≥90% (25/28 if using the full list, or ≥18/20 on a 20-item subset) |
| Pseudowords | ≥9/10 |
| Dictation | ≥7/8 items correct (word-level; minor letter-reversal noted separately, not auto-failed) |
| Connected text | learner can read it with phrasing that roughly follows the `/` marks (not word-by-word monotone); WCPM recorded as the learner's own Level 3 baseline, no external norm comparison (see Level 3 README) |
| Comprehension | 3/3 literal questions answered correctly |
| Flex-decode narration | can describe "try one sound, check if it's real, try the other if not" in their own words, unprompted |

**All components at or above threshold → advance to Level 4 (The Full Code).**
**One or two components below threshold, concentrated in a specific family (e.g., real-word misses were all -ild/-ind/-old/-ost) → return to that specific lesson only, reteach, re-check only that family, then re-run the full instrument once more.**
**Misses spread across many families, or comprehension/flex-narration both fail while decoding passes → return to L3.17 (Review) for a full pass before re-attempting the complete instrument. Never advance on decoding success alone if comprehension failed — record both scores separately, per DESIGN.md Rule 8.**
