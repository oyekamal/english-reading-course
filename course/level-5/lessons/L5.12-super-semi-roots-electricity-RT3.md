# L5.12 — Prefixes super-, semi- · Roots spect, port, dict, struct · Electricity, Simply Explained · Readers Theatre #3

**Goal:** Read, spell, and use words with *super-* and *semi-*; learn the first four
"commonly useful" roots (labelled that way deliberately — not research-ranked the
way the prefix list is, research/05 §5f); run this level's third and final Readers
Theatre script.
**New today:** *super-* ("above" / "beyond" / "very") and *semi-* ("half" /
"partly"); roots *spect* (see), *port* (carry), *dict* (say), *struct* (build).
Tier-2 words: *efficient*, *consume*.
**Review:** number roots — quick oral round (*bi-, tri-, oct-*).
**Time:** 30–45 min — content-dense, fine to run slightly long. **Materials:**
paper, pencil, timer; script below.

---

## 1. Retrieval warm-up (3–5 min)

Read: *happiness, agreement, movement*. Heart words (Fry 171–180): **different, home,
us, move, try, kind, hand, picture, again, change**. *Move* has a heart part (the *o*
says /oo/, not the usual VCe long-*o* sound — compare *stove*, where it does follow
the pattern). The rest are fully decodable.

**Timed affix round (30 sec):** name as many of *-ness, -ment* (last session) as
you can, meaning or one example word each.

## 2. Word work (10–12 min)

### super- ("above" / "beyond" / "very")

| Word | Meaning check |
|---|---|
| superhero | a hero with beyond-normal abilities |
| supermarket | a very large market |
| superpower | a power beyond the normal |
| superhuman | beyond normal human ability |
| supervise | to watch over/oversee |
| supersonic | beyond/faster than the speed of sound |

Dictate 3: *superhero, supervise, supersonic*.

### semi- ("half" / "partly")

| Word | Meaning check |
|---|---|
| semicircle | half a circle |
| semifinal | the round before the final (halfway to the final) |
| semiannual | happening every half-year (twice a year) |
| semiconscious | only half/partly conscious |
| semisweet | only partly sweet |
| semiformal | partly formal (dressier than casual, less than formal) |

Dictate 3: *semicircle, semifinal, semiannual*.

Meaning-check sentence: "The stadium was full for the ______ (semifinal/supermarket)
match, with the winner going on to play in the final." → *semifinal* fits.

### Roots — spect, port, dict, struct ("commonly useful," not research-ranked)

Say: "Unlike the prefix list, this root list isn't from a single study that counted
and ranked them — it's a widely used, practical teaching set (research/05 §5f). Say
it that way if asked: 'these are roots that show up a lot, by common teaching
practice,' not 'science proved these are the best 20.'"

| Root | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| spect | to see/look | in**spect** (look into/closely), re**spect** (look back at, hold in high regard), **spect**ator (someone who watches), **spect**acle (something worth looking at) |
| port | to carry | trans**port** (carry across), ex**port** (carry out, to another country), im**port** (carry in), **port**able (able to be carried), re**port** (carry back information) |
| dict | to say | pre**dict** (say beforehand), **dict**ionary (a book that "says" what words mean), **dict**ate (to say aloud for someone to write), ver**dict** (a "true saying" — a judge's/jury's decision) |
| struct | to build | con**struct** (build together), **struct**ure (a built thing, or the way something is built), in**struct** (to build knowledge in someone — teach), de**struct**ion (the undoing/opposite of building) |

For each root: read 2–3 example words, peel the root out each time, and ask "does
the root's meaning show up in what the whole word means?" (a genuine, checkable
question — not a guess).

**Self-correction drill (1 min):** say "please *in-SPECT-uh* it" (extra syllable
added) inside a sentence and have the learner correct it to *inspect*, using
context. Repeat with one more root word.

**Word harvest:** find one more *super-/semi-* or root word inside today's
script — *inspect, respect, construct, superhero* all appear; see how many you
can spot.

### Tier-2 vocabulary

**efficient** — friendly definition: "doing something well without wasting time,
effort, or energy." Examples: "LED bulbs are far more efficient than old-style
bulbs — they produce the same light using much less electricity." / "An efficient
morning routine gets you out the door without rushing." Generative use: "Name one
efficient habit or tool you use."

**consume** — friendly definition: "to use up." Examples: "A large appliance left
running all day consumes a lot of electricity." / "Reading consumes time, but it's
time well spent." Generative use: "Name one thing in your home that consumes a
noticeable amount of electricity."

## 3. Prime the topic (3–5 min)

Before reading: if possible, show or describe a simple circuit (a battery, wire,
and small bulb), or a household electricity bill. Give 2–3 quick facts: electricity
flows in a complete loop, called a circuit; if the loop breaks, the flow stops;
some electricity turns into heat as it moves, which is wasted energy. Orienting
question: "Have you ever wondered why an electricity bill is higher some months
than others?"

## 4. Fluency: Readers Theatre #3 — "The Circuit"

Reminder: Model read (all parts) → Rehearsal read (own parts) → Performance read
(timed, scored).

### Track A script — for a child cast (2–3 readers)

```
CAST: NARRATOR, HANIA (age 9), MR. KHAN (science fair judge)

NARRATOR: Hania stood proudly next to her science fair project. It was a
          small circuit with a battery, some wires, and a tiny light
          bulb.

KHAN: Can you inspect this circuit for me? Can you tell me how it works?

HANIA: Sure! The battery is like the "push." It pushes electricity
       through the wire. The wire is a path, like a road. It carries the
       electricity all the way around, in a loop. That is why it is
       called a circuit. "Circ" means circle. A circuit is a loop that
       goes around.

KHAN: And what happens when I break the loop, like this? (pretends to
      disconnect a wire)

HANIA: The light goes out! The electricity cannot move across a broken
       path. It needs the whole loop to work. It needs to be built as
       one complete shape.

KHAN: I respect that explanation. Very clear. What would happen with a
      superhero-strength battery instead of this small one?

HANIA: (laughing) It would probably burn out the little light bulb!
       Bigger isn't always better. This bulb is built to work with this
       exact amount of electricity. Not more.

KHAN: One more question. Why does the wire feel a little warm?

HANIA: Some electricity turns into heat as it moves through the wire.
       That is actually wasted energy. A more efficient circuit loses
       less heat. Real engineers try to fix that.

KHAN: Excellent. I'll happily construct a very good grade for this project.
```

(≈223 words. **FK grade: 4.1 · Flesch reading ease: 79.6**.)

**Comprehension check:** (1) What does Hania say the battery does? (2) What happens
when the wire's loop is broken? (3) Why does the wire feel warm, according to Hania?

### Track B script — for a teen/adult cast (2–3 readers)

```
CAST: NARRATOR, HOMEOWNER (Mrs. Baig), ELECTRICIAN (Imran)

NARRATOR: A routine home electrical inspection.

BAIG: Our electricity bill has gone up a lot this year. Can you inspect
      the wiring and tell me if something's wrong?

IMRAN: I'll take a look. First question, though. Has anything new been
       installed? A new appliance, an air conditioner?

BAIG: We added a second air conditioner about six months ago, for the
      hot season.

IMRAN: That would explain a lot. Older AC units use a lot more
       electricity than newer ones do, to cool the same amount of air.
       The newer models are built differently inside, and it makes a
       real difference.

BAIG: So the wiring itself isn't the problem?

IMRAN: I still want to inspect it properly before I say that for sure.
       Faulty wiring is a real safety issue, not just a cost issue. But
       my first guess, based on what I'm seeing, is that the higher
       bill matches the new unit. Not a fault in your wiring.

BAIG: Is there anything I can do to make things more efficient, without
      buying a whole new unit?

IMRAN: A few things. Clean the filters often. Seal any gaps around
       doors and windows, so cool air doesn't leak out. And set it to
       a semi-cool setting, instead of the coldest setting all day.
       None of that needs any construction work. Just simple habits.

BAIG: That's a relief. I half-expected you to report some expensive
      structural problem.

IMRAN: Not today. I'll still finish a full inspection, to be sure. But
       so far, this looks like an efficiency question, not a safety
       one.
```

(≈243 words. **FK grade: 4.8 · Flesch reading ease: 77.2**.)

**Comprehension check:** (1) What does Imran say might explain the higher bill? (2)
What three habits does he suggest to make the AC more efficient? (3) What is
Imran's "first guess," and what does he say he'll still do before being certain?

### Discussion — reciprocal roles

**Predictor**: what do you expect "The Circuit" to be about? **Questioner**: one
answered, one unanswered question (neither script explains exactly how an AC unit's
inside parts work). **Clarifier**: explain *circuit*, *faulty*, or *efficiency*
using context or the roots just taught. **Summarizer**: retell either script in 2–3
sentences.

## 5. Write to read (5 min)

way you could make it more efficient."

## Tutor notes

- Common error: treating a root as if it must always sit at the very front of a word
  (missing it in *inspect, respect*). Correction: point out roots can sit anywhere —
  peel prefixes and suffixes from both ends first, and whatever's left in the middle
  is usually the root.
- This is the level's third and last Readers Theatre script — from 5.13 onward,
  fluency returns to the standard Model→Assisted→Independent single-passage format.
- Both scripts model a genuinely useful adult habit: asking "what's your evidence for
  that conclusion, and what are you still checking?" — worth naming explicitly if a
  learner notices Mr. Khan and Imran both do this.
