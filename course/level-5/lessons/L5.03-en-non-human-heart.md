# L5.03 — Prefixes en-/em-, non- · The Human Heart (structure)

**Goal:** Read, spell, and use words with *en-/em-* and *non-*; keep building the
fluency program; continue heart-word coverage.
**New today:** *en-/em-* ("to cause / to put into a state") and *non-* ("not / the
absence of"). Tier-2 words: *vital*, *circulate*.
**Review:** the "not" family from 5.02 — *incorrect, disagree*.
**Time:** 30–45 min. **Materials:** paper, pencil, timer.

---

## 1. Retrieval warm-up (3–5 min)

Read: *impossible, irregular, dishonest*. Heart words (Fry 107–109): **know, place,
year**. *Place* and *year* are fully decodable (silent-*e*, *ea* vowel team). *Know*
has a real heart part: silent *k* before *n* — mark just the *k*, decode *-now*
normally (same silent-*k* pattern as *knee, knife* from earlier levels).

**Timed affix round (30 sec):** name as many of *in-/im-/ir-/il-, dis-* (last
session) as you can, meaning or one example word each.

## 2. Word work (10–12 min)

### en-/em- ("to cause / to put into a state")

Say: "This prefix means 'to cause' or 'to put into.' *Able* → *enable* means 'to
cause to be able.' It spells *em-* before *b* and *p*, same reasoning as *im-* last
session."

| Word | Meaning check | Spelling note |
|---|---|---|
| enable | to make able | en + able |
| enjoy | to take pleasure in | en (historical, treat as one unit) + joy |
| enlarge | to make larger | en + large |
| encourage | to give courage to | en + courage |
| endanger | to put in danger | en + danger |
| enrich | to make richer | en + rich |
| embrace | to hold close / accept fully | em + brace (before *b*) |
| empower | to give power to | em + power (before *p*) |

Dictate 3: *enable, enlarge, embrace*.

### non- ("not" / "the absence of")

| Word | Meaning check | Spelling note |
|---|---|---|
| nonfiction | writing that is not fiction (true) | non + fiction |
| nonstop | without stopping | non + stop |
| nonsense | words with no sense/meaning | non + sense |
| nonprofit | an organization not run for profit | non + profit |
| nonliving | not alive | non + living |
| nonviolent | without violence | non + violent |
| nonstick | a surface things don't stick to | non + stick |
| nonrefundable | money that can't be given back | non + refundable |

Dictate 3: *nonstop, nonliving, nonsense*.

Meaning-check sentence: "A rock is ______ (nonliving/nonstop) — it doesn't eat,
grow, or breathe." → *nonliving* fits.

**Self-correction drill (1 min):** say "she felt very *EN-cour-age*" (stress
shifted) inside a sentence and have the learner correct it to *encourage*, using
context, not just re-reading the letters. Repeat with one *non-* word.

**Word harvest:** find one more *en-/em-* or *non-* word inside today's passage —
*nonstop* appears in Track A's last line; see if you can spot it.

### Tier-2 vocabulary

**vital** — friendly definition: "so important that without it, things can't work or
survive." Examples: "Water is vital for every living thing." / "Getting enough sleep
is vital for your brain to work well." Generative use: "Name one thing that is vital
to you, and explain why."

**circulate** — friendly definition: "to move around continuously, through a system,
and come back to where it started." Examples: "Blood circulates through your body,
carrying oxygen to every part." / "Rumors can circulate around a school in a single
day." Generative use: "Name one thing (besides blood) that circulates — money,
water, air in a room — and describe its loop."

## 3. Prime the topic (3–5 min)

Before reading: put a hand on your chest — can you feel your own heartbeat? Give
2–3 quick facts: the heart is a pump; it never stops your whole life; doctors and
nurses check it constantly using nothing but two fingers and a watch. Orienting
question: "Have you ever felt your own pulse before? Where did you check it?"

## 4. Fluency & Knowledge Text (10–12 min) — phrase-cued (phrase marks continue
through 5.06, then fade — see README §4)

Model → echo → choral → **Perform it** (1–2 min: read the whole passage once more
aloud to someone — a family member, the tutor, or a voice note to a friend — as a
small performance; aim for phrasing that follows the punctuation) → independent
(timed, a second and unannounced read — this is the one that gets scored). WCPM +
prosody check after.

### Track A — The Human Heart (for a child)

> Put your hand / on your chest. / Can you feel it? / Thump, thump, thump. / That
> is your heart. / It never stops working. / Not even for one second, / your
> whole life. / Your heart is about the size / of your closed fist. / Its job /
> is to be a pump. / It pushes blood / all around your body. / It uses long,
> thin tubes / called blood vessels.
>
> Inside your heart / there are four rooms. / We call them chambers. / Blood
> comes in / from your body. / It is low on oxygen. / The heart sends it / to
> your lungs. / There it picks up / fresh oxygen. / Then the blood / comes back
> to the heart. / The heart pushes it out again. / It goes to every part of
> you. / It goes to your brain, / your fingers, / and your toes. / It carries
> fresh oxygen / to keep every part of you / working. / Blood goes around /
> this whole loop / about once every minute. / That is why oxygen / is so
> vital. / Without it, / your body's parts / cannot do their jobs. / A doctor
> listening / to your heartbeat / is really listening / to that pump. / It
> works over and over, / to keep you alive.

(≈185 words. **FK grade: 2.1 · Flesch reading ease: 92.6**.)

**Comprehension check:** (1) How many chambers does the heart have? (2) Why does
blood go to the lungs before going to the rest of the body? (3) Why does the text
say oxygen is "vital"?

### Track B — The Human Heart (for a teen/adult, checking your own pulse)

> Anyone can check / how hard their heart is working. / You only need / two
> fingers / and a watch.
>
> Press your first two fingers / gently on the inside of your wrist, / just below
> your thumb. / Or press them / against the side of your neck. / You will feel /
> a steady beat. / This is your pulse. / Each beat / is one push of your heart, /
> sending blood / out into your body.
>
> A resting adult heart / usually beats / sixty to a hundred times / a minute. /
> A lower number, / within reason, / is often a sign / of a fit heart. / Regular
> exercise / trains the heart muscle. / A trained heart / can pump more blood /
> with each beat. / So it does not need / to beat as often / to circulate / the
> same amount of blood.
>
> A higher number / can mean stress, / illness, / too much caffeine, / or simply
> climbing a flight of stairs. / The number alone / does not tell / the whole
> story.
>
> Doctors and nurses / check pulse all the time. / It is one of the fastest, /
> cheapest ways / to learn something important / about a person's health, /
> without any equipment / beyond a watch.
>
> Knowing your own / normal resting pulse / is useful. / Check it while you are
> resting, / not right after activity. / This gives you a baseline. / If your
> pulse ever feels / clearly different from normal, / especially if it feels
> uneven / rather than just fast or slow, / it is worth mentioning / to a
> doctor. / Do not just ignore it.

(≈235 words. **FK grade: 4.7 · Flesch reading ease: 81.0**.)

**Comprehension check:** (1) Where can you check your pulse? (2) Why might a
trained heart beat slower at rest? (3) What does the text say is worth mentioning
to a doctor?

### Discussion — reciprocal roles

**Predictor**: "What do you expect to learn about the heart from this text?"
**Questioner**: one question the text answers, one it doesn't (e.g., it doesn't say
what happens if the heart's pump fails).
**Clarifier**: explain *chambers*, *pulse*, or *vital signs* using context or
morphology (*vital* relates to *vita* = "life" — a small preview of the roots coming
in later sessions).
**Summarizer**: retell in 2–3 sentences.

## Tutor notes

- Common error: confusing *en-/em-* (cause/put into) with *in-/im-* (not) from last
  session, since both spell *em-* before *b/p*. Correction: check meaning, not just
  spelling — "does adding this prefix make the base *not true*, or make something
  *happen*?" *Impossible* = not possible (negation); *Empower* = cause power to happen
  (causative). Same spelling rule, opposite job.
- Adult learners: this session's health framing (pulse-checking) is a genuine,
  practical adult-life skill, not a dressed-up kids' topic — say so if a learner
  seems to expect "childish" content at this level.
- Urdu-speaker note: none specific to this session's target sounds; all patterns were
  covered in earlier levels.
