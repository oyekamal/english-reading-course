# L5.08 — Prefixes inter-, fore- · The Human Heart, Deeper (heartbeat & health) · Readers Theatre #2

**Goal:** Read, spell, and use words with *inter-* and *fore-*; run this level's
second Readers Theatre script.
**New today:** *inter-* ("between" / "among") and *fore-* ("before" / "front, ahead
of"). Tier-2 words: *essential*, *moderate*.
**Review:** *-tion/-sion, -able/-ible* — *action, comfortable*.
**Time:** 30–45 min. **Materials:** paper, pencil, timer; script below.

---

## 1. Retrieval warm-up (3–5 min)

Read: *invention, reliable, discussion*. Heart words (Fry 131–140): **before, line,
right, too, mean, old, any, same, tell, boy**. Of these, *any* has a real heart part
(the *a* says short-*e*, not its usual sound) — mark that letter. The rest are fully
decodable (*before, line, right, too, mean, old, same, tell, boy* — vowel teams,
*igh*, VCe, and the *-old* family already taught).

**Readers Theatre reminder (short form from here on):** Model read (tutor/most
confident reader, all parts) → Rehearsal read (own parts, fix hard words) →
Performance read (timed, scored for WCPM + prosody).

**Timed affix round (30 sec):** name as many of *-tion/-sion, -able/-ible* (last
session) as you can, meaning or one example word each.

## 2. Word work (10–12 min)

### inter- ("between" / "among")

| Word | Meaning check | Spelling note |
|---|---|---|
| international | between/among nations | inter + national |
| interact | to act between/among people | inter + act |
| interrupt | to break in between (someone speaking) | inter + rupt (root preview: *rupt* = break) |
| internet | a network between/among computers | inter + net |
| interview | a formal meeting/questioning between people | inter + view |
| intersection | where two roads cross between each other | inter + section |
| interconnect | to connect between/among things | inter + connect |
| intermediate | in between two levels (beginner and advanced) | inter + mediate |

Dictate 3: *interact, interrupt, interview*.

### fore- ("before" / "the front of")

| Word | Meaning check | Spelling note |
|---|---|---|
| forecast | a statement made before(hand) about what will happen | fore + cast |
| foresee | to see something before it happens | fore + see |
| forehead | the front part of the head | fore + head |
| foreground | the front part of a picture/scene | fore + ground |
| foretell | to tell something before it happens | fore + tell |
| forewarn | to warn someone before(hand) | fore + warn |
| forefront | the very front/most important position | fore + front |
| foreman | a person in front, leading a team of workers | fore + man |

Dictate 3: *forecast, foresee, forewarn*. Point out that *fore-* and *for-* sound
identical but mean different things (*forgive* is not "fore + give") — spelling,
not sound, tells you the prefix is there.

Meaning-check sentence: "A doctor can't always ______ (foresee/forecast) exactly
when someone will get sick, but regular checkups help catch problems early." →
either *foresee* or *forecast* works reasonably; discuss the slight difference
(*forecast* leans toward a stated prediction; *foresee* leans toward simply
anticipating).

**Self-correction drill (1 min):** say "the two teams will *in-ter-ACT*" (stress
shifted) inside a sentence and have the learner correct it to *interact*, using
context. Repeat with one *fore-* word.

**Word harvest:** find one more *inter-* or *fore-* word inside today's script —
*interrupt* and *forecast* both appear in both tracks; see if you can spot each one.

### Tier-2 vocabulary

**essential** — friendly definition: "so necessary that you can't really do without
it." Examples: "Regular movement is essential for keeping your heart healthy." /
"Sleep is essential — nobody can skip it forever without consequences."
Generative use: "Name one thing that's essential to your daily routine."

**moderate** — friendly definition: "a reasonable amount — not too much, not too
little." Examples: "Doctors usually recommend moderate exercise, not extreme,
exhausting workouts, for everyday heart health." / "A moderate amount of salt is
fine; a very high amount isn't." Generative use: "Name one activity where a
moderate amount is good, but too much or too little would be a problem."

## 3. Prime the topic (3–5 min)

Before reading: ask "have you ever felt your heart beating fast after exercise? What
happened to it afterward?" Give 2–3 quick facts: a fit heart calms back down faster
after exercise; regular, moderate activity matters more for heart health than rare,
intense bursts; doctors check the same few numbers (heart rate, blood pressure)
every year to track changes over time. Orienting question: "What's one health habit
you already do fairly regularly?"

## 4. Fluency: Readers Theatre #2 — "Check-Up"

### Track A script — for a child cast (2–3 readers)

```
CAST: NARRATOR, BILAL (age 11), COACH SANA

NARRATOR: Bilal was out of breath after just one lap around the field.

BILAL: (panting) Coach, is it bad that my heart is beating so fast?

SANA: Not at all — that's exactly what your heart is supposed to do when you
      run. It's working harder, pumping more blood, so your muscles get more
      oxygen. That fast heartbeat is your heart doing its job well.

BILAL: But it feels like it's about to interrupt itself or something!

SANA: (laughing) It won't. A healthy heart can speed up a lot during
      exercise and then calm back down afterward. That "calming back down"
      part matters too — it tells us something.

BILAL: What does it tell us?

SANA: The faster your heart rate comes back down to normal after exercise,
      the fitter your heart usually is. It's like your heart's own
      forecast for how strong it's getting.

BILAL: So I should run every day, as hard as I can, forever?

SANA: Not extreme — moderate. Regular movement, most days, is essential.
      But your body also needs rest to recover. Exercise, and rest, work
      together — you can't foresee good fitness from just one without the
      other.

BILAL: I didn't know a heartbeat could tell you that much!

SANA: A heartbeat is one of the best, simplest clues your body gives you
      about itself. Ready for one more lap — moderate pace this time?

BILAL: Ready.
```

(≈219 words. **FK grade: 4.7 · Flesch reading ease: 81.2**.)

**Comprehension check:** (1) Why does Bilal's heart beat fast when he runs? (2) What
does Coach Sana say tells you your heart is getting fitter? (3) What two things does
she say work together for a healthy heart?

### Track B script — for a teen/adult cast (2–3 readers)

```
CAST: NARRATOR, PATIENT (Mr. Rizvi), DR. FATIMA

NARRATOR: An annual check-up, at a small clinic.

FATIMA: Your blood pressure and resting heart rate both look good this
        year, Mr. Rizvi. Whatever you're doing, keep doing it.

RIZVI: Honestly, I started walking most mornings. About thirty
       minutes. Nothing extreme. I didn't want to interrupt my whole
       routine.

FATIMA: That's exactly the right kind of activity. Moderate, but done
        often. That makes the biggest difference for most people. More
        than any hard, short-term push. Doing it often matters more
        than doing it hard, for heart health.

RIZVI: Can you forecast anything from these numbers? Is there anything
       I should worry about?

FATIMA: I can't foretell the future from just one check-up. I wouldn't
        pretend to. But these numbers, tracked over time, tell us far
        more than any single visit. That's why we check every year,
        not just once.

RIZVI: Is there anything I should watch for between now and next year?

FATIMA: A few things. Chest discomfort that interrupts normal
        activity. Unusual shortness of breath. Or a heartbeat that
        feels uneven, not just fast. Any of those, don't wait for
        your next visit. Come in right away. Otherwise, keep the
        walks, keep it moderate, and I will see you in a year.

RIZVI: That's a relief. I half-expected a lecture.

FATIMA: The lecture is only for people who aren't already doing the
        essential thing. You are. Keep at it.
```

(≈213 words. **FK grade: 4.6 · Flesch reading ease: 75.6**.)

**Comprehension check:** (1) What did Mr. Rizvi start doing that improved his
numbers? (2) Why does Dr. Fatima say numbers "tracked over time" tell us more than
one visit? (3) What three warning signs does she say he shouldn't wait to report?

### Discussion — reciprocal roles

**Predictor**: what do you expect "Check-Up" to be about, from the title?
**Questioner**: one answered, one unanswered question (neither script gives exact
numbers for a "good" resting heart rate). **Clarifier**: explain *routine*,
*uneven*, or *doing it often matters more than doing it hard* using context.
**Summarizer**: retell either script in 2–3 sentences.

## Tutor notes

- Common error: confusing *fore-* with *for-* (as in *forgive, forget*) since they
  sound identical. Correction: check the base word — *fore-* attaches to a
  standalone base that keeps its meaning (*forecast* = fore + cast); *for-* in
  *forgive/forget* doesn't split into a meaningful base this way. This is a spelling
  distinction to note, not a rule to over-teach at this level.
- This session quietly previews *rupt* (in *interrupt*) — full root treatment in
  5.13.
- Adult learners: Dr. Fatima's specific warning-sign list is accurate general health
  information, not invented — but this is a reading lesson, not medical advice; if a
  learner raises a real health concern, the tutor's job is to encourage seeing an
  actual doctor, not to diagnose from the script.
