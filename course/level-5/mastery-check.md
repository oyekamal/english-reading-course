# Level 5 Mastery Check — Word Power & Fluency

Run this after L5.16. It has five parts. Passing criteria are per-part below; overall,
the learner needs to pass **4 of 5 parts** to move to Level 6. If 2 or fewer parts
pass, don't push forward — go back to whichever of L5.01–L5.16 covers the weakest
area and re-run its Word Work and Check sections before retesting.

This is a **skill demonstration, not a timed exam** — there's no penalty for pausing,
and it can be split across two sittings (Part 1–3 in one, Part 4–5 in another) if
that suits the learner better (DESIGN.md rule 10, pause-safe).

---

## Part 1 — Morphology: read and spell (10 min)

**Read aloud** (real words, decode by peeling affixes/roots first): *unbreakable,
disagreement, international, transportation, forewarning, superhuman, semicircular,
underestimate, biography, predictable, construction, invisible, curiosity,
generosity, thermometer, credible*.

**Read aloud — pseudowords** (made-up words using real morphemes, to confirm the
*rule* is understood, not just memorized whole words): *unspectable, reflatify,
midstruction, disgraphic, transrupt*. Say plainly: "these are made-up words — I want
to see if you can sound them out and guess what parts you recognize, not that
you've memorized them."

**Spell from dictation:** *unhappy, dishonest, forecast, subtract, activity,
government, careless, addition*.

**Pass:** ≥12/16 real words read correctly (peeling the right morpheme, not just
guessing the whole word) + ≥3/5 pseudowords decoded (any plausible pronunciation
built from real morpheme sounds counts, even if it "sounds silly") + ≥6/8 words
spelled correctly.

## Part 2 — Vocabulary: define in your own words (5 min)

Pick 6 of the level's 30 Tier-2 words at random (README §6, full list of 30 in
L5.16 §2): e.g. *gradually, valuable, vital, ancient, predict, transform, interest,
essential, resource, intense, enormous, efficient, reduce, network, route, exotic*
(or any others taught).

**Pass:** ≥5/6 defined correctly in the learner's own words (a friendly definition,
not a memorized dictionary phrase) — accept any answer that captures the real
meaning, even in different words than the lesson used.

## Part 3 — Fry heart-word spot-check (5 min)

Read a random sample of 15 words spanning the full Fry 1–300 range (not just the
Level 5 portion) — mix in a few from Levels 1–3's lists too, to confirm long-term
retention, not just this level's recency:

*was, because, there, only, know, great, want, does, move, should, thought, don't,
enough, once, afternoon*

**Pass:** ≥13/15 read correctly. For any miss, check: is it a decoding miss (didn't
apply the phonics code) or a heart-part miss (forgot the specific irregular piece)?
The correction differs — a decoding miss needs a segment-and-blend reteach; a
heart-part miss just needs the irregular piece re-flagged and reviewed again next
session.

## Part 4 — Fluency: cold read, unseen passage, WCPM + prosody (10 min)

Use the passage below — the learner has **not** seen this text in any L5 lesson.
Run the full Model→Assisted→Independent sequence is **not** used here (that would
defeat the point of a cold-read check) — instead: give 10 seconds to silently
preview the title only, then read aloud once, timed, no rehearsal. Score WCPM and
prosody per README §4's rubric immediately after.

### Track A — A Morning at the Market (for a child)

Rafey went to the market with his grandfather early on a Saturday morning. It was
before the day got too hot. The market was already busy. Vendors called out
prices. The smell of fresh bread drifted from a nearby stall. Baskets overflowed
with colorful vegetables.

"How do you decide who to buy from?" Rafey asked. He watched his grandfather walk
past several stalls without stopping.

"I look for a few things," his grandfather explained. "Is the fruit fresh, not
bruised? Is the seller honest about the price? Or does it change depending on who
is asking? And is it close by, so I am not walking far for something I could get
right here?"

They stopped at a stall with neat rows of oranges. The seller greeted Rafey's
grandfather by name. They had clearly done business many times before.

"Trust matters too," his grandfather added quietly, once they had walked on with a
full bag. "A seller who is honest with you today is usually worth coming back to
tomorrow. That is not something you can check just by looking at the fruit."

Rafey thought about that the whole walk home. So much of buying and selling
depended on things you could not see at all. It came down to remembering who had
been fair with you before.

(≈218 words. **FK grade: 4.2 · Flesch reading ease: 82.7**.)

### Track B — Negotiating a Fair Price (for a teen/adult)

Most people think you either have a talent for negotiating, or you don't. That's
not true. Negotiating is a skill you can learn. It starts with one idea. Know your
walk-away point before the talk even begins.

A walk-away point is the most you will pay. Or it is the least you will accept.
Decide this number in advance. Base it on research, like what similar items
actually sold for. Do not just decide in the moment, based on how you feel.
Without a walk-away point, it is easy to get stuck on whatever number the other
person says first. That first number might have nothing to do with a fair price.

Here is a second idea, and it is less obvious. Silence is a tool. It is not an
awkward gap you need to fill. After you state your offer, do not rush to explain
it further. Do not fill the quiet moment with more talking. Many people give away
value simply because silence felt uncomfortable. Not because the other side
actually pushed back.

Last, separate the person from the problem. Think of a negotiation as two
reasonable people with different needs. Do not think of it as a personal fight.
This keeps the talk useful, even when the numbers are still far apart. Often,
this one habit is the difference between reaching a fair deal and walking away
over nothing more than hurt pride.

(≈236 words. **FK grade: 5.2 · Flesch reading ease: 75.0**.)

**Pass:** WCPM roughly in the ~90–110+ range (README §4 — this is a floor, not a

prosody ≤2 has not passed this part, regardless of WCPM — speed without expression
is not the exit target (research/05 §5b.6).

**Comprehension check (both tracks — ask after the timed read, untimed):** (1) What
does the text say to decide before a negotiation/purchase begins? (2) Name one thing
the text says matters as much as, or more than, price. (3) In your own words, what
is the text's main piece of advice?

## Part 5 — Extensive reading log review (5 min)

Review the learner's wide-reading log built up across the level (README §7,
reinforced in L5.16 §4). **Pass:** at least 3 separate titles/articles logged across
the level, each with brief answers to its 2–3 comprehension questions on record.
If the log is thin, this isn't a hard block on moving to Level 6 — but flag it, and
make sure Level 6's early sessions build in the wide-reading habit more explicitly,
since it is one of the level's stated exit criteria (README, "Exit").

---

## Overall result

- **4–5 parts passed:** proceed to Level 6.
- **3 parts passed:** proceed to Level 6, but flag the failed part(s) for early
  review in Level 6's warm-ups — don't silently drop it.
- **≤2 parts passed:** do not proceed. Identify which L5 sessions cover the weakest
  1–2 areas (README §2's table), re-run those sessions' Word Work + Check sections,
  and retest in a week, not the same day.
