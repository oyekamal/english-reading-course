# L4.6 — Loud Clouds, Slow Cows

**Goal:** Read and spell words with the diphthong **ou** and **ow** (/ow/ as in *out* and
*cow* — a different sound from the /ō/ ow the learner already knows from *snow*).
**New today:** ou, ow (/ow/)
**Review:** oi, oy, ar, or, ore, er, ir, ur, air, are, ear, eer, war, wor
**Heart words:** none new — review *because, would*
**Time:** 25 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Read 5 review words: *oil, point, coin, boy, enjoy.* Flash sounds: oi, oy, air, war.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say *out*. Isolate: /ow/ — starts open, glides toward /oo/. Say *cow*: /c/ /ow/. This is
another diphthong, like today's oi/oy but with a different glide.

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
Show **ou** and **ow**: "Ou and ow can both spell this /ow/ sound. This time there's no
perfectly reliable middle/end rule like oi/oy had — **ow usually goes at the end of a word
or before n/l** (*cow, town, owl*), **ou is more common in the middle** (*out, cloud, house*),
but there are exceptions, so you'll keep learning them word by word." Also remind: "ow can
say two different sounds — /ō/ as in *snow* (already known) or /ow/ as in *cow* (new
today). Always try the word both ways if you're not sure, and pick the one that's a real
word." **Urdu-speaker tip:** this glide is close to the vowel sound in "اب" *ab* stretched —
practice slow-to-fast: "ah-oo" → "ow."

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
ou: out, house, mouse, mouth, loud, cloud, round, found, sound, ground, count, south, shout,
about, proud, ouch, pound, hound, county.
ow (/ow/): cow, how, now, down, town, brown, crown, gown, clown, frown, drown, owl, growl,
howl, crowd, power.

Pseudowords: glout, throud, plown, scrow, dround.

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
1. Sounds: /l/ /ow/ /d/ → *loud*. /t/ /ow/ /n/ → *town*. /h/ /ow/ /s/ → *house.*
2. Words: *cloud, brown, sound.*
3. Sentence: **"The loud cow made a sound down by the house."**

## 6. Heart word(s) (2 min)
No new heart word today. Review **would**: mark the silent "l" as the heart part now that the
learner has more spelling patterns to compare it against (it doesn't match ou/ow/oi/oy at
all — a good moment to reinforce that heart words are memorized precisely because they break
the rules just learned).

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

### Track A — "The Loud Clown"
> It was a bright, loud day at the county fair. Bilal and his mom walked past a brown cow, a
> tiny mouse in a cage, and a fluffy owl blinking in the sun. Then a clown in a big gown and
> a bright crown came by, honking a horn. "That is so loud!" Bilal said, covering his mouth. The
> clown made a crowd of kids laugh out loud. A dog started to howl at the clown's honking
> horn, and an owl in a nearby cage let out a soft growl-like hoot. Bilal counted five clowns
> before he and his mom found their way to the food stand for a snack.

*Questions:* (1) What animals do Bilal and his mom see at the fair? (2) What does the clown
do that makes the crowd laugh? (3) Why might the dog start to howl?

### Track B — "A Power Cut Downtown"
> It is a hot day, and Naveed is at work downtown when the power goes out. The whole office is
> dark, and a loud, low sound comes from the backup generator switching on. "How long will
> this last?" a coworker asks. Naveed checks his device — no signal, either. He walks down to
> the ground floor, where a tiny crowd has formed near the front doors. Someone found a radio
> and is counting down the minutes until the power comes back. After about an hour, the lights
> come back on, and a round of quiet cheers moves across the crowd. "I am proud we stayed so
> calm," Naveed's boss says as everyone heads back to their desks.

*Questions:* (1) What happens at Naveed's office? (2) What does someone use to pass the
time? (3) Why does the boss say she is "proud"?

## Fluency check (2–3 min, every lesson)
Have the learner read Track A or Track B aloud once, cold (no rehearsal first). Time it and
count **WCPM** (words correct per minute): count total words read in one minute, subtract
errors not self-corrected. Note one **prosody** observation too — did they pause at commas
and periods, use a questioning tone on "?", change their voice for dialogue in quotes? Log
both numbers in the learner's own tracker. At this level, texts are short and heavily
controlled, so WCPM will stay fairly flat lesson to lesson — that is expected. The target is
smooth, accurate, expressive reading, never raw speed (assisted-practice model, not a race).

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud: "When many strangers stay calm together during a problem, like a power cut or a
delay, we can say the group showed **resilience** — the ability to handle a hard moment
without falling apart." **Tier-2 word: resilience** — friendly definition: "the ability to
recover from something hard and keep going." Example 1: "The team showed resilience after
losing the first game." Example 2: "Her resilience helped her finish the race despite the
rain." Your turn: "Can you think of a time you or someone you know showed resilience?"
*Discuss:* What helps a group of people stay calm during an unexpected problem?

## 9. Check (2 min)
Real words: *loud, town, house, cow, sound.*
Pseudowords: *glout, throun, scrow, dround, plout.*
Dictate: *"cloud."*
**≥90%? → 4.7. Below 90%?** Reteach step 3's word-position hint with a sort task (words on
strips: middle-of-word ou vs. end-of-word/before-n-l ow), then re-blend.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** reading **ow** with the *snow* /ō/ sound in a word that needs the /ow/
  sound (or vice versa) — e.g., reading *cow* as if it rhymed with *low*. **Correction:**
  teach "try it both ways, pick the real word" explicitly as a spoken routine, every time an
  ow-word is unfamiliar.
- **Common error:** spelling /ow/ as "ou" at the end of a word (writing "cou" for *cow*).
  **Correction:** point back to the position rule from step 3 during Spell it.
- **For adults:** Track B's office/power-cut scenario is common and relatable across many
  workplaces in Pakistan; adapt "generator" and "power cut" language as needed for the
  learner's exact context (e.g., "load-shedding" if that is the locally familiar term —
  note it is not decodable with today's patterns, so introduce only in speech, not print).
- **For young kids:** the fair scene is a good chance to talk about how a clown's job is to
  be loud and silly on purpose — connects vocabulary to a real cultural experience.
- **Never say:** "it's an exception, don't worry about it" without following up with the
  correct real-word check — every irregular reading still gets resolved by the flex-and-check
  routine, never left as a shrug.
