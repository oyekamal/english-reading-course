# L4.4 — Fair Weather, Rare Deer

**Goal:** Read and spell words with **air, are, ear, eer** (all /air/ or /eer/ sounds); review
the **war/wor** quirk (taught in 4.2) alongside them, since all of today's material is other
vowel-r teams.
**New today:** air, are, ear (as /air/), eer
**Review:** ar, or, ore (Level 3.08/3.09); er, ir, ur (4.3); war/wor quirk (4.2)
**Heart words:** none new — review *four, water, earth*
**Time:** 30 min (slightly longer — two vowel-r families in one lesson)
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Read 5 review words: *bird, turn, first, church, winter.* Flash sounds: ar, or, er/ir/ur.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say *fair*. Isolate: /f/ /air/. Say *care*: /c/ /air/. Say *deer*: /d/ /eer/. Point out: air,
are, and (a third spelling) ear can all say /air/; eer and ear can both say /eer/. This is
the trickiest vowel-r family so far — go slowly.

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
Show **air, are, ear** for the /air/ sound and **eer, ear** for the /eer/ sound (yes — *ear*
does double duty; the learner will need to try a word both ways and pick the one that's a
real word, previewing the "flex" strategy taught fully in 4.12). Quick review only of the
**war/wor** quirk from 4.2: "war says /wor/ like in *war, warm*; wor says /wer/ like in *work,
world* — the w bends the vowel sound." This is worth a fast recap here since it's the same
"a consonant changes the vowel that follows it" idea as everything else in this lesson.
**Urdu-speaker tip:** /air/ and /eer/ are both diphthong-like glides — practice saying them
slowly, then fast: "eh-uh-r" → "air"; "ee-uh-r" → "eer."

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
air: air, fair, hair, pair, chair, stair, flair.
are: care, dare, share, scare, stare, square, spare, bare, rare, flare.
ear (/air/): bear, wear, pear.
eer/ear (/eer/): deer, cheer, steer, sneer, near, dear, hear, fear, year, clear.
war/wor: war, warm, wart, ward, warn, work, word, world, worm, worth, worst.

Pseudowords: blair, zair, drare, sturn, worb.

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
1. Sounds: /f/ /air/ → *fair*. /d/ /eer/ → *deer*. /w/ /or/ /k/ → *work.*
2. Words: *chair, near, world.*
3. Sentence: **"We wear a coat when the air is not warm."**

## 6. Heart word(s) (2 min)
Review **water**: /w/ regular, "ater" is a heart part (not "a-ter" as expected). Review
**earth**: mark that "ear" here says a third sound, /er/ — flag this as an alternate reading
of *ear*, one the learner should hold loosely and check against real words, not assume for
every new ear-word.

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

### Track A — "The Rare Deer"
> On a cold, clear day, Hina and her dad hike near the hills. Hina wears a warm coat and a
> hat, but no gloves — she likes to feel the crisp air. "Look — a deer!" Dad says, showing her.
> It is a rare deer with pale hair on its back. The deer does not run. It stares at Hina, its
> ears turning this way and that. Hina stands still and does not dare move. After a year of
> hiking with her dad, this is the closest she has come to a deer. "I will never forget this,"
> Hina says. On the walk back, they share a pear and talk of the deer the whole way home.

*Questions:* (1) What does Hina wear, and what does she choose not to wear? (2) Why does
Hina stand still near the deer? (3) What do Hina and her dad do on the walk back?

### Track B — "A Hard Day's Work"
> Farah works at a tiny shop that sells chairs and tables. It is a warm day, and the work is
> hard. A pair of chairs must be moved to the back room, and Farah has to clear a path first.
> "Be careful — that chair leg is not square," her boss warns. Farah takes care, but her arms
> still hurt by the end of her shift. By closing time, a man comes in to buy a rare, old
> chair worth a fair price. Farah helps him carry it to his car. "That was the worst part of
> my day, but also the best sale," she tells her sister that night, and they share a laugh.

*Questions:* (1) What does Farah's shop sell? (2) Why does her boss warn her about the
chair? (3) What does Farah mean when she calls it "the worst part... but also the best sale"?

## Fluency check (2–3 min, every lesson)
Have the learner read Track A or Track B aloud once, cold (no rehearsal first). Time it and
count **WCPM** (words correct per minute): count total words read in one minute, subtract
errors not self-corrected. Note one **prosody** observation too — did they pause at commas
and periods, use a questioning tone on "?", change their voice for dialogue in quotes? Log
both numbers in the learner's own tracker. At this level, texts are short and heavily
controlled, so WCPM will stay fairly flat lesson to lesson — that is expected. The target is
smooth, accurate, expressive reading, never raw speed (assisted-practice model, not a race).

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud: "In many places, wild animals like deer must be **cautious** around people, even
kind ones, because their bodies are built to sense danger first and think later." **Tier-2
word: cautious** — friendly definition: "careful, watching closely for danger before acting."
Example 1: "The cat was cautious around the new dog." Example 2: "Be cautious when you cross
a busy road." Your turn: "Can you think of a time you felt cautious?"
*Discuss:* Why might a wild deer be cautious even around someone who means no harm? Is it
ever good for people to be cautious too?

## 9. Check (2 min)
Real words: *fair, care, deer, work, world.*
Pseudowords: *blar, ferl, worg, stort, zare.*
Dictate: *"near."*
**≥90%? → 4.5. Below 90%?** Reteach step 3, splitting the /air/ family and /eer/ family into
two separate mini-drills of 5 words each before re-checking.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** guessing between /air/ and /eer/ readings of **ear** without checking.
  **Correction:** teach the exact preview line: "try it one way — is that a real word? No?
  Try the other way." This is honest flex, not guessing, because it is anchored to checking
  against a real-word test, never to a picture or story context.
- **Common error:** missing the war/wor vowel shift and reading *work* as "work" with a
  regular or-sound (like "cork"). **Correction:** isolate war/wor as their own mini-family —
  drill *war, warm, work, world* together, pointing out the w each time.
- **For adults:** Track B's workplace framing (a shop, a shift, a sale) keeps vocabulary
  dignified; avoid switching to Track A's hiking scenario for an adult learner who has never
  hiked — use whichever track matches the learner's real life, and it's fine to write a
  third localized version if neither fits (a market stall instead of a furniture shop, etc.).
- **For young kids:** the deer story is a good moment to model "freeze and observe" as a real
  skill, separate from the phonics content.
- **Never say:** "just pick the one that looks right" — the real-word check must be spoken
  aloud and explicit every time.
