# L4.10 — Little Candle, Little Table

**Goal:** Read and spell two-syllable words ending in **consonant + le** (the sixth and last
syllable type: candle, table, little).
**New today:** consonant-le (the "C-le" syllable)
**Review:** ph, ch(/k/,/sh/), gh, and all earlier vowel-r/diphthong/aw teams
**Heart words:** none new
**Time:** 25 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Read 5 review words: *phone, school, ghost, rough, laugh.* Flash sounds: ph, ch(/k/), gh(/g/).

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say *candle*. Clap the syllables: can-dle. Ask: "What do you hear at the end — is it 'le'
like the letter L by itself, or something else?" Point out it's a soft, quick /əl/ sound —
not a strong vowel, more like a small "uhl."

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
Show **consonant + le**: "When a word ends in a consonant followed by 'le,' that whole chunk
— consonant plus le — is its own syllable, and it always sounds like /əl/. This is the sixth
and final syllable type — you now know all six: closed, open, VCe, vowel team, r-controlled,
and consonant-le." Show the split rule: **divide right before the consonant that comes
before le** (can-dle, ta-ble, ap-ple — note ap-ple splits between the two p's, a doubled
consonant, which is common with this pattern). **Urdu-speaker tip:** the quick, unstressed
/əl/ at the end can be easy to drop or over-pronounce; model saying it lightly, almost
swallowed, not as a full separate "ul" syllable.

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
candle, table, apple, little, bottle, purple, simple, uncle, bubble, puzzle, handle, needle,
cattle, saddle, wiggle, giggle, ankle, sample, wobble, tumble.

Pseudowords: fottle, glimple, thandle, scaddle, zuddle.

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
1. Sounds: /c/ /a/ /n/ /d/ /əl/ → *candle* (tutor: help the learner hear the chunk as one
   syllable, not sound-by-sound). /t/ /ay/ /b/ /əl/ → *table.*
2. Words: *little, purple, uncle.*
3. Sentence: **"Uncle put the purple candle on the little table."**

## 6. Heart word(s) (2 min)
No new heart word today — use this time for a quick cumulative review: pick 3 heart words at
random from the Level 1–3 list and have the learner read and spell them.

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

### Track A — "The Little Purple Table"
> Maya's uncle made her a little purple table for her room. It was simple but sturdy, with
> four short legs and a smooth top. "You can put your candle, your puzzle, and your bottle of
> paint on it," her uncle said. Maya set a small candle in the middle, next to a puzzle piece
> shaped like an apple. Her little sister Zoe came in, saw the table, and let out a giggle.
> "Can I have a table too, Uncle?" Zoe asked. "Maybe a purple one for you as well," he said
> with a smile. That night, Maya sat at her little table and worked on her puzzle until she
> could hardly keep her eyes open.

*Questions:* (1) What does Maya's uncle make for her? (2) What three things does Maya put on
the table? (3) What does Zoe ask her uncle?

### Track B — "Fixing the Wobbly Table"
> At the repair shop, Bilal is asked to fix a wobbly table for a customer. One leg is a little
> shorter than the others, and the handle on the drawer has come loose. Bilal takes a sample of
> the wood to check the color match, then tightens the handle with a small tool. He turns the
> table upside down and adds a thin block under the short leg so it no longer wobbles. "It was
> a simple fix," Bilal tells the customer when she comes to collect it, "but I wanted to be
> sure it was sturdy before you took it home." The customer checks the table, gives it a gentle
> push, and smiles — no wobble at all.

*Questions:* (1) What two problems does the table have? (2) How does Bilal fix the wobble?
(3) What does the customer do to check Bilal's work?

## Fluency check (2–3 min, every lesson)
Have the learner read Track A or Track B aloud once, cold (no rehearsal first). Time it and
count **WCPM** (words correct per minute): count total words read in one minute, subtract
errors not self-corrected. Note one **prosody** observation too — did they pause at commas
and periods, use a questioning tone on "?", change their voice for dialogue in quotes? Log
both numbers in the learner's own tracker. At this level, texts are short and heavily
controlled, so WCPM will stay fairly flat lesson to lesson — that is expected. The target is
smooth, accurate, expressive reading, never raw speed (assisted-practice model, not a race).

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud: "A worker who checks their own work carefully before calling a job finished shows
**diligence** — steady, careful effort, even on small details other people might not notice."
**Tier-2 word: diligence** — friendly definition: "careful, steady effort, especially in
checking details before finishing a job." Example 1: "Her diligence meant she checked every
answer twice." Example 2: "The carpenter's diligence showed in how smoothly the drawer
slid." Your turn: "Can you think of a time your own diligence (or someone else's) made a
difference?"
*Discuss:* Why might a customer trust a worker who shows diligence, even for a small job?

## 9. Check (2 min)
Real words: *candle, table, little, purple, handle.*
Pseudowords: *fottle, glimple, thandle, scaddle, zuddle.*
Dictate: *"apple."*
**≥90%? → 4.11. Below 90%?** Reteach step 3's split rule using letter tiles or written
syllable cards: physically separate can/dle, ta/ble, ap/ple, then re-blend each half before
joining.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** reading the final "le" as a full extra syllable with a strong vowel
  (saying "can-dul-eh"). **Correction:** model the light, quick /əl/ — exaggerate how brief
  it is compared to a stressed syllable.
- **Common error:** splitting the word in the wrong place (e.g., "ca-ndle" instead of
  "can-dle"). **Correction:** always split right before the consonant that comes right before
  le — practice tapping the word into two claps to find the natural break.
- **For adults:** Track B's repair-shop context gives concrete vocabulary (*wobble, sample,
  sturdy*) with clear real-world use, not childish framing.
- **For young kids:** the "why does Zoe want a table too" moment is a natural sharing/fairness
  discussion beyond the phonics content.
- **Never say:** "just say le at the end" without connecting it back to the consonant before
  it — the consonant is part of the syllable, not a separate unit.
