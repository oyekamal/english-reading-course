# L4.1 — Cars in the Yard (ar extended)

**Goal:** Review **ar** (/ar/ as in *car*, taught in Level 3.08) and extend it to
**multisyllabic ar words** (*garden, market, carpet, harvest, target*) — the first place in
Level 4 the learner meets the "chunk a longer word by syllable" habit that 4.12/4.14 will
name formally.
**New today:** nothing brand-new at the grapheme level — this lesson's job is fluency and
range: single-syllable ar review → confident multisyllabic ar words, both closed-first-
syllable (gar-den) and ar-first-syllable (tar-get) shapes.
**Review:** ar (Level 3.08); all Level 1–3 patterns (closed syllables, VCe, open syllables,
vowel teams, blends, digraphs)
**Heart words:** none new — review *are, water, once, only*
**Time:** 25 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil; optional letter tiles or index cards for the "ar" tile

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Flash these sounds/words for instant response: sh, oa, igh, ee, dge, ar. Then read 5 review
words: *car, star, dark, yard, spark* — these should already be fast and automatic from
Level 3; if they're not, pause here and re-drill single-syllable ar before moving to
multisyllabic words below.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say *garden*. Clap it: gar-den. Ask: "You already know /ar/ from *car* — can you hear it
inside this bigger word?" Do 2 more: *market* (mar-ket), *carpet* (car-pet). The point: ar
doesn't change or hide inside a longer word — the same sound, the same spelling, just one
chunk among several.

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
Quick review only (skip if the learner is already fast on Warm-up): "ar together says /ar/ —
one sound, mouth open, tongue back, no rolled r." Then introduce today's actual new
territory: "Longer words are just two or more of these familiar chunks stuck together. If
you can read *car*, you can read *car-pet* — read each chunk, then blend them." **Urdu-speaker
tip:** Urdu speakers often want to add a light vowel before the r or roll the r more than
English does, especially under the extra pressure of a longer word — slow the multisyllabic
words down chunk by chunk rather than trying to say the whole thing at speed first.

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
Single-syllable review: car, far, jar, bar, star, scar, art, cart, part, smart, start, chart,
spark, shark, sharp, harm, farm, yard, hard, dark, park, arm, alarm.

Multisyllabic ar words (today's extension — clap/chunk each one first): market, garden,
carpet, target, harvest, garment, cargo, barnyard, farmhand, artist.

Pseudowords (label "alien words"): sart, darm, plarn, farl, starb, clart, gartel (2-syllable
alien word — practice chunking: gar-tel).

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
Dictate sounds → words → sentence.
1. Sounds: /c/ /ar/ → *car*. /d/ /ar/ /k/ → *dark*. /s/ /t/ /ar/ /t/ → *start*.
2. Words: *yard, farm, spark, chart.*
3. Sentence (segment with fingers first): **"The car is in the yard."**

## 6. Heart word(s) (2 min)
Review **are** (already taught): point out are is now fully explainable — /ar/ is exactly the
sound just learned, only the spelling **are** drops the sound of the silent final e. Mark it:
"ar" is the regular, decodable part; the silent e at the end is the only quirk.

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

### Track A — "Star Night on the Farm"
> Sam and his dad live on a farm. At dark, they walk to the yard. Sam can see a big, bright
> star. "That star is so very far," Dad said. Sam runs to get his cart. He puts a jar, a
> hard hat, and a sharp pencil in the cart. He wants to make a star chart. Dad helps Sam
> mark each star on the chart. "You are a smart artist," Dad said. Sam grins. The farm is
> still, and the stars spark and shine in the dark yard.

*Questions:* (1) Where do Sam and his dad walk? (2) What does Sam put in his cart? (3) Why
do you think Sam wants to make a star chart?

### Track B — "The Market Car Park"
> Mr. Khan drives his car to the market. He must park in the car park at the back. It is
> hard to find a spot — the car park is full. At last, Mr. Khan spots a gap by the yard. He
> parks his car, locks it, and walks to the market. He needs a jar of jam, a sharp pencil, and
> a tiny chart for his garden. On the way back, a spark of light catches his eye — the sun
> is starting to sink behind the dark market roofs. Mr. Khan is glad he did not start so late.

*Questions:* (1) Why is it hard for Mr. Khan to park? (2) What three things does he buy?
(3) What tells you the day is ending?

## Fluency check (2–3 min, every lesson)
Have the learner read Track A or Track B aloud once, cold (no rehearsal first). Time it and
count **WCPM** (words correct per minute): count total words read in one minute, subtract
errors not self-corrected. Note one **prosody** observation too — did they pause at commas
and periods, use a questioning tone on "?", change their voice for dialogue in quotes? Log
both numbers in the learner's own tracker. At this level, texts are short and heavily
controlled, so WCPM will stay fairly flat lesson to lesson — that is expected. The target is
smooth, accurate, expressive reading, never raw speed (assisted-practice model, not a race).

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud (learner listens only): "Long ago, before streetlights, farmers and travelers
used the stars to find their way home. A bright, easy-to-spot group of stars is called a
**constellation**." **Tier-2 word: constellation** — friendly definition: "a group of stars
that makes a shape people gave a name to, like a picture in the sky." Example 1: "The Big
Dipper is a constellation shaped like a spoon." Example 2: "Sailors once used constellations
like a map." Your turn: "Can you think of a shape you'd draw by connecting stars?"
*Discuss:* Why might people long ago need the stars to find their way? Have you ever seen a
very bright, very dark sky, in a village or the countryside?

## 9. Check (2 min)
Real words: *car, dark, spark, market, carpet* — read aloud, multisyllabic ones chunked.
Pseudowords: *starn, farb, sarb, tarl, gartel* (2-syllable).
Dictate: *"park."*
**≥90% (9/10 + dictation)? → go to 4.2.** **Below 90%?** If single-syllable words are the
problem, re-teach step 2 (Hear it) with only 3 words (*car, star, farm*), isolating /ar/
orally before repeating step 4's single-syllable list. If single-syllable words are solid but
multisyllabic ones aren't, the issue is chunking, not the ar sound itself — go straight back
to step 4's multisyllabic list and physically clap/mark the syllable break before re-blending.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** reading "ar" as two separate sounds (short a + rolled r), especially for
  Urdu-first learners. **Correction:** never say "sound it out letter by letter" for ar —
  say "ar is a team, like ee or oa. What does the team say?" and re-model the single sound.
- **Common error:** confusing **ar** with **or** words seen elsewhere already (this is
  pre-4.2, so this shouldn't occur yet — flag if it does, it usually means the learner is
  guessing from a sibling's book, not decoding).
- **For adults:** use Track B and point out that "car park," "market," and "garden" are
  everyday words they already say — this lesson gives them the spelling, not new vocabulary.
- **For young kids:** the "alien word" framing works well here — let them use a silly voice
  for pseudowords to keep segmenting playful.
- **Never say:** "guess from the picture" or "does that look right?" before decoding. Only
  after a word is fully sounded out may you ask "does that make sense?"
