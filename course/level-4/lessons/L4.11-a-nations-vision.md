# L4.11 — A Nation's Vision

**Goal:** Read words with five common Latin/French endings: **-tion, -sion, -ture, -ous,
-cial/-tial** — the endings that show up constantly in longer, more grown-up words.
**New today:** -tion, -sion, -ture, -ous, -cial/-tial
**Review:** consonant-le, and all earlier vowel-r/diphthong/aw/silent-letter/ph-ch-gh patterns
**Heart words:** none new
**Time:** 30 min
**Materials:** paper, pencil

## 1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
Read 5 review words: *candle, table, little, purple, handle.* Flash sounds: consonant-le.

## 2. Hear it (2 min)
Say *nation*. Ask: "What does the end of this word sound like?" (/shən/.) Say *vision*: "And
this one?" (/zhən/ — same idea, slightly buzzier.) Say *picture*: "And this?" (/chər/.) Say
*famous*: "And this?" (/əs/.) Say *special*: "And this?" (/shəl/.)

## 3. Meet it (3 min)
Show all five endings together as a "these all show up at the end of longer words" set:
- **-tion** and **-cial/-tial** → say /shən/ or /shəl/ (most common spelling for that sound).
- **-sion** → usually /shən/ too, but sometimes buzzes to /zhən/ (*vision, decision*) —
  no clean rule for which; learn each by ear.
- **-ture** → always /chər/.
- **-ous** → always /əs/, meaning "full of" (*dangerous* = full of danger).
Point out: none of these endings are sounded out letter-by-letter — each is read as one
chunk, the way consonant-le was. **Urdu-speaker tip:** English loves stacking these endings
onto shorter root words (*act → action, danger → dangerous*) — noticing the root word inside
the longer word is often the fastest way in, more useful than sounding letter-by-letter.

## 4. Blend it (5 min)
-tion: action, nation, station, motion, lotion, attention, invention, direction, position,
portion, section.
-sion: vision, decision, mission, session, explosion, division, confusion.
-ture: picture, nature, future, adventure, capture, feature, mixture, creature.
-ous: famous, dangerous, curious, nervous, jealous, generous, enormous.
-cial/-tial: special, social, official, partial, initial.
*Tutor note: after **s**, -tion says /chən/, not /shən/: question /ˈkwɛs.tʃən/, suggestion. This word appears in today's story, so point it out before reading.*

Pseudowords: gorsion, plection, thastial, frenture, blezious.

## 5. Spell it (4 min)
1. Sounds/chunks: /n/ /ay/ /shən/ → *nation.* /p/ /i/ /k/ /chər/ → *picture.* /d/ /ay/
   /n/ /jər/ /əs/ → *dangerous* (tutor: point out this word's root, "danger," inside it).
2. Words: *action, vision, nature.*
3. Sentence: **"Pay attention — that creature looks dangerous!"**

## 6. Heart word(s) (2 min)
No new heart word today. Use the time to check two or three words from the growing bank of
multisyllabic words across 4.1–4.10 (e.g., *market, morning, wobbly*) to keep long-word
reading warmed up.

## 7. Read it (5–8 min)

### Track A — "The Curious Creature"
> On a school trip to the nature center, Bina's class saw a strange creature in a tank. "Pay
> attention," her teacher said, "this fish is famous for its unusual motion — watch how it
> moves." Bina leaned in, curious, and took a picture with her class tablet. The creature was
> not dangerous, the guide explained, just shy. "It hides when there's too much attention on
> it," the guide said. Bina made a decision: she would ask her question quietly. "What does it
> eat?" she whispered. The guide smiled at her care and gave a full explanation. Bina felt she
> had learned something special about nature that day, and she couldn't wait to share her
> picture with her family.

*Questions:* (1) What creature does Bina's class see? (2) Why does the guide say the
creature hides? (3) What decision does Bina make?

### Track B — "A Session at the Clinic"
> Dr. Rehman starts a special session with a new patient today. "I want to give you my full
> attention," she says, "so let's talk about your question in detail." The patient explains a
> mixture of symptoms — some minor, some she's nervous about. Dr. Rehman takes note of each one
> with care, then explains her decision: a short series of tests, plus a change in diet. "This
> plan should not cause any confusion," Dr. Rehman says, "and I will check on you next
> session." The patient feels reassured by Dr. Rehman's calm, generous attention. "Thank you
> for taking this so seriously," she says as she leaves. Dr. Rehman notes the session in the
> patient's official file before her next appointment.

*Questions:* (1) What does Dr. Rehman want to give her patient? (2) What does the patient
explain during the session? (3) What is Dr. Rehman's decision at the end?

## Fluency check (2–3 min, every lesson)
Have the learner read Track A or Track B aloud once, cold (no rehearsal first). Time it and
count **WCPM** (words correct per minute): count total words read in one minute, subtract
errors not self-corrected. Note one **prosody** observation too — did they pause at commas
and periods, use a questioning tone on "?", change their voice for dialogue in quotes? Log
both numbers in the learner's own tracker. At this level, texts are short and heavily
controlled, so WCPM will stay fairly flat lesson to lesson — that is expected. The target is
smooth, accurate, expressive reading, never raw speed (assisted-practice model, not a race).

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)
Read aloud: "A good doctor, teacher, or guide often shows **attentiveness** — noticing small
details about a person or a situation that someone in a hurry might miss." **Tier-2 word:
attentiveness** — friendly definition: "paying close, careful attention to someone or
something, noticing details." Example 1: "The waiter's attentiveness made the meal feel
special." Example 2: "Her attentiveness to her patients' worries made them trust her." Your
turn: "Can you think of someone whose attentiveness made you feel cared for?"
*Discuss:* What is the difference between someone being attentive and someone just being
polite?

## 9. Check (2 min)
Real words: *nation, vision, picture, famous, special.*
Pseudowords: *gorsion, plection, thastial, frenture, blezious.*
Dictate: *"action."*
**≥90%? → 4.12. Below 90%?** Reteach by ending, one at a time: drill 5 -tion words to
mastery, then 5 -ture words, then combine — don't reteach all five endings in one pass if a
learner is overwhelmed.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** sounding out these endings letter by letter (t-i-o-n) instead of reading
  the whole chunk as /shən/. **Correction:** always model "this whole ending is one chunk,
  said one way" — treat it exactly like a vowel team or consonant-le, never phoneme-by-
  phoneme.
- **Common error:** guessing whether -sion says /shən/ or /zhən/ by feel and getting
  frustrated. **Correction:** reassure the learner this is genuinely unpredictable and
  learned word by word — it's not a failure to "not know the rule," because there isn't one.
- **For adults:** Track B's clinic-session context uses these five endings constantly and
  naturally (*session, attention, decision, confusion, official*) — this is realistic adult
  register, not simplified.
- **For young kids:** the "notice the root word inside" strategy (danger→dangerous) is worth
  practicing explicitly — ask "what smaller word do you see hiding in this long word?"
- **Never say:** "sound it out" for these five endings — say "chunk it" instead, to keep the
  strategy distinct from letter-by-letter blending.

## Decodability audit (Track A + Track B texts)
Graphemes/patterns used beyond today's **-tion/-sion/-ture/-ous/-cial-tial**: all single
letters; sh, ch, th, ng; blends (bl, cl, dr, cr, gr, sp, st, tr, sk, sw); -s/-es, -ed, -ing;
VCe (a_e as in *take→not used*, i_e as in *time→not used, smile→not used*); open syllables
(*so, no, Bina*); ee (*seen→not used, need→not used, feel, three→not used*); ai/ay (*explain,
today, way→not used*); oi/oy/ou/ow (*wait, out→not used*); ar (*care, started→not used,

consonant-le (*table→not used, special→cial today*); ea(/ĕ/) (*ready→not used, instead→not
used*). Heart words used: *the, is, to, and, was, her, said, she, do, they, are, would,
