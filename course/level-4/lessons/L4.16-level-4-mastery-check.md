# L4.16 — Level 4 Mastery Check

**Goal:** Confirm the learner has mastered every Level 4 pattern and the word-attack routine
well enough to move to Level 5 (Word Power & Fluency), which shifts from controlled decodable
text to wide reading.
**New today:** nothing new — this is a consolidation + testing lesson
**Review:** every pattern taught in 4.1–4.15
**Heart words:** full cumulative review (Levels 1–4)
**Time:** 35–40 min (may be split across two sittings)
**Materials:** paper, pencil; the standalone `mastery-check.md` instrument in this folder

## 1. Warm-up review (5 min)
Rapid-fire read (no more than 2 seconds per word) one review word from each Level 4 lesson:
*car, fork, bird, chair, boy, cloud, ball, bread, phone, table, nation, napkin, running,
unfriendly.* This is a temperature check, not the real test — note which patterns feel shaky.

## 2. Full pattern review (8–10 min)
Go through the six syllable types by name, with one example each (learner supplies the
example if possible): closed, open, VCe, vowel team, r-controlled, consonant-le. Then review,
in order: ar / or, ore / er, ir, ur / air, are, ear, eer, war, wor / oi, oy / ou, ow(/ow/) /
aw, au, al(l) / ea(/ĕ/) + kn, wr, gn, mb / ph, ch(/k/,/sh/), gh / consonant-le / -tion, -sion,
-ture, -ous, -cial/-tial / syllable division + schwa / suffix spelling rules / the five-step
word-attack routine.

## 3. Run the standalone mastery check (15–20 min)
Open `../mastery-check.md` and administer it in full: real-word list, pseudoword list
(including multisyllabic pseudowords), dictation, and the authentic-text passage with the
unknown-word protocol demonstration. Score exactly as that document specifies.

## Fluency check (2–3 min)
As part of the mastery check's passage reading, record final WCPM and a prosody rating using
the same method as every Level 4 lesson. This becomes the learner's Level 4 exit fluency
baseline, referenced again at the start of Level 5.

## 4. Decide (2 min)
**≥90% on the full instrument, AND correct demonstration of the word-attack routine on at
least 2 of 3 pseudowords, AND correct narration of the unknown-word protocol on the authentic
passage?** → the learner exits Level 4. Proceed to Level 5.
**Below 90%, or routine/protocol not yet reliable?** Do not advance on calendar time alone
(DESIGN.md rule 4). Identify exactly which pattern(s) failed using the per-section breakdown
in `mastery-check.md`, return to that specific lesson (4.1–4.15) for a full reteach using the
"below 90%" reteach step already written into that lesson, then re-administer only the
failed section(s) of the mastery check — not the whole instrument — before reassessing.

## Tutor notes
- **Common error:** treating this as a pass/fail exam with no diagnostic value. **Correction:**
  the whole point of a per-section breakdown is to send the learner back to exactly the right
  lesson, not to "review everything" vaguely. This matters most because Wanzek et al. (2013)
  found **group size** — not calendar time or session frequency — was the significant
  moderator of outcomes for older struggling readers, with the smallest groups (1:1 or near
  it) showing the largest effects (research/03 §1.5); a self-paced course's real advantage is
  effectively "group size = 1," and a vague, unfocused re-review of everything squanders that
  advantage by diluting the 1:1 attention back toward a whole-class-style pass.
- **Common error:** letting a strong score on real words mask a weak score on pseudowords
  (i.e., the learner has memorized real words rather than truly decoding). **Correction:**
  the pseudoword and multisyllabic-pseudoword sections are not optional extras — they are the
  actual proof of decoding skill, per Level 1's founding principle (self-teaching only works
  from real decoding, not memorization).
- **For adults:** frame this milestone explicitly and respectfully: reaching the end of
  Level 4 means the learner can now decode virtually any English word using rules and
  routines, the same toolkit a skilled adult reader uses (research/05's finding that even
  skilled adult readers parse words by syllable/morpheme, not whole-word memory) — this is
  not a "kids' milestone," it is the core mechanical skill of reading, full stop.
- **For young kids:** celebrate this as a real milestone (finishing "The Full Code") without
  turning it into pressure — the mastery check is there to help, not to be feared; pause-safe
  and resumable as always (DESIGN.md rule 10).
- **Never say:** "you failed" — always frame a below-90% result as "here's exactly what to
  practice next," in keeping with the course's no-streak-shaming rule.

## What comes next
Level 5 (Word Power & Fluency) shifts weight from phonics to morphology (20 prefixes, key
suffixes, Greek/Latin roots), builds a dedicated fluency program (WCPM + prosody targets
against real benchmarks), and moves from controlled decodable text to wide reading — the
word-attack routine from 4.14 keeps working throughout, now applied to real books, articles,
and the learner's own reading choices instead of purpose-written lesson texts.
