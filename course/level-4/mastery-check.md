# Level 4 Mastery Check — "The Full Code"

**Purpose:** a standalone, printable/readable testing instrument confirming a learner can
exit Level 4 and enter Level 5. Administer after lesson 4.16's review, or any time a learner
believes they are ready (pause-safe, resumable — DESIGN.md rule 10; "skip if you know it"
still means take this check first, per rule 10).

**How to score:** each section below has its own pass line. Record raw scores per section —
do not average sections together into one blended number, since that would hide exactly
which pattern needs reteaching (this is the diagnostic point of the instrument, per rule 4:
mastery gate, not calendar).

---

## Section 2 — Pseudowords, one syllable (10 words)

Label these "alien words" for the learner. ✓ if blended correctly using the target pattern
(any plausible reading of an ambiguous vowel-r sound counts — the test is decoding
mechanics, not one "true" pronunciation).

*darl, storb, thurl, blair, throud, scall, gnop, wrab, phend, chask*

**Pass line: 9/10 (90%).**

## Section 3 — Multisyllabic pseudowords (8 words — the real proof of the word-attack routine)

Have the learner narrate at least the peel/chunk steps aloud for the first 3, then read the
rest with or without narration.

*contramble, fenscription, disflorted, unpladding, remistion, gorsional, thendiculate,
zampervent*

**Pass line: 7/8 (87.5%)**, AND correct verbal narration of at least the "peel affix, chunk
by syllable type" steps on 2 of the first 3 words.

## Section 4 — Real multisyllabic words (10 words, drawing on 4.10–4.14 content)

*candle, napkin, spider, cabin, dangerous, attention, picture, unfriendly, disconnected,
reconsidering*

**Pass line: 9/10 (90%).**

## Section 5 — Dictation (spelling; 8 items)

Read each aloud once at normal speed, then again slowly if needed. Learner writes it.

1. yard (ar)
2. store (ore)
3. shirt (ir)
4. square (are)
5. join (oi)
6. town (ow)
7. wall (al)
8. hoping → running → happier (say all three; learner applies drop-e, doubling, y-to-i
   correctly — score each of the three separately)

**Pass line: 8/10 individual items correct (counting the three-word #8 as three items,
10 total).**

## Section 6 — Authentic passage + unknown-word protocol (the off-ramp test)

Have the learner read the following unedited-style authentic passage aloud, cold:

> **Community Notice: Road Closure**
> Residents are informed that Elm Street will be closed to traffic between the market and
> the health clinic from Monday to Thursday for scheduled resurfacing work. Pedestrians may
> still access the footpath on the north side of the street. Officials recommend allowing
> extra time for any journey through this area during the closure. Vehicles requiring
> emergency access should contact the number listed below. We appreciate residents'
> patience during this necessary maintenance.

**Score two things separately:**
- **Accuracy:** words read correctly ÷ total words (this passage is 78 words). **Pass line:
  ≥95% accuracy (no more than 4 errors).**
- **Protocol demonstration:** pick 2 words the learner hesitated on or misread first (likely
  candidates: *residents, resurfacing, pedestrians, journey, emergency, maintenance*) and have
  them re-decode using the full unknown-word protocol from 4.15, narrating each step aloud.
  **Pass line: correct, complete narration of the 5-step protocol on both chosen words.**

### Fluency reading (WCPM + prosody)
Time the same passage reading. Record **WCPM** (words correct per minute) and rate prosody on
this simple 3-point scale: **1 = word-by-word, flat** · **2 = some phrasing, some flat spots**
· **3 = smooth phrasing, appropriate pauses at punctuation, expression on the notice's formal
tone.** There is no fixed WCPM pass line at this level (Level 5 introduces benchmarked WCPM
targets) — record the number as this learner's Level 4 exit baseline and log the prosody
rating; a rating of 1 with otherwise passing accuracy is a flag to keep an eye on in Level 5,
not a reason to hold the learner back from advancing on this instrument alone.

---

## Overall exit decision

**Exit Level 4 (move to Level 5) if ALL of the following hold:**
- Section 1 ≥ 14/16
- Section 2 ≥ 9/10
- Section 3 ≥ 7/8 AND narration passes
- Section 4 ≥ 9/10
- Section 5 ≥ 8/10
- Section 6 accuracy ≥ 95% AND protocol narration passes on both chosen words

**If any section falls short:** do not advance. Return to the specific Level 4 lesson(s)
tied to the missed pattern(s) (see the table below), reteach using that lesson's built-in
"below 90%" reteach step, then re-administer only the failed section(s) — never the whole
instrument — before reassessing. This is diagnostic, not punitive (DESIGN.md rule 10:
no streak-shaming, pause-safe).

| If this section is weak... | ...reteach from |
|---|---|
| Section 1, items 1–5 | L4.1–L4.4 |
| Section 1, items 6–10 | L4.5–L4.7 |
| Section 1, items 11–16 | L4.8–L4.9 |
| Section 2 | whichever pattern(s) the missed pseudowords used |
| Section 3 | L4.12 (syllable division/schwa) and L4.14 (word-attack routine) |
| Section 4, consonant-le / multisyllabic items | L4.10, L4.12 |
| Section 4, -tion/-ous/-ture items | L4.11 |
| Section 5, doubling / y-to-i / drop-e | L4.13 |
| Section 6 (either accuracy or protocol) | L4.15 |

## Heart words — full cumulative check (optional but recommended)

Quickly re-check a sample of 10 heart words drawn across Levels 1–4 (the full list lives in
each level's README): *was, does, they, could, laugh, water, earth, money, people, because.*
This is not scored toward the exit decision above but flags any heart word worth a quick
refresh before Level 5 begins its expanded vocabulary work.
