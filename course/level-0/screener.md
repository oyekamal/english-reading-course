# Reading-Difficulty Screener (Non-Diagnostic)

## What this is — and is not

This is a short set of questions that flags "it might be worth getting a formal evaluation for
a reading difficulty (such as dyslexia)." **It does not diagnose anything.** Nobody using this
course is ever labeled dyslexic, slow, or anything else by this tool. It only ever produces one
of two outcomes: "no flags — keep going with your placed level" or "some flags — the course
still works for you, and here's what to consider doing alongside it."

Why include this at all: adult and child reading difficulties (including dyslexia) are common,
under-diagnosed, and the signs are well documented — but self-report screeners like the one
this is modeled on (the Adult Reading History Questionnaire, ARHQ) are explicitly validated as
screening tools, not diagnostic instruments (`research/03` §1 item 10, §5). This course follows
that same non-diagnostic design.

## Part A — Adult self-report (answer about yourself)

Answer Yes/No to each. Be honest — there's no "good" score here, only useful information.

1. Did you find learning to read noticeably harder than people around you, when you were
   first taught (in any language)?
2. Do you still read slowly or with real effort compared to people your age?
3. Do you often mix up the order of letters in a word, or misread similar-looking words
   (e.g., "form" for "from")?
4. Do you avoid reading aloud in front of others if you can help it?
5. Do you have trouble spelling words you can say easily, even simple ones?
6. Have you ever struggled a lot more than others to learn another language — including your
   first language, if you learned it later, or a second language?
7. Does/did anyone in your immediate family (parent, sibling, child) have a diagnosed reading
   difficulty, or struggle noticeably with reading?
8. Do you sometimes mix up left/right, or find it hard to follow multi-step spoken
   directions?
9. Did you need much more practice than classmates/peers to memorize letter-sound
   correspondences, times tables, or sequences (days of the week, months)?
10. Do you often lose your place when reading, or need to re-read a line or sentence to
    understand it?

**Source basis:** items adapted from IDA and Additude's published adult dyslexia sign lists and
the ARHQ's self-report item style (`research/03` §1 item 10, §6, source list).

## Part B — Child observation checklist (a parent/tutor answers about a child learner)

1. Did the child start talking noticeably later than siblings/peers?
2. Does the child still confuse similar sounds when speaking (e.g., "aminal" for "animal")?
3. Does the child have unusual trouble learning nursery rhymes or noticing rhyme?
4. Does the child struggle to remember the names of familiar objects, or often say "thing"/
   "that" instead of the actual word?
5. Does the child find it much harder than peers to learn the alphabet or letter sounds,
   despite repeated practice?
6. Does the child reverse letters (b/d, p/q) or numbers well past the age this is expected to
   fade (roughly by age 7–8, though this varies)?
7. Does the child avoid reading activities, or become unusually frustrated/tired during them?
8. Is there a family history of reading difficulty or diagnosed dyslexia?
9. Does the child follow multi-step spoken instructions less well than same-age peers?
10. Does the child's spoken vocabulary and general understanding seem stronger than their
    reading — i.e., they clearly "get" things when told aloud, but not from print?

## Scoring

Count "Yes" answers. There is no fixed pass/fail cutoff — this is a flag, not a test — but as
a rough guide:

- **0–2 Yes:** No particular flag. Proceed with the course as placed.
- **3–5 Yes:** Some flags. The course still works — structured, explicit, cumulative
  instruction benefits everyone, including learners with reading difficulties
  (`research/03` §2) — but consider mentioning this to a doctor, school counselor, or
  learning-difficulties specialist if one is available to you, and read the "if flagged"
  section below.
- **6+ Yes:** Several flags. Same as above, more strongly: this course remains the right place
  to start (there is no other prerequisite), and a formal evaluation is worth actively seeking
  out if you're able to.

## If flagged: what to do

1. **Keep going with the course.** Nothing about a flag here changes what lesson you start at
   — that's decided by `placement-test.md` alone. Structured, explicit, systematic,
   cumulative phonics instruction (exactly what this course is built on) is the same approach
   recommended for learners with diagnosed reading difficulties — it isn't a "typical learner
   only" method (`research/03` §2, IDA Structured Literacy definition).
2. **Expect the pace to matter more than the method.** If you scored several flags, plan for
   more repetition and smaller steps than the standard lesson pace assumes — that's what the
   mastery-gate rule is for (never advance on a calendar, always on a ≥90% check —
   `DESIGN.md` rule 4). Re-do a lesson as many times as needed; there's no penalty.
3. **Seek a formal evaluation if you can.** This screener cannot diagnose dyslexia or any other
   condition — only a qualified professional (educational psychologist, learning-difficulties
   specialist, or in some places a pediatrician/GP referral) can do that. A formal diagnosis
   can unlock specific accommodations (extra exam time, assistive technology) that this course
   alone cannot provide.
4. **Never treat "difficulty learning a foreign language" as automatically an English-specific
   problem.** If you're an adult ESL learner, remember that ordinary second-language
   acquisition is hard for everyone; this screener specifically asks whether you *also* found
   your first language difficult, because that's the more specific signal for an underlying
   reading difficulty rather than normal L2 struggle (`research/03` §6).
5. **You are not your score.** This tool exists to route you to the right kind of support, not
   to label you. Many people with flagged profiles read very well as adults, with the right
   instruction and enough time.

## What this screener never does

- It never appears anywhere else in the course as a label on your profile.
- It is never used to deny anyone access to any level or lesson.
- It never substitutes for a professional diagnosis, and this document does not claim to
  diagnose dyslexia, ADHD, or any other condition.
