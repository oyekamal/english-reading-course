# Start Here — Read English

## What this is

This is a free, complete course that takes a person from "I cannot read a single English
letter" to "I can read a policy paper, a contract, or a news article and judge whether to
believe it." It is built only from what reading research actually supports — no guessing
games, no "look at the picture," no rote flashcard drilling passed off as reading instruction.
Every method used here is named in `research/01`–`07` (in the course's `research/` folder) with
its evidence rating; every method rejected is named too, with why.

It is **one course**, not a children's course and a separate adult course bolted together.
The sounds, spelling patterns, and reading skills are identical whether you are 6 or 60. Only
the *sentences and pictures used to practice them* differ — see "Track A / Track B" below.
Nobody using the adult track will ever be handed a lesson written for a child (DESIGN.md
rule 6; research/04 on adult stigma).

## Who it's for

- A child who has never been taught to read.
- A teenager or adult — native English speaker or not — who was never taught properly, or
  who was taught with methods that didn't work (guessing from pictures, memorizing whole
  words).
- An Urdu, Punjabi, Pashto, or other South-Asian-language speaker learning English as a
  second language, at any age.
- A parent or tutor teaching any of the above, with no teaching qualification required.
- A classroom teacher who wants a scope-and-sequence that is honest about what is proven and
  what is not.

There is no age-gating anywhere in this course. You are placed by what you can actually do,
not by your birthday (this is deliberate — see `research/04` §5 on "Teaching at the Right
Level," the single best-evidenced placement mechanism found in the research pass, 0.07–0.70 SD
gains across 6 Indian RCTs).

## The 7 levels, in one table

| Level | Name | What you learn | You're done when… | Roughly like… |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Start Here | How to use this course, the placement test, a reading-difficulty screener, the Urdu-speaker guide, the tutor/parent guide | You know where to start | — |
| 1 | First Sounds | Hearing sounds in words, all single letters, simple 3-sound words (cat, sun, big) | You can read and spell simple real and made-up 3-sound words, ~9 times out of 10 | Pre-A1 |
| 2 | Sounds Together | Letter pairs (sh, ch, th), blends (st, fl, sp), plurals, -ed/-ing endings | You can read words like "shrimp," "jumped," "napkin" | Pre-A1 / A1 |
| 3 | Long Vowels | Silent-e (hope), vowel teams (ai, ee, oa), y as a vowel | You can read words like "explain," "coach," "happy" | A1 |
| 4 | The Full Code | r-controlled vowels (ar, or, er), diphthongs (oi, ow), syllable division, schwa | You can read most multisyllable English words you've never seen before, and start reading real (non-simplified) text | A1 / A2 |
| 5 | Word Power & Fluency | Prefixes, suffixes, word roots, reading smoothly and with expression, a large vocabulary | You read a paragraph aloud smoothly, at a natural pace, and understand it | A2–B1 |
| 6 | Reading to Learn | Reading to understand and remember non-fiction, spotting how a text is organized, reading across several paragraphs, checking sources online | You can read a B2-level article and explain what it's really saying | B1–B2 |
| 7 | Advanced & Critical | Reading a history source, a science article, a contract, an argument, data in a chart — and judging whether to trust it | You read C1-level material critically, across multiple sources | C1–C2 |

Full detail on why each level is built the way it is: `DESIGN.md` §1 and §3 in the course root.

## How to use this course

### With a tutor or parent

Read `guide-tutors-parents.md` in this folder before your first lesson. It has the exact
words to use when correcting a mistake, what to do when a learner is stuck, and a progress
sheet. You do **not** need a teaching qualification — the lessons are scripted.

### Alone, as an adult

You can run this course yourself. `placement-test.md` has a self-test version — record
yourself on your phone reading the word lists, then check against the answer key. Levels 1–4
lessons work solo with paper and a pencil; from Level 5 you'll want someone (or a recording)
to check your reading aloud occasionally, but everything else is self-checkable.

### In a classroom

Place each learner individually using `placement-test.md` (it takes 5–20 minutes per learner).
Group learners by placement level, not by age or grade — this is the single most evidence-backed
design choice in this whole course (research/04 §2, TaRL). A class can have several
placement groups running the same lesson template at different levels simultaneously.

## Time per day

Aim for one 20–35 minute lesson (Levels 1–4) or one 30–45 minute session (Levels 5–7), most
days. The strongest evidence-backed lever for older struggling readers isn't session length or
frequency — it's **group size**: Wanzek et al.'s (2013) synthesis of reading interventions for
grades 4–12 found group size was a significant moderator of comprehension outcomes, with the
smallest groups (1:1 or very small group) showing the largest effects, and that more calendar
weeks alone did not help without matched intensity/small group size (`research/03` §5). A
single learner working alone or with one tutor already has this advantage built in — you don't
need a classroom of one to get it. Short, frequent sessions are still good general practice for
keeping momentum and are recommended here for that reason, but that specific claim isn't what
Wanzek et al. tested — group size is the cited finding, not scheduling frequency.

## What each lesson looks like (Levels 1–4)

Every lesson follows the same 9-step shape, so nobody has to relearn "how the course works"
each time:

1. Warm-up review (2–3 min)
2. Hear it — oral sound work, then the letter (2 min)
3. Meet it — explicit teaching of the new sound/letter (3 min)
4. Blend it — read a word list, real + made-up words (5 min)
5. Spell it — write from dictation (4 min)
6. Heart word(s) — the small number of words that don't fully follow the rules yet (2 min)
7. Read it — a short decodable text, in your track (Track A or Track B) (5–8 min)
8. Listen & Talk — a passage read aloud to you, above your own reading level, with 1–2 new
   words and a couple of questions (5 min)
9. Check — a short quiz; 9/10 or better and you move on (2 min)

Levels 5–7 use a slightly longer session template — see the Level 5 README when you get
there.

## Track A / Track B — same skill, different content

From Level 1 onward, every reading passage exists in two versions covering the exact same
letters and patterns:

- **Track A (child):** stories, animals, school, family.
- **Track B (teen/adult):** work, money, family life, health, using the phone, the market,
  the bus, the news. Nothing childish — a 16-year-old or a 50-year-old reading Track B will
  never hit a sentence about a talking rabbit.

Pick whichever track fits you (or the person you're teaching) and stay on it. You can switch
if your circumstances change.

## Pause-safe rules

- There is no penalty for stopping and coming back later, no matter how long the gap.
- There is no "streak" to lose and no shame in re-doing a mastery check.
- "I think I already know this" → take the mastery check for that lesson first; pass it, skip
  ahead; don't pass it, do the lesson.
- Progress is tracked by what you can demonstrate, not by how many days in a row you showed up.

This is a deliberate design choice, not an oversight: adult learners in particular have
irregular attendance for real-life reasons (work, childcare, shame about returning after a
gap), and a course that punishes that gets abandoned. (research/04 §5, rule 6.)
