# Level 1 — First Sounds

Pre-A1. For a learner who cannot yet reliably read a single letter, up to full CVC
(consonant-vowel-consonant) real-word and pseudoword reading/spelling at ≥90% accuracy.

## Who this is for

- A 5-year-old who knows no letters yet.
- A teenager or adult (native English speaker or Urdu-first ESL learner) who never learned
  to decode, or who guesses words from shape/context and needs to rebuild from the ground up.
- Anyone placed here by the Level 0 placement test (accuracy dropped below 90% at the very
  first checkpoint — letter-sounds/CVC).

This level does **not** assume the learner is a child. Every lesson from L1.02 onward gives a
**Track A (child)** and a **Track B (teen/adult)** decodable text built from the *identical*
letters and heart words — Track B never uses baby-talk, animals-as-pets-for-a-child framing,
or toy vocabulary. Track A and B necessarily look similar in the earliest lessons (L1.02–L1.04)
because only 3–7 letters exist to build words from — this is a real constraint of decodability,
not a shortcut; the tracks diverge more as the letter pool grows (see each lesson's
Decodability Audit for an honest accounting of what was/wasn't possible that day).

## Entry criteria

- Placement test (Level 0) puts the learner here, **or**
- Learner cannot read any of: a, s, t, p, i, n as single letter-sounds, **or**
- Learner is a complete non-reader / pre-literate adult, **or**
- Learner has some letter knowledge but cannot blend three sounds into a word.

## Exit criteria

- Reads AND spells real CVC words (e.g., "sat," "dog," "kid") at **≥90% accuracy**.
- Reads AND spells CVC **pseudowords** ("nonsense/alien words," e.g., "fom," "zid") at
  **≥90% accuracy** — this is the proof the skill is decoding, not memorized shapes.
- Knows all 26 letter-sounds (not just names) plus ck, ff, ll, ss, zz, qu, and the -s plural.
- Correctly identifies all 24 Level 1 heart words in and out of context (§ below), naming the
  regular part and the "heart" (irregular) part of each.
- Can distinguish letter **name** from letter **sound** on request (e.g., "the letter is called
  'bee', it says /b/").
- Reliably tells apart b/d/p/q (the four-way reversal-confusable set), on paper and in text.
- If any of the above is below 90%: use the reteach map in `mastery-check.md`, not the Level 2
  entry gate.

## Master sequence (14 lessons + mastery check)

**Pace note (Round 1 builder, post-critique):** every lesson that introduces new letter-sounds
is now split into **sittings** — one new sound (or grapheme) per ~10–20 minute sitting, plus a
final sitting that combines them with Read it/Check. This matches the research/01 §5 rule 2
cadence of roughly 3–5 new GPCs a week, mastery-gated rather than calendar-gated, and is a
closer match to UFLI's own one-sound-per-instructional-block pace than the previous single-
session-per-lesson design was (see `GAUNTLET.md` Round 1). Lessons with no new sounds (L1.10,
L1.13) stay single-session, since the pace constraint doesn't apply to them.

**Fast track (adults/older learners):** every multi-sitting lesson carries this note — if a
sitting's mini check is passed at ≥90%, combine two or more sittings into one longer session
instead of spreading them across days. One-sound-per-sitting is the default, recommended path,
not the only path; a confident adult clearing checks quickly can do a whole 2–3-sitting lesson
in one sensible session.

By the end of L1.13 every one of the 26 letters, the four doubled-consonant spellings

have been introduced. That is the complete word-attack toolkit needed for the mastery check.

## Heart-word schedule (≤2 per lesson, cumulative from L1.02)

a · I · the · is · to · of · and · was · said · you · are · he · she · we · me · be · do ·
what · they · one · have · go · no · so

Each lesson's "Heart word(s)" section marks exactly which letters in that day's two words are
**regular** (sound as expected, or were already taught) versus the **heart** part — irregular,
or spelled with a grapheme this course hasn't formally taught yet (its formal lesson is named
so the tutor/learner knows it's coming, not being skipped). A few of these words — "and," "me,"
"be" — turn out to be fully decodable the moment they're introduced; they stay on the heart-word
list anyway because DESIGN.md's master sequence places them there and because treating a handful
of very-high-frequency words as "heart words we happen to already have the code for" costs
nothing and builds confidence.

## Multisensory and pacing

All tapping/tracing/air-writing in every lesson is **optional** (DESIGN.md rule 11) — call it
out, never require it. Pace is mastery-gated, not calendar-gated (rule 4): if a learner needs
extra sittings or extra sessions on one sitting, that is normal, not a failure; nothing is
timed against a clock except each sitting's ~10–20 minute estimate for planning purposes.

## Time

Each **sitting** runs ~10–20 minutes and introduces at most one new sound/grapheme (see the
Master sequence table above); a lesson with 3 new sounds is 4 sittings (3 single-sound + 1
combined), not one long session. At the default pace this is roughly **3–5 new sounds a week**
(research/01 §5 rule 2), close to UFLI's own cadence and slower than an earlier draft of this
course, which compressed 2–3 new sounds into a single sitting (flagged and fixed per
`GAUNTLET.md` Round 1). **Fast track:** an adult or older learner clearing each sitting's mini
check at ≥90% may combine sittings into fewer, longer sessions — the total instructional
content is the same either way, only the grouping changes. Across the whole level: **41
sittings** (summing the table above), delivered at whatever pace the
learner needs (one sitting a day, several a day, one a week — all are fine; see DESIGN.md
rule 10, pause-safe).

## Language-Experience option (adult/older beginners)

Any lesson from L1.04 onward, once "and"/"me"/"be"-level decoding exists, **or** right after
L1.01's oral work: ask an adult or older learner to say one true sentence about their own day
("I sat. Dad had a job.") using only words they can already say aloud (not read). The tutor
writes it down exactly as said. Read it back together, pointing at each word. This is not a
replacement for the decodable texts in section 7 of each lesson (LEA doesn't teach the code,
research/02 §1.9) — it is a five-minute add-on, best used when an adult says a lesson's Track B
text feels too constrained or impersonal. Never force it; offer it once per week of use.

## Urdu-speaker tips in Level 1

Level 1's phoneme set is mostly Urdu-transferable (see `research/04` §6), so most Urdu-L1
tips are deferred to L1.07 (short e/u, since Urdu's short-vowel inventory is smaller than
English's) and L1.11 (v/w, since Urdu و covers both). L1.13's review is a natural point to
also flag b/d/p/q as a *visual* mirror-confusion issue distinct from the v/w *auditory* one.
