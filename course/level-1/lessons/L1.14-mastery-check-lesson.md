# L1.14 — Level 1 Mastery Check

**Goal:** Confirm the learner reads and spells real CVC words and pseudowords at ≥90%, knows
all 26 letter-sounds, all 24 heart words, and reliably tells apart b/d/p/q — the Level 1 exit
gate.
**New today:** nothing new — this session administers the full instrument.
**Review:** everything, L1.01–L1.13.
**Heart words:** cumulative review only (all 24, no new ones).
**Time:** 20–30 minutes, can be split across two short sessions if needed (this is a test
session, not a teaching one — fatigue affects the score, so don't push through if the learner
is visibly tired).
**Materials:** paper, pencil; the full instrument (word lists, dictation sentences, scoring
sheet, and reteach map) lives in `../mastery-check.md` — **use that file to actually run this
session**; this lesson page is the short-form overview and the "what happens next" logic.

## 1–6. (Not applicable — this is a test session, not a teaching session)

Skip the usual warm-up/hear-it/meet-it/blend-it/spell-it/heart-word structure. Go straight to
administering the instrument in `mastery-check.md`, which covers, in order:
1. Letter-sound check (all 26 + ck, FLOSS, qu) — say the sound, not the name.
2. Real-word reading list (20 words spanning every lesson's new sounds).
3. Pseudoword reading list (10 "alien words").
4. Heart-word check (all 24, read in and out of context).
5. Dictation (words + one full sentence).
6. b/d/p/q discrimination check (both reading and writing).

## 7. Read it

**Use the two placement-style passages in `mastery-check.md` §5 (one per track) as the final "can you read connected text built entirely from what you've learned" check — this is the closest thing to a real decodable "off-ramp preview" the learner will do in Level 1, and it's worth treating as a small celebration regardless of the score, since simply attempting a whole paragraph is new.**

## 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)

> **You Did Something Real**
> Fourteen lessons ago, this course started with three sounds and a handful of made-up "alien
> words." Today's check uses every single letter in English, a spelling rule, a plural ending,
> and twenty-four words that don't even follow the normal rules — and asks for all of it back,
> cold, without any warm-up hints. That's not a small ask. Whatever the score turns out to be,
> getting through fourteen full lessons of new material, in order, each one building on the
> last, is real, durable progress — the kind that doesn't disappear the way a memorized page
> would. What comes next, in Level 2, isn't starting over; it's the exact same skill, aimed at
> bigger and bigger words.

**Tier-2 words:**
- **durable** — lasting a long time without wearing out or disappearing. "A durable skill
  doesn't fade the way a memorized fact does." "These boots are durable enough for years of
  work." Your turn: name something in your life that's durable.
- **cold** *(as used above: "asks for all of it back, cold")* — done without any warm-up,
  hints, or preparation right beforehand. "She walked into the test cold, with no review."
  "He can recite the poem cold, no notes." Your turn: is there something you can do "cold,"
  with zero warm-up?

**Discussion questions:**
1. What does the passage say is different about a "durable" skill compared to a memorized
   page?
2. How does the passage describe what Level 2 will be, compared to Level 1?

## 9. Check

This entire session *is* the check — see `mastery-check.md` for the full scoring rubric,
the ≥90% threshold applied per component (not just overall), and the exact reteach map for
each component that comes in under threshold. **Do not average across components to reach
90%** — decoding and comprehension (and, within decoding, real-word vs. pseudoword accuracy)
are tracked and gated separately, per DESIGN.md rule 8 (two metrics, never conflated).

---

### Tutor notes

- **This is the one lesson in the course where "give a hint" is actively wrong** — a mastery
  check measures what's automatic without support; hinting inflates the score and sends a
  learner into Level 2 under-prepared.
- **Never** frame a below-threshold result as failure — frame it exactly as the reteach map in
  `mastery-check.md` frames it: a precise, short, targeted return to one specific lesson, not
  a restart of the whole level.
- **For adults:** say explicitly that this is not a school exam with a permanent record — it's
  a private diagnostic that only decides which lesson happens next.
- **For young kids:** keep the tone light and matter-of-fact; a stopwatch or exam-hall
  atmosphere is not appropriate for a 20-minute checklist.

Not applicable in the usual sense — this lesson's "text" is the mastery instrument itself,
