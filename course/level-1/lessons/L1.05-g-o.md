# L1.05 — g o

**Goal:** Read and spell g, o; unlock a full vocabulary of common CVC words (got, dog, mop,
top…) and the first fully regular heart word, "and."
**New today:** letters g, o (sounds /g/, /ɒ/ — short o) — **one new sound per sitting**.
**Review:** s, a, t, p, i, n, m, d.
**Heart words:** and, was (taught in the final combined sitting).
**Time:** three sittings — A (~15 min), B (~15 min), C (~25–30 min).
**Materials:** paper, pencil.

**Fast track (adults/older learners):** if a sitting's mini check is passed at ≥90%, combine
sittings into one session. The one-sound-per-sitting pace is the default, recommended path
(research/01 §5 rule 2), not the only path.

---

## Sitting A — g (~15 min)

**Warm-up:** flash m, d (yesterday's letters). Read 3 review words: mad, dip, sad.

**Hear it:** /g/ — back of the tongue taps the soft palate, voiced (like /k/'s "voiced twin").
Like the start of "got."

**Meet it:** "This says /g/" (g). **Mouth cue:** made at the very back of the mouth — put a
finger gently on the throat and feel the buzz, further back than /d/'s tap.

**Blend it (with known sounds only — s, a, t, p, i, n, m, d + today's g):** gap, tag, nag, sag,
gas.

**Spell it:** say /g/ → write **g**. Segment and write "gap."

**Mini check:** g on sight 5/5; blend the 5 words above at ≥4/5.

## Sitting B — o (~15 min)

**Warm-up:** flash g. Read 3 review words: gap, tag, sag.

**Hear it:** /o/ — short, round, open mouth, like the start of "on."

**Meet it:** "This says /o/" (o). **Mouth cue:** rounds the lips slightly, unlike /a/'s
flat-open shape — contrast "at" vs. "ot" side by side.

**Blend it (with known sounds only — s, a, t, p, i, n, m, d, g + today's o):** got, dog, top,
pot, nod.

**Spell it:** say /o/ → write **o**. Segment and write "dog."

**Mini check:** o on sight 5/5; blend the 5 words above at ≥4/5.

## Sitting C — g, o together: heart words, Read it, Check (~25–30 min)

**Warm-up review (2–3 min):** flash g, o, and 5 mixed review words: mad, dip, sad, pit, sat.

**Blend it — full cumulative list (5 min):**

**Real words (15, cumulative):** got, dog, log, mop, top, pot, dot, gap, tag, nag, sag, gas,
mad, dip, sat
**Pseudowords (5):** dop, gid, pog, mig, sog

**Spell it (4 min):**
1. Say /g/, /o/ → write **g**, **o**.
2. Segment and write: "got," "dog."
3. Sentence dictation: **"Sam got a dog."** (Sam: /s/ /a/ /m/; got: /g/ /o/ /t/; a: heart
   word.)

**Heart word(s) (2 min):**
- **and** — a (✓), n (✓, L1.03), d (✓, L1.04) — this is the one word DESIGN's own schedule
  flags as **fully decodable, not actually irregular** at all: blend it exactly like any other
  word, /a/ /n/ /d/ → "and." It stays on the "heart word" list purely because it's extremely
  high-frequency, not because any part of it is irregular. Say this explicitly: "this one's a
  gift — nothing to memorize, just blend it."
- **was** — w hasn't been taught yet (that's L1.11); even once it is, the vowel here says
  /ɒ/ instead of the /æ/ you'd expect from "wa-", and the final s says /z/. **Mark the whole
  word as heart for now.** Revisit fully once w is taught.

### 7. Read it

**Track A (child — a new pet):**
> Sam got a dog. The dog sat. Sam pat the dog. The dog is mad. Is the dog sad? The dog sat
> and sat.

**Track B (adult — a delivery route):**
*(Tutor context, spoken not decoded: "Dan and Pam are planning tomorrow's delivery route.")*
> Dan got a map. Pam got a pot. Dan and Pam sat. Is Dan mad? Is Pam mad? Dan and Pam got a tip.

**Questions (2 literal, 1 think):**
1. What did the main character get at the start?
2. What word tells you two people or things were joined together in the last sentence?
3. Think: in Track B, what do you think "got a tip" means here — money, or something else?

### 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)

> **A Dog for the Whole Street**
> When old Mrs. Chaudhry got too tired to walk her dog Bruno every morning, the neighbors
> worried he'd be stuck inside all day. Instead, something wonderful happened: a rotation
> started. On Mondays, the twins next door took Bruno to the park. On Tuesdays, it was the
> teenager from the corner house, who said walking Bruno was the only exercise he actually
> enjoyed. By the weekend, half the street had a turn. Bruno, delighted, seemed to know
> exactly whose door to wait outside each morning, tail thumping the second the right person
> stepped out.

**Tier-2 words:**
- **rotation** — a system where people take turns doing something, one after another in order.
  "We set up a rotation for who cooks dinner each night." "The nurses work on a rotation, so
  someone is always on duty." Your turn: is there a rotation in your house or work?
- **delighted** — extremely happy and pleased about something. "She was delighted to see her
  old friend." "He was delighted when the rain finally stopped." Your turn: tell me about a
  time something delighted you.

**Discussion questions:**
1. What problem did the street solve, and how?
2. How did Bruno seem to feel about the new arrangement — what in the story tells you that?

### 9. Check (2 min)

- **Real (5):** got, top, gas, nag, dot
- **Pseudo (5):** dop, gid, pog, mig, sog
- **Dictated (1):** "log"

**≥10/11 → move to L1.06.** Below that: re-teach /g/ vs /d/ if those two are being confused
(both are stop sounds close in the mouth) with a minimal-pair drill (dot/got, dog/gad-style
pairs) before re-checking.

---

### Tutor notes

- **Common error: /g/ read as /d/ or vice versa** (both are "stop" consonants and can blur for
  some learners). Correction: feel the throat buzz further back for /g/ (near the back of the
  jaw) vs. the tap just behind the teeth for /d/.
- **Common error: o read as short u** (some accents/L1 backgrounds pull /ɒ/ toward /ʌ/).
  Correction: exaggerate the lip-rounding on /o/ and contrast with the flat-mouthed /a/ they
  already know.
- **Never** let "and" be treated as a memorize-the-shape heart word just because it's on the
  list — it is genuinely decodable now; always ask for the blend (DESIGN.md rule 5's spirit:
  even "heart" words should be decoded wherever the code allows it).
- **For adults:** "got a tip" in Track B is a nice moment to ask directly whether they've ever
  worked a tipped job — a natural LEA opening if they want to say more.
- **For young kids:** if a real pet is in the household, swap "the dog" for the pet's actual
  name if it's spellable with taught letters (or approximate) — personalizing decodable text is
  encouraged as long as decodability holds.
