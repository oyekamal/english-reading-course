# L1.04 — m d

**Goal:** Read and spell m, d; blend an 8-letter set into a richer bank of real CVC words,
including the first simple family/scene sentences.
**New today:** letters m, d (sounds /m/, /d/) — **one new sound per sitting**.
**Review:** s, a, t, p, i, n.
**Heart words:** to, of (taught in the final combined sitting).
**Time:** three sittings — A (~15 min), B (~15 min), C (~25–30 min).
**Materials:** paper, pencil.

**Fast track (adults/older learners):** if a sitting's mini check is passed at ≥90%, combine
sittings into one session. The one-sound-per-sitting pace is the default, recommended path
(research/01 §5 rule 2), not the only path.

---

## Sitting A — m (~15 min)

**Warm-up:** flash s, a, t, p, i, n. Read 3 review words: sat, pit, tan.

**Hear it:** /m/ — lips pressed together, sound hums out through the nose (like /n/, but lips
closed instead of tongue on the ridge). Like the start of "map."

**Meet it:** "This says /m/" (m). **Mouth cue:** feel the hum on closed lips — the same
nose-buzz feeling as /n/, but with the mouth shut instead of open. **Confusable pair to watch:**
/m/ and /n/ are both nasal hums; the difference is lips-closed (m) vs. tongue-on-ridge (n).

**Blend it (with known sounds only — s, a, t, p, i, n + today's m):** am, Sam, map, mat, man.

**Spell it:** say /m/ → write **m**. Segment and write "map."

**Mini check:** m on sight 5/5; blend the 5 words above at ≥4/5.

## Sitting B — d (~15 min)

**Warm-up:** flash m. Read 3 review words: am, map, man.

**Hear it:** /d/ — tongue taps the same ridge as /t/, but the voice buzzes ("d" is /t/'s
"voiced twin"). Like the start of "dog."

**Meet it:** "This says /d/" (d). **Mouth cue:** put a hand on the throat for /d/ — feel the
buzz that /t/ doesn't have.

**Blend it (with known sounds only — s, a, t, p, i, n, m + today's d):** mad, dad, dip, dam,
sad.

**Spell it:** say /d/ → write **d**. Segment and write "mad."

**Mini check:** d on sight 5/5; blend the 5 words above at ≥4/5.

## Sitting C — m, d together: heart words, Read it, Check (~25–30 min)

**Warm-up review (2–3 min):** flash m, d, and 5 mixed review words: sat, mad, pit, dad, nap.

**Blend it — full cumulative list (5 min):**

**Real words (15, cumulative):** sat, pit, sip, tan, nap, it, mad, man, map, mat, dad, dip,
dim, dam, sad
**Pseudowords (5):** mip, nad, dap, tid, nam

**Spell it (4 min):**
1. Say /m/, /d/ → write **m**, **d**.
2. Segment and write: "mad," "dip."
3. Sentence dictation: **"Sam is sad."** (Sam: /s/ /a/ /m/; is: heart word; sad: /s/ /a/ /d/.)

**Heart word(s) (2 min):**
- **to** — t is regular (✓, taught). The **o** hasn't been formally taught yet (that's next
  lesson, L1.05) *and* even once it is, "to" doesn't use the short-o sound you'll learn there —
  it says a long /uː/. **Heart part: the whole "o."** Note aloud: "the letter o gets its own
  lesson tomorrow — this is a sneak preview of just one of its jobs."
- **of** — the trickiest heart word so far: o isn't taught yet, and **f says /v/ here, not
  /f/** — the only English word where "f" does that. Mark the entire word as heart for now;
  revisit once f is formally taught (L1.09) to show how unusual this one word really is.

### 7. Read it

**Track A (child — a small family scene):**
> Sam sat. Dad sat. Sam is sad. Dad is mad at Sam. Sam pat Dad. Is Dad sad? Dad is sad.

**Track B (adult — a small shop, after a missed delivery):**
*(Tutor context, spoken not decoded: "Pam and Dan run a small shop. A delivery didn't show up
today.")*
> Pam sat. Dan sat. Pam is sad. Dan is mad at Pam. Pam pat Dan. Is Dan sad? Dan is sad.

A note on the grammar: "Sam pat Dad" (not "Sam pats Dad") is intentional, not a typo — the -s
verb ending isn't taught until L1.10. Genuine decodable texts at this stage read a little
stiffly for exactly this reason; that's an honest, temporary feature, not sloppy writing (see
Decodability Audit).

**Questions (2 literal, 1 think):**
1. Who was mad, and who were they mad at?
2. What did the sad/mad character do at the end?
3. Think: why do you think Dad/Dan was mad — what might have happened just before this story
   starts?

### 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)

> **The Missed Delivery**
> Every Tuesday, a truck brings fresh bread to Mr. Iqbal's small shop before sunrise. He
> counts on it — his whole morning depends on those loaves being on the shelf before the first
> customers arrive. But this Tuesday, seven o'clock came and went, and the truck never
> appeared. Mr. Iqbal called the bakery three times, growing more frustrated with each
> unanswered ring. Finally, his daughter suggested checking the neighborhood group chat — and
> there it was: the delivery driver's van had a flat tire two streets away. Mr. Iqbal grabbed
> his bicycle pump without a second thought and pedaled over to help.

**Tier-2 words:**
- **frustrated** — annoyed and a little helpless because something isn't working the way you
  need it to. "She felt frustrated when the bus was late again." "He was frustrated trying to
  fix the broken lock." Your turn: what's something that frustrates you?
- **depends on** — needs something in order to work or succeed. "The picnic depends on good
  weather." "My whole morning depends on a strong cup of tea." Your turn: what does your
  morning depend on?

**Discussion questions:**
1. What problem did Mr. Iqbal have, and how did he find out what caused it?
2. What does it tell you about Mr. Iqbal that he grabbed his bicycle pump to go help?

### 9. Check (2 min)

- **Real (5):** mad, dip, sad, tan, pit
- **Pseudo (5):** mip, nad, dap, tid, nam
- **Dictated (1):** "dam"

**≥10/11 → move to L1.05.** Below that: isolate whichever of m/d is weaker with a 2-minute
mini-drill (word pairs like "mad/dad," "man/dan") before re-testing.

### Tutor notes

- **Common error: m/n mix-up**, especially for learners whose first language doesn't
  distinguish these as sharply. Correction: hand on lips for m (closed), hand under chin/on
  tongue-ridge feeling for n (tongue up) — a quick physical check, not a guess.
- **Common error: writing "b" for "d" or vice versa.** This is completely normal at this age/
  stage and is not corrected by explanation yet — a dedicated b/d/p/q lesson is coming at
  L1.13; for now, just gently re-model the correct shape without dwelling on it.
- **Never** accept "it looks like it starts with an M so it's probably..." as a strategy — full
  blend, every time (DESIGN.md rule 1).
- **For adults:** the shop/delivery Track B works well to open a short real conversation about
  a similar frustration from their own week — a natural, low-pressure LEA-style bridge (see
  README's Language-Experience option) if the learner wants to try it.
- **For young kids:** "Sam pat Dad" reads oddly to an adult ear but is genuinely normal/expected
  phrasing in this style of book — don't over-correct the grammar; the goal today is decoding,
  not syntax polish.
