# L1.03 — p i n

**Goal:** Read and spell p, i, n; blend the growing letter set into a full first wave of real
CVC words.
**New today:** letters p, i, n (sounds /p/, /ɪ/, /n/) — **one new sound per sitting**.
**Review:** s, a, t; the words at, as, sat.
**Heart words:** the, is (taught in the final combined sitting).
**Time:** four sittings — A (~15 min), B (~15 min), C (~15 min), D (~25–30 min).
**Materials:** paper, pencil; optional letter tiles.

**Fast track (adults/older learners):** if a sitting's mini check is passed at ≥90%, combine
two or more sittings into one session. The one-sound-per-sitting pace is the default,
recommended path (research/01 §5 rule 2), not the only path.

---

## Sitting A — p (~15 min)

**Warm-up:** flash s, a, t. Read 3 review words: at, sat, as.

**Hear it:** /p/ — lips pop apart, a tiny puff of air, like the start of "pat."

**Meet it:** "This says /p/" (p). **Mouth cue:** a stop-and-release with the lips; feel the
tiny puff of air on the back of your hand.

**Blend it (with known sounds only — s, a, t + today's p):** pat, tap, sap, at, sat.

**Spell it:** say /p/ → write **p**. Segment and write "tap."

**Mini check:** p on sight 5/5; blend the 5 words above at ≥4/5.

## Sitting B — i (~15 min)

**Warm-up:** flash p. Read 3 review words: pat, tap, sap.

**Hear it:** /i/ — short, wide smile shape, tongue high and forward, like the start of "it."

**Meet it:** "This says /i/" (i). **Mouth cue:** keeps the mouth almost smiling.

**Blend it (with known sounds only — s, a, t, p + today's i):** pit, tip, sip, sit, pip.

**Spell it:** say /i/ → write **i**. Segment and write "sit."

**Mini check:** i on sight 5/5; blend the 5 words above at ≥4/5.

## Sitting C — n (~15 min)

**Warm-up:** flash i. Read 3 review words: pit, sip, tip.

**Hear it:** /n/ — tongue behind top teeth, sound comes through the nose, like the start of
"net."

**Meet it:** "This says /n/" (n). **Mouth cue:** the first sound so far that hums through the
nose — put a finger lightly on the nose and feel it buzz.

**Blend it (all six letters now known):** pin, nip, tan, pan, nap, in.

**Spell it:** say /n/ → write **n**. Segment and write "pin," "in."

**Mini check:** n on sight 5/5; blend the 6 words above at ≥5/6.

## Sitting D — p, i, n together: heart words, Read it, Check (~25–30 min)

**Warm-up review (2–3 min):** flash all six letters (s, a, t, p, i, n) in mixed order; blend
5 words spanning both older and newer sounds: sat, pit, nap, tan, pin.

**Blend it — full cumulative list (5 min):**

**Real words (15, cumulative):** at, sat, in, pit, pin, nip, sip, sit, pat, tap, tan, pan, nap,
tip, pip
**Pseudowords / "alien words" (5):** tas, nas, pas, tis, nis

**Spell it (4 min):**
1. Say /p/ → write **p**. Say /i/ → write **i**. Say /n/ → write **n**.
2. Segment and write: "pin," "sit."
3. Sentence dictation: **"Tip is in."** — segment each word (Tip: /t/ /i/ /p/; is: heart word,
   map i + the "s says /z/" heart part; in: /i/ /n/), then write the whole sentence.

**Heart word(s) (2 min):**
- **the** — none of its letters have a formal lesson yet that matches how it sounds here (the
  "th" digraph isn't taught until Level 2.3, and even the "e" isn't formally taught until
  L1.07). Treat the whole word as one memorized heart shape for now: "the." Point out that it's
  one of the most common words in English, which is exactly why it's worth memorizing early.
- **is** — i (✓) + s. The letter s says /s/ everywhere else so far, but in "is" it actually
  says /z/. **Heart part: the s says /z/, not /s/.** The i is fully regular.

### 7. Read it

**Track A (child — a sandpit at the playground):**
> Tip sat. Pat sat. Tip sat in the pit. Pat sat in the pit. Is Tip in? Is Pat in? Tip is in!
> Pat is in!

**Track B (adult — a pit crew at a racetrack):**
*(Tutor context, said aloud but not part of the decodable text itself: "Pat and Nat work
together on a pit crew.")*
> Pat sat. Nat sat. Pat is in the pit. Nat is in the pit. Is Pat in? Is Nat in? Pat is in.
> Nat is in.

**Questions (2 literal, 1 think):**
1. Who sat down first in the text?
2. Where did they end up — in or out of the pit?
3. Think: why might someone need to go "in the pit" — what job could that be, in Track B's
   world?

### 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)

> **The Pit Stop**
> At the big race, cars zoom around the track faster than the eye can follow. But every few
> laps, a driver has to pull into a special lane called the pit. There, a whole team is
> waiting — not to rest, but to work at incredible speed. In under ten seconds, they can
> change all four tires, clean the windshield, and send the car roaring back onto the track.
> Everyone has one exact job, and they practice it over and over so nothing goes wrong when
> it really counts. A single second lost in the pit can cost a driver the whole race.

**Tier-2 words:**
- **incredible** — so surprising or impressive that it's hard to believe. "That was an
  incredible jump!" "She has an incredible memory for names." Your turn: tell me about
  something you find incredible.
- **exact** — precise, correct down to the smallest detail, not approximate. "He gave the
  exact time the train would arrive." "I need the exact number, not a guess." Your turn: when
  do you need to be exact instead of just close?

**Discussion questions:**
1. Why does the team practice their jobs "over and over," according to the passage?
2. What do you think would happen if the pit crew were slow?

### 9. Check (2 min)

- **Real (5):** pit, sap, nap, sit, tan
- **Pseudo (5):** tas, nas, pas, tis, nis
- **Dictated (1):** "pin"

**≥10/11 (≈90%) → move to L1.04.** Below that: reteach Sitting C's blend list (just words
containing today's newest letter, n) before mixing back in review words, then re-check.

### Tutor notes

- **Common error: reversing i and n order when writing "in"/"ni".** Correction: segment aloud
  first (say "in" slowly — which sound comes first, /i/ or /n/?) before writing, every time,
  rather than writing from memory of the shape.
- **Common error: confusing /i/ and /e/ sounds** (e isn't taught until L1.07, so this shouldn't
  arise yet, but some learners import a vowel sound from another language). If it happens,
  just re-model the /i/ sound cleanly — do not explain e yet.
- Never accept a guessed word based on the first letter alone — always require the full
  blend (DESIGN.md rule 1).
- **For adults:** the racetrack pit-crew framing works well as an analogy for "practice until
  it's automatic," which is literally the mechanism this whole course is built on — feel free
  to say so explicitly.
- **For young kids:** "alien words" (tas, nis, etc.) can be said in a silly alien voice — this
  is a fine, optional way to keep it playful without turning it into cueing/guessing.

### Decodability audit

All words in Sitting D's Read-it texts, checked against letters taught through L1.03 (s, a, t,
p, i, n) and this lesson's heart words (the, is):
- **Tip / Pat / sat / in / pit / Nat** — every letter taught (s,a,t,p,i,n). Capitalized proper
  names (Tip, Pat, Nat) use only taught letter-sounds; capitalization itself is not a
  decoding issue, just a print convention noted in passing. "Nat" is used only as a character
  name here — it was deliberately removed from this lesson's pseudoword list (now "nis"
  instead) so it never appears as both a nonsense string and a real name in the same lesson.
- **the** — heart word, introduced this lesson.
- **is** — heart word, introduced this lesson; regular i + heart /z/-sound s.
100% of the Read-it text is taught letters or this lesson's heart words. The tutor's one-line
scene-setting context for Track B ("Pat and Nat work together on a pit crew") is explicitly
*not* part of the learner's decodable text — it is spoken by the tutor, not read by the
learner — and is excluded from this audit for that reason.
