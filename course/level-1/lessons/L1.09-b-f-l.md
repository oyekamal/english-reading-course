# L1.09 — b f l

**Goal:** Read and spell b, f, l; add two more fully decodable "heart" words (me, be) now that
b is taught.
**New today:** letters b, f, l (sounds /b/, /f/, /l/) — **one new sound per sitting**.
**Review:** r, h; words rat, hop, hat.
**Heart words:** me, be (taught in the final combined sitting).
**Time:** four sittings — A (~12 min), B (~12 min), C (~12 min), D (~25–30 min).
**Materials:** paper, pencil.

**Fast track (adults/older learners):** if a sitting's mini check is passed at ≥90%, combine
sittings into one session. The one-sound-per-sitting pace is the default, recommended path
(research/01 §5 rule 2), not the only path.

---

## Sitting A — b (~12 min)

**Warm-up:** flash r, h. Read 3 review words: rat, hop, hat.

**Hear it:** /b/ — lips press together then pop apart with voice buzzing (b is /p/'s "voiced
twin"), like the start of "bat."

**Meet it:** "This says /b/" (b). **Mouth cue:** contrast b and p — say "pat" then "bat,"
feeling the buzz on b that's missing on p.

**Blend it (with known sounds only — s, a, t, p, i, n, m, d, g, o, c, k, ck, e, u, r, h + today's
b):** bat, big, bit, bag, bed.

**Spell it:** say /b/ → write **b**. Segment and write "big."

**Mini check:** b on sight 5/5; blend the 5 words above at ≥4/5.

## Sitting B — f (~12 min)

**Warm-up:** flash b. Read 3 review words: bat, big, bag.

**Hear it:** /f/ — top teeth rest gently on the bottom lip, air hisses through, no buzz, like
the start of "fan."

**Meet it:** "This says /f/" (f). **Mouth cue:** show the teeth-on-lip position clearly.

**Blend it (with known sounds only — all prior + today's f):** fan, fat, fig, fun, fog.

**Spell it:** say /f/ → write **f**. Segment and write "fan."

**Mini check:** f on sight 5/5; blend the 5 words above at ≥4/5.

## Sitting C — l (~12 min)

**Warm-up:** flash f. Read 3 review words: fan, fat, fig.

**Hear it:** /l/ — tongue tip touches right behind the top front teeth, sound flows around the
sides of the tongue, like the start of "leg."

**Meet it:** "This says /l/" (l). **Mouth cue:** the tongue tip stays up the whole time the
sound continues — unlike every consonant so far, you can "hold" /l/ as long as you have breath.

**Blend it (with known sounds only — all prior + today's l):** leg, lap, lip, let, lid.

**Spell it:** say /l/ → write **l**. Segment and write "leg."

**Mini check:** l on sight 5/5; blend the 5 words above at ≥4/5.

## Sitting D — b, f, l together: heart words, Read it, Check (~25–30 min)

**Warm-up review (2–3 min):** flash b, f, l; blend 5 mixed words spanning all three: bat, fan,
leg, big, fig.

**Blend it — full cumulative list (5 min):**

**Real words (15, cumulative):** fan, fat, fig, leg, lap, lip, bat, big, bit, bag, bed, bud,
bun, lid, lock
**Pseudowords (5):** fon, lir, bof, lut, fim

**Spell it (4 min):**
1. Segment and write: "big," "fan."
2. Sentence dictation: **"Let me get the bag."** (Let: /l/ /e/ /t/; me: heart word, but fully
   decodable — see below; get: /g/ /e/ /t/; the: heart word; bag: /b/ /a/ /g/.)

**Heart word(s) (2 min):**
- **me** — m (✓) + e (✓). **Fully decodable, no heart part at all.** Like "and" back in L1.05,
  this one's a gift: blend it normally.
- **be** — b (✓, taught today) + e (✓). **Also fully decodable.** Two free ones in one lesson —
  point this out; it's a nice confidence boost after several lessons of genuinely irregular
  words.

### 7. Read it

**Track A (child — a bug in the bedroom):**
> Let me get the big bug! The bug is on the bed. The bug ran. Sam ran. Sam got the bug. Sam
> let the bug run.

**Track B (adult — a lost-and-found bag at the dock):**
*(Tutor context, spoken not decoded: "Dan finds a bag someone left behind at the loading
dock.")*
> Let me get the big bag. The bag is at the dock. Dan ran. Dan got the bag. Dan let Nat get the
> bag.

**Questions (2 literal, 1 think):**
1. What did the main character say at the very start ("Let me...")?
2. Who ran, and what did they end up doing with the bug/bag?
3. Think: in Track B, why do you think Dan let Nat have the bag instead of keeping it?

### 8. Listen & Talk (5 min)

> **Whose Bag Is It?**
> When a stranger's bag turns up with no name on it, most people feel a small pull in two
> directions at once — curiosity about what's inside, and a clear sense that opening it isn't
> quite right. Workplaces that handle a lot of lost property usually have a simple rule for
> this: log it, store it somewhere safe, and hold it for a set number of days before deciding
> what happens next. It sounds almost too basic to matter, but that small bit of structure is
> exactly what stops an honest mistake from turning into an argument later about who touched
> what, and when.

**Tier-2 words:**
- **curiosity** — a strong wish to know or learn something. "Her curiosity got the better of
  her and she peeked inside." "A good scientist starts with curiosity." Your turn: what makes
  you curious?
- **structure** — an organized way of doing something, with clear steps or rules, instead of
  handling things randomly. "The team added more structure to their morning routine." "Without
  structure, the project fell apart." Your turn: where in your day do you rely on structure?

**Discussion questions:**
1. What two feelings does the passage say people get when they find an unlabeled bag?
2. Why does having a simple rule (log it, store it, wait) help avoid arguments later?

### 9. Check (2 min)

- **Real (5):** fan, big, bag, lip, bed
- **Pseudo (5):** fon, lir, bof, lut, fim
- **Dictated (1):** "let"

**≥10/11 → move to L1.10.** Below that: check specifically whether b/p are being reversed in
either direction (common at this stage, not a comprehension problem) with a quick minimal-pair
sort (bat/pat, big/pig) before re-testing.

---

### Tutor notes

- **Common error: b/p reversal**, both in reading and in writing — completely typical at this
  age/stage and not yet formally addressed with the full b/d/p/q lesson (that's L1.13).
  Correction for now: feel the throat buzz on b, no buzz on p, as a quick physical check.
- **Common error: f produced without teeth-on-lip contact**, sometimes sounding closer to /p/
  or /h/. Correction: model the visible teeth-on-lip position and let the learner check their
  own reflection if a mirror is handy.
- **Never** skip straight to "it starts with a loop so it's probably..." — always blend fully.
- **For adults:** the lost-property story is a nice, low-stakes moment to talk about honesty
  norms at their own workplace, if they want to share — optional, not required.
- **For young kids:** "me" and "be" being fully decodable today is worth celebrating out loud —
  it's genuinely motivating to see the heart-word list shrink in real time.
