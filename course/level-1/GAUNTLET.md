### Blind comparison: our L1.05 (g, o) vs. UFLI Lesson 16 (g) + its decodable passage

UFLI reaches **g** at Lesson 16 of its Alphabet unit, having spent 15 full lessons (each run
across **two instructional days**, per the Pocket Reference: Day 1 = phonemic awareness →
new-concept teaching, Day 2 = review → word chains → irregular words → connected text) getting
there — one new sound at a time, with "practice" consolidation lessons folded in at L5 and L10.
Its decodable for that single lesson, **"Gus the Dog"** (excerpt): *"Gus is a dog. Gus the dog is fun. …"* (full passage: UFLI Foundations decodable, © University of Florida Literacy Institute — not reproduced here) — six sentences, a repeated character, an exclamation,
and embedded dialogue, at a point where the learner has had 15 lessons (≈30 instructional days)
of runway.

Our L1.05 teaches **g and o together, in one 20–25 minute sitting**, as the level's 5th lesson.
Track A: *"Sam got a dog. The dog sat. Sam pat the dog. The dog is mad. Is the dog sad? The dog
sat and sat."* Sentence-for-sentence the craft is comparable (both repetitive, both age-neutral
in tone, both hit a question), and ours does something UFLI's sample never attempts — the same
scene rewritten for an adult register in Track B. But the run-up to get there is not comparable:
an expert would notice immediately that this course puts a *pair* of brand-new sounds where UFLI
would still be four lessons and two calendar days away from finishing just **one** of them. The
text quality is a wash; the pacing discipline behind it is not.

### Blind comparison: heart words — ours vs. UFLI's actual routine

UFLI's own Pocket Reference describes the mechanism almost exactly as this course implements it
in prose form: *"The blue square icons under the word represent a regular grapheme-phoneme
correspondence. The red hearts represent the irregular part that must be 'remembered by heart.'"*
That is precisely this course's "mark which part is regular, mark the heart part" convention in
every lesson's §6 — a genuine, faithful match to the gold-standard technique, just delivered in
text instead of a slide deck with physical heart icons. **No defect here; flagging as a place
this course correctly reproduces best practice**, worth keeping exactly as-is through Level 2–4.

One more point in this course's favor: UFLI's own public materials have **no discoverable
mastery-check or formal assessment instrument** — its only checkpoints are Step 8's one-sentence
dictation and an informal "illustrate the passage" comprehension gauge. This course's six-
component, separately-gated `mastery-check.md` (letter-sounds, real words, pseudowords, heart
words in/out of context, dictation, b/d/p/q) is more rigorous than what UFLI publishes,
independent of defect #2 below about that instrument's letter-count bug.

### VERDICT: **REFERENCE WINS** (this round)

Not because the underlying design is weak — the Track A/B adult-dignity split, the embedded
Listen & Talk/Tier-2 vocabulary in every single lesson, and the heart-word "map the irregular
part" routine are all genuine strengths that **no surveyed reference program has at this
level** (research/07 §3.1: "None of the free/public materials located address adult-specific
needs... all decodable texts found in these programs' public samples are child-content"). An
expert reading specialist choosing between L1.09 (b f l) and UFLI's equivalent lessons would
credit ours for the adult track and the built-in vocabulary strand.

But a vetted program (UFLI: EdReports "Meets Expectations"; Jolly Phonics: DfE-validated) does
not ship a lesson that teaches a word using a grapheme not due for another three whole levels,
does not ship a mastery gate that silently skips testing three of the sounds it claims to
certify, and does not model incorrect grammar in the same breath it teaches the correct rule.
Those are the kind of concrete, avoidable defects that decide a side-by-side, and this round has
three of them. Fix the worklist below and this flips to OURS WINS — the bones are genuinely
good.

### SINGLE BIGGEST GAP: pace — confirmed against primary sources, not just secondary summaries

Verified directly this round, not just cited secondhand: **UFLI's own toolbox scope-and-sequence
(lessons 1–34) introduces exactly one new letter-sound per lesson** (L1 a /ă/, L2 m, L3 s, L4 t,
L6 p, L7 f, L8 i, L9 n, ... one per lesson, with pure practice/consolidation lessons at L5, L10,
L19), **and each of those single-sound lessons runs across two full instructional days** (Day 1:
phonemic awareness → visual/auditory/blending drills → new-concept teaching; Day 2: review →
word-chain work → irregular words → connected text). At a 5-day week that is roughly **one new
sound per 2 days ≈ 2.5 new sounds/week** — even slower than the "3–6/week" band `research/01`
credits UFLI with citing secondhand; the primary source shows UFLI is more conservative than its
own secondary-source reputation. Jolly Phonics' own materials (secondary-source lesson breakdown,
not the Teacher's Book itself) describe "one or two new sounds per session," which is close to
this course's pace — so the pace gap is really **specifically against UFLI**, not universal
across every reference program, and should be stated that precisely rather than as a blanket
"2–3× the field."

This course's Level 1 defaults to **two or three brand-new letter-sounds per single 20–25
minute sitting** — L1.02 (s+a+t), L1.05 (g+o), L1.06 (c+k+ck), L1.09 (b+f+l) — delivered daily
per the lessons' own "yesterday"/"tomorrow" cross-references (L1.02: "tomorrow adds three
more"), with no equivalent of UFLI's two-day, drill-heavy review structure inside a single new-
sound introduction. Against UFLI specifically, that means this course would finish what UFLI
calls its "Alphabet unit" (lessons 1–34, roughly 13–14 real weeks at UFLI's own two-day/sound
cadence) in about 12 lessons — a genuine, primary-source-confirmed compression, not a citation
artifact. Rule 4 ("mastery gate, not calendar") is the only safety valve, but nothing in the
lessons as written signals that a slower, one-sound-per-sitting path is equally valid — the
default reads as the only path. This matters most for this course's own target adult-ESL-beginner
population, who are building working-memory capacity for an entirely new representational system
(`research/01` §Urdu/adult notes), not just new sounds in a familiar script — exactly the group
UFLI's much slower, drill-repeated cadence was implicitly built to support.
