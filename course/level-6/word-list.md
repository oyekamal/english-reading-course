Every Tier-2 word taught across the level's 16 lessons, in teaching order, with its friendly
definition source lesson and an honest Urdu-cognate/loanword note (DESIGN.md rule 9). Where a
word genuinely has no everyday Urdu loanword, that's stated plainly rather than invented — most
of this level's academic vocabulary is Latin/Greek-rooted and simply doesn't have one; where a
real loanword or a useful (non-cognate) Urdu concept-bridge exists, it's given.

Use this list for review, or as a quick pre-teach check before a lesson ("has the learner met

## What this tells us, honestly

Of 36 words taught across the level, **4 have a genuine, confident everyday Urdu loanword**
(infection, guarantee, sponsored, and condensed-as-in-"condensed milk"), **1 is a flagged
false-friend risk** (contract), and the rest have no true cognate — most of this level's
vocabulary is Latin/Greek-rooted academic register, which simply doesn't overlap with Urdu's
mostly Perso-Arabic root stock for these particular concepts. Where a strong Urdu word for the
*same concept* exists (hijrat for migration, jazb for absorb, chanda for contribute, and others
above), it's given as a useful bridge for discussion — but explicitly flagged as a concept
parallel, not a linguistic cognate, so a tutor never repeats a false etymology claim to a
learner. This is the honest version of DESIGN.md rule 9 at this vocabulary register: real
transfer where it exists, no invented transfer where it doesn't.
